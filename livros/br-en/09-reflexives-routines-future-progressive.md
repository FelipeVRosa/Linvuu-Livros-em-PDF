# Reflexives, routines, ir + infinitive and estar + gerund

By the end of this lesson you can describe your day in order, say what you are going to do tomorrow, say what is happening right now, and use the small pronouns {{me}}, {{te}} and {{se}} where Brazilians use them.

## 9.1 A scene you will live

It is Sunday night. Clara, your Brazilian flatmate, sees your open backpack on the floor and asks about your week.

> **Clara:** {{O que você está fazendo?}}
> **Emma:** {{Estou arrumando a mochila. Amanhã eu começo no trabalho novo.}}
> **Clara:** {{A que horas você se levanta?}}
> **Emma:** {{Eu acordo às seis e meia, mas só me levanto às sete. Tomo banho, tomo café e saio às oito.}}
> **Clara:** {{E vai de quê?}}
> **Emma:** {{Vou pegar o ônibus. Depois, à noite, a gente se vê, né?}}
> **Clara:** {{Claro! Eu vou chegar tarde. Boa sorte!}}

Translation: *What are you doing? / I am packing my backpack. Tomorrow I start at the new job. / What time do you get up? / I wake up at six thirty, but I only get up at seven. I take a shower, have coffee and leave at eight. / And how are you going? / I am going to take the bus. Then, in the evening, we will see each other, right? / Of course! I will be home late. Good luck!*

Three things in this scene are new, and they are the three pillars of the lesson. First, the little words {{se}} and {{me}} ({{você se levanta}}, {{eu me levanto}}, {{a gente se vê}}). Second, {{vou pegar}} and {{vai chegar}}: a future built from {{ir}}. Third, {{estou arrumando}}: what is happening now.

New words from the scene, with guides:

| Portuguese | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{arrumar}} | `ah-hoo-MAR` | to tidy; to pack; to arrange |
| {{acordar}} | `ah-kor-DAR` | to wake up |
| {{levantar-se}} | `leh-vah~-TAR-see` | to get up |
| {{tomar banho}} | `toh-MAR BAH-nyoo` | to take a shower or bath |
| {{sair}} | `sah-EER` | to leave, to go out |
| {{pegar}} | `peh-GAR` | to take (a bus), to get, to catch |
| {{né?}} | `neh` | right? isn't it? (spoken, short for {{não é?}}) |

## 9.2 The trap: why does Portuguese say "I raise myself"?

In English, many daily actions need no pronoun: *I wake up, I get up, I shower, I get dressed, I sit down, I lie down.* English does not say *I wake myself*.

Portuguese grammar says that, for certain verbs, the action returns to the person who does it, and so it adds a pronoun: {{eu me levanto}} (literally *I raise myself*), {{ele se veste}} (he dresses himself), {{nós nos sentamos}} (we sit ourselves down). These are called **reflexive verbs**, and the pronoun is a **reflexive pronoun**.

If you leave the pronoun out, Brazilians understand you almost always. But in some cases you say something different (or odd), and in others you sound wrong. The second trap is that the verbs are not the same in both languages. Portuguese has reflexives where English does not, and **does not** have them where you would expect: English *to take a shower* has no reflexive in Portuguese ({{tomar banho}}), while English *to feel* ({{sentir-se}}) becomes reflexive when you talk about yourself.

## 9.3 The reflexive pronouns

| Subject | Reflexive pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| eu | {{me}} | {{Eu me levanto às sete.}} |
| você, ele, ela | {{se}} | {{Ela se levanta cedo.}} |
| a gente | {{se}} | {{A gente se encontra às cinco.}} |
| nós | {{nos}} | {{Nós nos levantamos tarde.}} |
| vocês, eles, elas | {{se}} | {{Eles se levantam às seis.}} |
| tu (regions) | {{te}} | {{Tu te levantas cedo?}} |

The dictionary form of such a verb is written with {{-se}} attached by a hyphen: {{levantar-se}}, {{sentar-se}}, {{chamar-se}}. When you conjugate it, the pronoun goes **before** the verb.

::: passo 1 Put the pronoun before the verb
In Brazilian speech the pronoun comes before the conjugated verb: {{eu me levanto}}, {{ela se chama Ana}}, {{a gente se vê}}. Written texts and European Portuguese often put it after, with a hyphen: {{levanto-me}}, {{chama-se Ana}}. You will read these forms, but do not use them when speaking Brazilian Portuguese.
:::

::: passo 2 Keep the pronoun in a negative sentence, between {{não}} and the verb
{{Eu não me levanto cedo.}} (I do not get up early.) {{Ela não se sente bem.}} (She does not feel well.)
:::

::: passo 3 With two verbs, put the pronoun before the second verb, the infinitive
{{Eu preciso me levantar cedo.}} {{Vou me deitar agora.}} {{Quero me sentar aqui.}} This is the normal spoken Brazilian pattern. The written style, {{preciso levantar-me}}, is formal.
:::

::: atencao
**{{A gente}} and {{se}}.** {{A gente}} takes the third-person form of the verb and {{se}}: {{a gente se vê}}, {{a gente se levanta}}. Only the very careful {{nós}} takes {{nos}}: {{nós nos vemos}}. In speech you will hear {{a gente se vê}} a hundred times for each {{nós nos vemos}}.
:::

## 9.4 Which verbs are reflexive in Brazil?

You do not need to memorize a long list. Sort the verbs into three groups.

### Group 1: reflexive in everyday speech

These verbs are used with {{me/se/nos}} all the time. Learn them as a unit.

| Verb | Guide | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| {{chamar-se}} | `shah-MAR-see` | to be called | {{Eu me chamo Emma.}} / {{Como você se chama?}} |
| {{sentir-se}} | `seh~-TCHEER-see` | to feel (yourself) | {{Eu me sinto cansado.}} (I feel tired) |
| {{sentar-se}} | `seh~-TAR-see` | to sit down | {{Pode se sentar.}} (You may sit down) |
| {{deitar-se}} | `day-TAR-see` | to lie down | {{Eu me deito às onze.}} |
| {{divertir-se}} | `djee-ver-TCHEER-see` | to have fun | {{A gente se diverte muito.}} / {{Divirta-se!}} |
| {{preocupar-se}} | `preh-oh-koo-PAR-see` | to worry | {{Não se preocupe.}} (Don't worry) |
| {{vestir-se}} | `vehs-TCHEER-see` | to get dressed | {{Eu me visto rápido.}} |

Notice {{sentir-se}} and {{vestir-se}}. In the {{eu}} form, the vowel changes: {{sinto}} `SEE~-too`, {{visto}} `VEES-too`, {{divirto}} `djee-VEER-too`. The other persons keep the {{e}}: {{sente}}, {{veste}}, {{diverte}}. These are common verbs, so it is worth hearing them in the table above.

### Group 2: verbs where the pronoun is optional

For some verbs, the version with {{me}} and the version without it are both correct, and Brazilians choose either one. This applies to {{levantar}}, {{lembrar}} and {{esquecer}}:

| With pronoun | Without pronoun | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{Eu me levanto às sete.}} | {{Eu levanto às sete.}} | I get up at seven. |
| {{Eu me lembro dele.}} | {{Eu lembro dele.}} | I remember him. |
| {{Eu me esqueci da chave.}} | {{Eu esqueci a chave.}} | I forgot the key. |

Notice that {{lembrar}} and {{esquecer}} take {{de}} after the pronoun version ({{me lembro de}}, {{me esqueci de}}), while the version without the pronoun can take the thing directly ({{esqueci a chave}}). Choose one version of each verb and use it consistently; you will understand both.

### Group 3: no pronoun in Brazil

Many English reflexive-sounding actions have **no** pronoun in Portuguese. Do not add one.

| Say | Not | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{Eu acordo às seis.}} | ~~Eu me acordo~~ | I wake up |
| {{Eu tomo banho.}} | ~~Eu me banho~~ | I take a shower |
| {{Eu durmo oito horas.}} | ~~Eu me durmo~~ | I sleep |
| {{Eu escovo os dentes.}} | ~~Eu me escovo os dentes~~ | I brush my teeth |
| {{Eu lavo as mãos.}} | ~~Eu me lavo as mãos~~ | I wash my hands |

The last two follow a pattern. **When a body part or a piece of clothing is the object, you do not need the pronoun**: {{Eu lavo o rosto}}, {{Eu visto o casaco}}, {{Ele corta o cabelo}}. The article ({{o}}, {{a}}) replaces the possessive, as in Lesson 8.

::: dica
{{Acordar}} also takes an object: {{Eu acordo as crianças às sete}} (I wake the children up at seven). With no object, {{acordar}} means that you wake up yourself. The sentence is clear from context, and that is why Brazilians do not need {{me}}.
:::

### The same pronoun for "each other"

{{Se}} and {{nos}} also mean "each other". The situation decides which meaning you hear.

- {{A gente se vê amanhã.}} (We will see each other tomorrow. A common farewell.)
- {{Eles se amam.}} (They love each other.)
- {{Vamos nos encontrar às cinco?}} (Shall we meet at five?)
- {{Nós nos falamos por WhatsApp.}} (We talk to each other on WhatsApp.)

Here is the vocabulary of a normal day. Say each phrase in the first person, with {{eu}} or without.

| Portuguese | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{acordar}} | `ah-kor-DAR` | to wake up |
| {{levantar}} | `leh-vah~-TAR` | to get up |
| {{tomar café da manhã}} | `toh-MAR kah-FEH dah mah-NYAH~` | to have breakfast |
| {{ir para o trabalho}} | `eer PAH-rah oo trah-BAH-lyoo` | to go to work |
| {{chegar em casa}} | `sheh-GAR eh~ee KAH-zah` | to get home |
| {{almoçar}} | `ahl-moh-SAR` | to have lunch |
| {{jantar}} | `zhah~-TAR` | to have dinner |
| {{voltar}} | `vol-TAR` | to come back |
| {{assistir}} | `ah-sees-TCHEER` | to watch (TV, a series) |
| {{dormir}} | `dor-MEER` | to sleep |

Some forms to know in the present tense:

| Verb | eu | você, ele, ela, a gente | nós | vocês, eles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| {{sair}} | {{saio}} `SAH-yoo` | {{sai}} `sie` | {{saímos}} `sah-EE-mooss` | {{saem}} `SAH-eh~ee` |
| {{dormir}} | {{durmo}} `DOOR-moo` | {{dorme}} `DOR-mee` | {{dormimos}} | {{dormem}} |
| {{pegar}} | {{pego}} `PEH-goo` | {{pega}} | {{pegamos}} | {{pegam}} |

{{Sair}} is irregular in the {{eu}} form ({{saio}}) and in {{nós}} ({{saímos}}, with the accent). {{Dormir}} changes {{o}} to {{u}} in {{eu durmo}}, the same type of change as {{sinto}} and {{visto}}. Everything else is regular.

### When do you do it? The words that organize a day

| Portuguese | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{de manhã}} | `djee mah-NYAH~` | in the morning |
| {{à tarde}} | `ah TAR-djee` | in the afternoon |
| {{à noite}} | `ah NOY-tchee` | at night |
| {{de madrugada}} | `djee mah-droo-GAH-dah` | very early, in the small hours |
| {{depois}} | `deh-POYSS` | afterwards |
| {{antes}} | `AH~-tcheess` | before |
| {{primeiro}} | `pree-MAY-roo` | first |
| {{por último}} | `por OOL-tchee-moo` | lastly |

Notice {{de manhã}} but {{à tarde}} and {{à noite}}. The first one uses {{de}}, and the other two use {{a}} + {{a}} = {{à}}. You also met {{à}} in Lesson 1: that is the grave accent. For exact times use {{às}}: {{às sete}}, {{às oito e meia}}, {{ao meio-dia}} (at noon).

### How often?

| Portuguese | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{sempre}} | `SEH~-pree` | always |
| {{geralmente}} | `zheh-rah-oo-MEH~-tchee` | usually |
| {{normalmente}} | `nor-mah-oo-MEH~-tchee` | normally |
| {{às vezes}} | `ahss VEH-zeess` | sometimes |
| {{quase nunca}} | `KWAH-zee NOO~-kah` | almost never |
| {{nunca}} | `NOO~-kah` | never |
| {{todo dia}} / {{todos os dias}} | `TOH-doo DJEE-ah` | every day |
| {{toda semana}} | `TOH-dah seh-MAH-nah` | every week |

Put {{sempre}}, {{geralmente}}, {{normalmente}} and {{quase nunca}} before the verb: {{Eu sempre tomo café.}} {{Às vezes}} can start or end the sentence.

::: atencao
**{{Nunca}} can be used with or without {{não}}, and the position decides.** Before the verb, {{nunca}} stands alone: {{Eu nunca como carne.}} After the verb, you need {{não}} before it: {{Eu não como carne nunca.}} Do not put both before the verb: ~~Eu não nunca como carne~~. In Portuguese, double negation is correct (and expected); English *I don't never* is wrong, Portuguese {{não ... nunca}} is right. The same applies to {{ninguém}} (nobody) and {{nada}} (nothing): {{Não vi ninguém}}, {{Ninguém me viu}}.
:::

## 9.6 Ir + infinitive: the everyday future

In the earlier lessons you met {{ir}}: {{vou}}, {{vai}}, {{vamos}}, {{vão}}. With an infinitive after it, {{ir}} builds the normal spoken future, and the structure is the same as English *I am going to…*

| Subject | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| eu | {{vou}} | {{Eu vou trabalhar amanhã.}} |
| você, ele, ela, a gente | {{vai}} | {{Ela vai viajar no sábado.}} |
| nós | {{vamos}} | {{Nós vamos almoçar juntos.}} |
| vocês, eles, elas | {{vão}} | {{Eles vão chegar tarde.}} |

Only {{ir}} changes. The second verb stays in the infinitive, in any person. Negative: {{não}} before {{ir}}: {{Eu não vou sair hoje.}} Question: use the same order as a statement, with the rising voice: {{Você vai almoçar em casa?}} or add a question word: {{O que você vai fazer amanhã?}}

With a reflexive verb, the pronoun comes before the infinitive: {{Amanhã eu vou me levantar mais cedo.}} {{A gente vai se encontrar às cinco.}}

::: passo 1 Add a time expression
The future is clearer when it has a time word.
:::

| Portuguese | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{hoje à noite}} | `OH-zhee ah NOY-tchee` | tonight |
| {{amanhã}} | `ah-mah-NYAH~` | tomorrow |
| {{depois}} | `deh-POYSS` | later |
| {{daqui a pouco}} | `dah-KEE ah POH-koo` | in a little while |
| {{semana que vem}} | `seh-MAH-nah kee veh~ee` | next week |
| {{mês que vem}} | `mehss kee veh~ee` | next month |
| {{ano que vem}} | `AH-noo kee veh~ee` | next year |
| {{daqui a dois dias}} | `dah-KEE ah doyss DJEE-ahss` | two days from now |

{{Que vem}} literally means "that comes". In Brazil it is the normal spoken way to say *next*: {{na semana que vem}}, {{no mês que vem}}.

::: passo 2 Remember the suggestion: {{vamos}} + infinitive
{{Vamos}} followed by an infinitive means *let's*: {{Vamos comer?}} (Shall we eat?), {{Vamos nos ver amanhã.}} (Let's see each other tomorrow.) The same word is also the {{nós}} form of {{ir}}, and the context tells you which.
:::

::: comparacao
| In English | In Portuguese |
|---|---|
| *I am going to the beach tomorrow* (present continuous for the future) | {{Amanhã eu vou à praia}}: the plain present of {{ir}} already gives the future |
| *I'm working tomorrow* | {{Amanhã eu trabalho}} or {{Amanhã eu vou trabalhar}}: not {{estou trabalhando}} |
| *I will call you* | {{Vou te ligar}} (more on {{te}} in Lesson 12) |
:::

Portuguese also has a one-word future ({{falarei}}, {{comerei}}). It is used in writing, in formal speech and in some set phrases, but in conversation Brazilians use {{vou falar}}. You do not need to produce it at this level.

## 9.7 Estar + gerund: what is happening now

To say what is in progress, use {{estar}} + the **gerund**, which ends in {{-ndo}}.

| Subject | {{estar}} | Spoken form |
|---|---|---|
| eu | {{estou}} `ehs-TOH` | {{tô}} `toh` |
| você, ele, ela, a gente | {{está}} `ehs-TAH` | {{tá}} `tah` |
| nós | {{estamos}} `ehs-TAH-mooss` | |
| vocês, eles, elas | {{estão}} `ehs-TOW~` | {{tão}} `tow~` |

In relaxed speech, {{estou}} becomes {{tô}} and {{está}} becomes {{tá}}: {{Tô saindo}}, {{Ela tá dormindo}}. You will hear this constantly, so recognize it. In writing and in a more careful style, use the full form.

### How to form the gerund

| Verb type | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| {{-ar}} | drop {{-r}}, add {{-ndo}}: {{-ando}} | {{falar}} → {{falando}} `fah-LAH~-doo` |
| {{-er}} | {{-endo}} | {{comer}} → {{comendo}} `koh-MEH~-doo` |
| {{-ir}} | {{-indo}} | {{abrir}} → {{abrindo}} `ah-BREE~-doo` |

The rule is regular for every verb you have met, with one exception: {{pôr}} (to put) makes {{pondo}} `POH~-doo`. Verbs that are irregular in other tenses have regular gerunds: {{fazer}} → {{fazendo}} `fah-ZEH~-doo`, {{dizer}} → {{dizendo}}, {{ver}} → {{vendo}}, {{ir}} → {{indo}}, {{vir}} → {{vindo}}, {{sair}} → {{saindo}}, {{ter}} → {{tendo}}, {{dormir}} → {{dormindo}}.

The gerund never changes: not for person, not for gender, not for number.

- {{Eu estou estudando português.}} (I am studying Portuguese.)
- {{Você está fazendo o quê?}} (What are you doing?)
- {{Eles estão dormindo.}} (They are asleep.)
- {{A gente tá chegando.}} (We are arriving.)

With a reflexive verb, the pronoun comes **before the gerund** in Brazilian speech: {{Estou me arrumando}} (I am getting ready), {{Ela está se vestindo}}.

::: dica
In fast speech many Brazilians drop the {{d}} of {{-ndo}}: {{falando}} sounds like {{falano}}, {{fazendo}} like {{fazeno}}. It is real, and it is not an error. Write the full word.
:::

### What {{estar + gerund}} is NOT

English uses *-ing* for a lot of things. Portuguese is stricter.

::: passo 1 Use it for something happening now, or in a temporary period
{{Estou lendo um livro ótimo.}} (I am reading a great book. Not necessarily at this second.) {{Estou morando em São Paulo.}} (I am living in São Paulo. A temporary situation.)
:::

::: passo 2 Use the plain present for habits and facts
{{Eu trabalho num banco.}} (I work at a bank.) {{Ela mora no Rio.}} (She lives in Rio.) The progressive would suggest that the situation is temporary. If you say {{Estou trabalhando num banco}}, you are saying that you are working there for now.
:::

::: passo 3 Do not use it for the future
{{Amanhã eu viajo}} or {{Amanhã eu vou viajar}} (tomorrow I travel / I am going to travel), but not ~~Amanhã estou viajando~~ in this sense.
:::

Here is a small contrast that Brazilians use all the time. {{Já estou indo}} (literally *I am already going*) means "I am on my way". {{Estou chegando}} means "I am almost there". Both are the answer to a worried "Where are you?" on WhatsApp.

::: comparacao
| Where | How to say *I am working* |
|---|---|
| Brazil | {{Estou trabalhando.}} (estar + gerund) |
| Portugal | {{Estou a trabalhar.}} (estar + {{a}} + infinitive) |
:::

In Portugal people use {{estar a}} + infinitive instead of the gerund. If you watch Portuguese films or talk with Portuguese friends, you will hear {{estou a fazer}} where a Brazilian says {{estou fazendo}}. Both are correct in their country.

## 9.8 Putting it together

Read this text aloud. It uses all three tools from this lesson: reflexives, {{ir}} + infinitive and {{estar}} + gerund.

> {{Normalmente eu acordo às seis e meia, mas só me levanto às sete. Tomo banho, escovo os dentes e tomo café. Saio de casa às oito e pego o ônibus. Chego ao trabalho às nove. Almoço ao meio-dia com os colegas. À noite eu volto para casa, jantamos e assistimos a uma série. Eu quase nunca me deito depois da meia-noite. Hoje é domingo, então estou descansando e estou arrumando a mochila. Amanhã vou começar um trabalho novo e vou me levantar mais cedo.}}

Translation: *Normally I wake up at six thirty, but I only get up at seven. I shower, brush my teeth and have coffee. I leave the house at eight and take the bus. I get to work at nine. I have lunch at noon with my colleagues. In the evening I go back home, we have dinner and watch a series. I almost never go to bed after midnight. Today is Sunday, so I am resting and I am packing my backpack. Tomorrow I am going to start a new job and I am going to get up earlier.*

Two details worth noticing. {{Assistimos a uma série}} uses {{a}} after {{assistir}} when it means *to watch* in careful Portuguese ({{assistir a}}); in speech people often drop the {{a}}: {{assistimos uma série}}. And {{chego ao trabalho}} is the careful form; in speech {{chego no trabalho}} is the normal one, as in {{chego em casa}}.

New words in this text: {{colega}} `koh-LEH-gah` (colleague), {{série}} `SEH-ree-ee` (series), {{descansar}} `djees-kah~-SAR` (to rest), {{meia-noite}} `MAY-ah NOY-tchee` (midnight).

## 9.12 Pocket summary: Lesson 9

::: resumo
- **Reflexive pronouns:** {{me}} (eu), {{se}} (você, ele, ela, a gente, eles), {{nos}} (nós), {{te}} (tu). Before the verb: {{eu me levanto}}, {{eu não me levanto}}, {{vou me levantar}}.
- **Reflexive in speech:** {{chamar-se}}, {{sentir-se}}, {{sentar-se}}, {{deitar-se}}, {{divertir-se}}, {{preocupar-se}}, {{vestir-se}}. **Without pronoun:** {{acordar}}, {{tomar banho}}, {{dormir}}, {{escovar os dentes}}, {{lavar as mãos}}.
- **Each other:** {{a gente se vê}}, {{eles se amam}}.
- **Routine words:** {{de manhã}}, {{à tarde}}, {{à noite}}, {{às sete}}; {{sempre}}, {{geralmente}}, {{às vezes}}, {{quase nunca}}, {{nunca}}. Use {{não ... nunca}} or {{nunca}} alone before the verb.
- **Irregular present:** {{saio}}, {{sai}}, {{saímos}}; {{durmo}}, {{dorme}}; {{sinto}}, {{visto}}, {{divirto}}.
- **Future with {{ir}}:** {{vou, vai, vamos, vão}} + infinitive. {{Vamos}} + infinitive = let's. Time words: {{amanhã}}, {{semana que vem}}, {{daqui a pouco}}.
- **Now:** {{estar}} + gerund: {{-ando}}, {{-endo}}, {{-indo}}. Spoken: {{tô}}, {{tá}}. Habits take the plain present; the future does not take {{estar + gerund}}.
- **Portugal:** {{estar a}} + infinitive, and the pronoun after the verb: {{levanto-me}}.
:::
