# Comparing, giving instructions, asking politely, por and para, and que

By the end of this lesson you can compare two things, give and understand a command or a direction, ask for something politely, choose between {{por}} and {{para}}, and join two ideas with {{que}}. At the end of the lesson you will find a checkpoint of twelve "I can…" statements for the whole book.

## 13.1 A scene you will live

On your last evening in Belo Horizonte you invite Clara to dinner. You arrive at a small restaurant on a street full of bars.

> **Garçom:** {{Boa noite! Querem sentar aqui dentro ou lá fora?}}
> **Emma:** {{Aqui dentro, por favor. Eu queria uma mesa pra dois.}}
> **Garçom:** {{Claro. Fiquem à vontade, podem sentar ali, na janela.}}
> **Emma:** {{Obrigada. Você poderia me recomendar um prato?}}
> **Garçom:** {{O peixe é melhor do que a carne hoje. É mais leve e é mais barato. É o prato que todo mundo pede.}}
> **Emma:** {{Então vou querer o peixe. Obrigada pela dica!}}
> **Garçom:** {{Imagina! Já trago o cardápio.}}

Translation: *Good evening! Would you like to sit inside or outside? / Inside, please. I'd like a table for two. / Of course. Make yourselves comfortable, you can sit over there, by the window. / Thank you. Could you recommend a dish? / The fish is better than the meat today. It is lighter and it is cheaper. It is the dish that everybody orders. / Then I'll have the fish. Thank you for the tip! / Don't mention it! I'll bring the menu right away.*

The scene contains the five tools of the lesson, all in a few lines. {{Melhor do que}} and {{mais barato}} compare. {{Fiquem}} is a command. {{Eu queria}} and {{você poderia}} are polite requests. {{Pra dois}} and {{pela dica}} are {{para}} and {{por}}. {{O prato que todo mundo pede}} uses the relative {{que}}.

New words from the scene:

| Portuguese | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{o garçom}} | `oo gar-SOH~` | the waiter |
| {{lá fora}} | `lah FAW-rah` | outside |
| {{aqui dentro}} | `ah-KEE DEH~-troo` | inside |
| {{à vontade}} | `ah voh~-TAH-djee` | at ease, comfortable |
| {{recomendar}} | `heh-koh-meh~-DAR` | to recommend |
| {{o prato}} | `oo PRAH-too` | the dish, the plate |
| {{o peixe}} | `oo PAY-shee` | the fish |
| {{leve}} | `LEH-vee` | light |
| {{a dica}} | `ah DJEE-kah` | the tip, the hint |
| {{imagina}} | `ee-mah-ZHEE-nah` | don't mention it, no problem |

## 13.2 Comparing

### The trap: English changes the adjective, Portuguese adds a word

In English, short adjectives take *-er* (*cheaper, taller*) and long ones take *more* (*more expensive*). Portuguese has no *-er*. It puts {{mais}} (more) or {{menos}} (less) before **every** adjective, and then {{do que}} (than).

| English | Portuguese |
|---|---|
| *cheaper than* | {{mais barato do que}} |
| *taller than* | {{mais alto do que}} |
| *more expensive than* | {{mais caro do que}} |
| *less expensive than* | {{menos caro do que}} |

::: passo 1 Compare unequal things: {{mais}} / {{menos}} + adjective + {{do que}}
- {{O ônibus é mais barato do que o táxi.}} (The bus is cheaper than the taxi.)
- {{Belo Horizonte é menos quente do que Salvador.}} (Belo Horizonte is less hot than Salvador.)
- {{Minha irmã é mais velha do que eu.}} (My sister is older than I am.)

The adjective agrees with the first noun, as usual: {{A pizza é mais cara}}, {{Os pães são mais baratos}}.
:::

::: passo 2 Know that {{do que}} can be shortened to {{que}}
In speech you will hear both {{mais alto do que eu}} and {{mais alto que eu}}. {{Do que}} is always correct and it is clearer, so use it. After it, use the **subject** pronoun: {{do que eu}}, not "do que mim".
:::

::: passo 3 Compare equal things: {{tão}} + adjective + {{quanto}} (or {{como}})
- {{Ele é tão alto quanto o pai.}} (He is as tall as his father.)
- {{Aqui é tão caro quanto lá.}} (It is as expensive here as there.)
:::

::: passo 4 With nouns, use {{tanto}} and make it agree
- {{Tenho tanto trabalho quanto você.}} (I have as much work as you.)
- {{Ela tem tantos amigos quanto eu.}} (She has as many friends as I do.)
- {{Tem tanta gente quanto ontem.}} (There are as many people as yesterday.)
:::

::: passo 5 With numbers, use {{de}}, not {{que}}
- {{Vieram mais de vinte pessoas.}} (More than twenty people came.)
- {{Custa menos de cem reais.}} (It costs less than a hundred reais.)
:::

### Four adjectives that do not use {{mais}}

Four common adjectives have their own comparative forms. Using {{mais}} with the first three is a mistake; {{mais pequeno}} exists, but in Brazil {{menor}} is the normal word. Learn them as pairs.

| Adjective | Comparative | Guide | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| {{bom}} (good) | {{melhor}} (better) | `meh-LYOR` | {{Este café é melhor do que aquele.}} |
| {{mau, ruim}} (bad) | {{pior}} (worse) | `pyor` | {{O tempo hoje está pior do que ontem.}} |
| {{grande}} (big) | {{maior}} (bigger) | `mah-YOR` | {{A minha casa é maior do que a sua.}} |
| {{pequeno}} (small) | {{menor}} (smaller) | `meh-NOR` | {{O quarto é menor do que a sala.}} |

{{Melhor}} and {{pior}} also work as adverbs: {{Ela fala melhor do que eu}} (She speaks better than I do); {{Eu durmo pior no calor}} (I sleep worse in the heat). For size, {{maior}} and {{menor}} are the usual words. For age, {{mais velho}} and {{mais novo}}: {{Meu irmão é mais novo do que eu}}.

::: atencao
**Never say** ~~mais bom~~, ~~mais ruim~~, ~~mais grande~~. Use {{melhor}}, {{pior}} and {{maior}}. One apparent exception: in speech you may hear {{mais ruim}}, but {{pior}} is the form to use and write.
:::

### The most: the superlative

Put the article before {{mais}} and use {{de}} (or {{do}}, {{da}}) for "in" or "of".

- {{Esse é o prato mais caro do cardápio.}} (This is the most expensive dish on the menu.)
- {{A Clara é a pessoa mais simpática do prédio.}} (Clara is the nicest person in the building.)
- {{Estes são os melhores pães da cidade.}} (These are the best breads in town.)
- {{O pior dia foi segunda-feira.}} (The worst day was Monday.)

### Reinforcing the comparison

| Portuguese | Meaning |
|---|---|
| {{muito mais barato}} / {{bem mais barato}} | much cheaper |
| {{um pouco mais caro}} | a bit more expensive |
| {{cada vez mais}} | more and more |
| {{mais ou menos}} | so-so, approximately |
| {{Quanto mais eu estudo, mais eu aprendo.}} | The more I study, the more I learn. |

::: erros
| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ele é mais alto que de mim. | {{Ele é mais alto do que eu.}} | after {{que}} use the subject pronoun |
| Esse hotel é mais bom. | {{Esse hotel é melhor.}} | {{melhor}}, not {{mais bom}} |
| Tem mais que vinte pessoas. | {{Tem mais de vinte pessoas.}} | numbers take {{de}} |
| Ela é tão alta como mim. | {{Ela é tão alta quanto eu.}} | the subject pronoun, not {{mim}} |
| Tenho tanto amigos quanto você. | {{Tenho tantos amigos quanto você.}} | {{tanto}} agrees with its noun |
:::

## 13.3 Giving instructions: the imperative

### The trap: English has one command form, Portuguese has two registers

English says *Open the door!* to anyone: a friend, a stranger, a child. Portuguese has the formal command, which is the form in grammars, on signs and in instructions, and the everyday command, which is the form you hear among friends.

### The formal command ({{você}}, {{vocês}})

The command for {{você}} and {{vocês}} is built from the {{eu}} form of the present. Take the {{eu}} form, drop {{-o}}, and add the "opposite" vowel: {{-e}} for {{-ar}} verbs, {{-a}} for {{-er}} and {{-ir}} verbs. For {{vocês}}, add {{-m}}.

| Verb | {{eu}} form | {{você}} | {{vocês}} | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| {{falar}} | {{falo}} | {{fale}} `FAH-lee` | {{falem}} `FAH-leh~ee` | speak |
| {{comer}} | {{como}} | {{coma}} `KOH-mah` | {{comam}} `KOH-mah~oo` | eat |
| {{abrir}} | {{abro}} | {{abra}} `AH-brah` | {{abram}} `AH-brah~oo` | open |
| {{fazer}} | {{faço}} | {{faça}} `FAH-sah` | {{façam}} | do |
| {{dizer}} | {{digo}} | {{diga}} `DJEE-gah` | {{digam}} | say |
| {{ter}} | {{tenho}} | {{tenha}} `TEH~-nyah` | {{tenham}} | have |
| {{vir}} | {{venho}} | {{venha}} `VEH~-nyah` | {{venham}} | come |
| {{ver}} | {{vejo}} | {{veja}} `VEH-zhah` | {{vejam}} | see |
| {{trazer}} | {{trago}} | {{traga}} `TRAH-gah` | {{tragam}} | bring |
| {{sair}} | {{saio}} | {{saia}} `SAH-yah` | {{saiam}} | leave |

The method gives all the irregulars of the {{eu}} form automatically (compare {{faço}} → {{faça}}). Five common verbs do not follow it (a sixth, {{querer}} → {{queira}}, is rare as a command). Learn them as they are.

| Verb | {{você}} | {{vocês}} | Guide of {{você}} |
|---|---|---|---|
| {{ser}} | {{seja}} | {{sejam}} | `SEH-zhah` |
| {{estar}} | {{esteja}} | {{estejam}} | `ehs-TEH-zhah` |
| {{ir}} | {{vá}} | {{vão}} | `vah` |
| {{dar}} | {{dê}} | {{deem}} | `deh` |
| {{saber}} | {{saiba}} | {{saibam}} | `SIE-bah` |

Verbs with a spelling pattern keep the sound: {{ficar}} → {{fique}} (`FEE-kee`), {{pagar}} → {{pague}} (`PAH-gee`), {{chegar}} → {{chegue}}, {{começar}} → {{comece}}.

**Negative:** put {{não}} before the same form: {{Não fale}}, {{Não abra}}, {{Não se preocupe}} (Don't worry).

With reflexive and object pronouns in the formal command, the pronoun traditionally follows the verb: {{Sente-se}}, {{Divirta-se}}, {{Diga-me}}. Negative commands put it before: {{Não se preocupe}}, {{Não me diga}}.

### The everyday command

Among friends, in the street and at home, many Brazilians (especially in São Paulo and Rio) give commands with the plain present form of {{você}}, the same form as {{ele}}:

| Formal, signs, instructions | Everyday, friends |
|---|---|
| {{Fale mais devagar.}} | {{Fala mais devagar.}} |
| {{Venha cá.}} | {{Vem cá.}} |
| {{Espere um pouco.}} | {{Espera um pouco.}} |
| {{Me ligue depois.}} | {{Me liga depois.}} |
| {{Sente aqui.}} | {{Senta aí.}} |
| {{Pegue o ônibus.}} | {{Pega o ônibus.}} |

You will hear both. Use the formal command (with {{por favor}}) with strangers, in shops, with older people and in writing. Understand the everyday command, and use it when you are with friends. Of the five irregular verbs above, {{seja}} is used in both registers, but among friends people say {{Vai!}} (not {{Vá!}}) and {{Me dá…}} (not {{Me dê…}}), and {{Vem!}} for {{vir}}.

::: dica
{{Vamos}} + infinitive is a gentle command for "us": {{Vamos comer?}}, {{Vamos sair?}} The reply to a request is often {{Claro!}}, {{Pode deixar!}} (Leave it to me!), {{Já vou!}} (Coming!).
:::

## 13.4 Asking politely

### The trap: English has "please" and "could", Portuguese has a ladder of verb forms

In English, you can soften a request with *please* and *could*. In Portuguese, *por favor* is necessary but not sufficient. The verb form itself carries the politeness. Here is the ladder, from the most direct to the most careful.

| Level | Portuguese | When |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Direct | {{Quero um café.}} | with a friend in a hurry, or with {{por favor}} in a bar |
| 2. Informal request | {{Me dá um café?}} / {{Me vê um café, por favor.}} | in a café, a bakery, a bar |
| 3. Neutral polite | {{Eu queria um café, por favor.}} | restaurants, shops: the standard |
| 4. Polite question | {{Pode me trazer um café?}} | with the waiter |
| 5. More polite | {{Você poderia me trazer um café?}} | with a stranger, an older person |
| 6. Very formal | {{Eu gostaria de um café.}} / {{Seria possível um café?}} | hotels, offices, e-mails |

::: passo 1 Use {{queria}} for "I'd like"
{{Queria}} is the imperfect of {{querer}} (Lesson 11) used in the present to be polite. {{Eu queria uma água}} (I'd like a water). It is the most useful polite verb for a learner, because it works everywhere: {{Eu queria saber o preço}} (I'd like to know the price), {{Queria um quarto para duas noites}} (I'd like a room for two nights).
:::

::: passo 2 Use {{poderia}} for "could you"
{{Poderia}} is the conditional of {{poder}}. The conditional is formed by adding {{-ia}} endings to the **whole infinitive**: {{poder}} + {{ia}}.

| Verb | {{eu}}, {{você}}, {{ele}} | {{nós}} | {{vocês}} |
|---|---|---|---|
| {{poder}} | {{poderia}} `poh-deh-REE-ah` | {{poderíamos}} | {{poderiam}} |
| {{gostar}} | {{gostaria}} `gohs-tah-REE-ah` | {{gostaríamos}} | {{gostariam}} |
| {{dever}} | {{deveria}} `deh-veh-REE-ah` | {{deveríamos}} | {{deveriam}} |
| {{ser}} | {{seria}} `seh-REE-ah` | {{seríamos}} | {{seriam}} |

At this level you need these four, which cover almost every polite sentence. A few common verbs have irregular conditionals ({{fazer}} → {{faria}}, {{dizer}} → {{diria}}, {{trazer}} → {{traria}}), but you can wait for them.

- {{Você poderia repetir, por favor?}} (Could you repeat that, please?)
- {{Poderia me ajudar?}} (Could you help me?)
- {{O senhor poderia me dizer onde fica o banco?}} (Could you tell me where the bank is, sir?)
- {{Você deveria descansar um pouco.}} (You should rest a little.)
:::

::: passo 3 Use {{gostaria de}} for formal "I'd like"
{{Gostaria}} takes {{de}}, like {{gostar}} (Lesson 8): {{Gostaria de um quarto}}, {{Gostaria de falar com a gerente}}, {{Gostaríamos de reservar uma mesa}}.
:::

::: passo 4 Soften with {{pode}}, {{dá pra}} or {{seria possível}}
- {{Pode abrir a janela?}} (Can you open the window?)
- {{Dá pra abrir a janela?}} (Is it possible to open the window?) A very common spoken form.
- {{Seria possível trocar de mesa?}} (Would it be possible to change tables?)
:::

### How to answer

| To say yes | To say no |
|---|---|
| {{Claro!}} / {{Com certeza.}} | {{Desculpe, não posso.}} |
| {{Pois não.}} (a clerk's "certainly") | {{Infelizmente não dá.}} (Unfortunately it's not possible.) |
| {{Pode deixar.}} / {{Já trago.}} | {{Só um momento, por favor.}} (Just a moment, please.) |

{{Pois não}} `poyss NOW~` has two meanings you should know. Said by a shop assistant at the start (**{{Pois não?}}**), it means "Yes? How can I help you?" Said as an answer to a request, it means "Certainly."

::: comparacao
| In English | In Portuguese |
|---|---|
| *I want a coffee.* (blunt) | {{Quero um café.}} (blunt too: soften it) |
| *I'd like a coffee, please.* | {{Eu queria um café, por favor.}} |
| *Could you…?* | {{Você poderia…?}} / {{Pode…?}} / {{Dá pra…?}} |
| *Would you mind…?* | {{Você se importaria de…?}} (formal, recognition) |
:::

## 13.5 Por and para

### The trap: English has "for" and "to", Portuguese has {{por}} and {{para}} with different jobs

English *for* can mean a goal, a recipient, a duration, a price, a reason. Portuguese divides those meanings between two words. The essential idea:

- **{{Para}}** looks **forward**: where you are going, what you want to achieve, who gets it.
- **{{Por}}** looks **at the road or the cause**: through where, because of what, in exchange for what, by which means.

| Use {{para}} for… | Example |
|---|---|
| destination | {{Vou para São Paulo amanhã.}} |
| purpose (+ infinitive) | {{Estudo português para trabalhar no Brasil.}} |
| recipient | {{Este presente é para você.}} |
| deadline | {{Preciso do relatório para sexta-feira.}} |
| intended use | {{uma xícara para café}} |
| opinion | {{Para mim, o peixe é melhor.}} |
| employer | {{Ela trabalha para uma empresa grande.}} |

| Use {{por}} for… | Example |
|---|---|
| cause, thanks | {{Obrigada pela dica.}} |
| through, along | {{Passamos pelo centro.}} / {{Andei pela praia.}} |
| duration | {{Morei lá por dois anos.}} |
| price, exchange | {{Paguei vinte reais pelo livro.}} |
| means | {{Mandei por e-mail.}} / {{Falei pelo telefone.}} |
| per, each | {{dez reais por pessoa}} / {{duas vezes por semana}} |
| fixed phrases | {{por favor}}, {{por exemplo}}, {{por isso}}, {{por enquanto}} |

### The contractions

{{Por}} fuses with the articles: {{por}} + {{o}} = {{pelo}}, {{por}} + {{a}} = {{pela}}, {{por}} + {{os}} = {{pelos}}, {{por}} + {{as}} = {{pelas}} (`PEH-loo`, `PEH-lah`). {{Para}} does not fuse in writing, but in speech it shrinks: {{para}} → {{pra}} and {{para o}} → {{pro}}, {{para a}} → {{pra}}: {{Vou pro trabalho}}, {{Vou pra casa}}. In writing, use {{para}}, {{para o}}, {{para a}}.

### Pairs that show the difference

| With {{para}} | With {{por}} |
|---|---|
| {{Fiz isso para você.}} (I did it for you: as a gift, for you to have it) | {{Fiz isso por você.}} (I did it for you: for your sake, because of you) |
| {{Vou para o Rio.}} (I am going to Rio: destination) | {{Passo pelo Rio.}} (I go through Rio on the way) |
| {{Trabalho para o banco.}} (My employer is the bank) | {{Trabalho pelo dinheiro.}} (Money is why I work) |

::: dica
**Test:** if you can put "in order to" in the English sentence, use {{para}} + infinitive. If you can put "because of" or "through", use {{por}}. For a place you are going to, {{para}}; for a place you go through or along, {{por}}.
:::

### The four "porquês"

The question "why?" and the answer "because" are written differently, and Brazilians care about it.

| Written | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| {{por que}} (two words) | the question | {{Por que você estuda português?}} |
| {{porque}} (one word) | the answer, "because" | {{Porque vou morar no Rio.}} |
| {{por quê}} (with an accent) | at the end of a sentence | {{Você não vai? Por quê?}} |
| {{porquê}} (one word, accent) | the noun, "the reason" | {{Não entendi o porquê.}} |

## 13.6 Joining ideas: que

### The trap: English can leave out "that", Portuguese never can

*The book I read* is a complete English phrase. The Portuguese is {{o livro que eu li}}: the relative pronoun {{que}} cannot be left out. And {{que}} translates *that*, *which* and *who* alike.

| Function | English | Portuguese |
|---|---|---|
| for a thing | *the dish that everybody orders* | {{o prato que todo mundo pede}} |
| for a person | *the woman who lives here* | {{a mulher que mora aqui}} |
| the object | *the film (that) I saw* | {{o filme que eu vi}} |
| after a verb of thought or speech | *I think (that) he is tired* | {{Eu acho que ele está cansado.}} |

::: passo 1 Use {{que}} to link a clause to a noun
Take two sentences: {{Eu conheço uma mulher. Ela trabalha na embaixada.}} Join them: {{Eu conheço uma mulher que trabalha na embaixada.}} The word {{que}} replaces the repeated noun ({{ela}}). Do not repeat the pronoun: ~~O livro que eu li ele~~.
:::

::: passo 2 Add a preposition before {{que}} when the verb needs one
- {{O lugar em que eu moro}} (the place where I live), or simply {{O lugar onde eu moro.}}
- {{A pessoa com quem eu falei}} (the person I talked to), with {{quem}} for people after a preposition.
- {{O filme de que eu gosto}} (the film I like). In speech, many Brazilians say {{O filme que eu gosto}}; the written norm keeps {{de que}}.
:::

::: passo 3 Use {{onde}} for places
{{A cidade onde eu nasci é pequena.}} {{O restaurante onde jantamos fecha às onze.}}
:::

### Que, o que, quem

| Word | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| {{que}} | relative: who, that, which; or conjunction after a verb | {{O prato que eu pedi}}; {{Eu acho que sim}} |
| {{o que}} | what | {{Não sei o que ele disse.}} / {{Tudo o que eu tenho}} |
| {{o que}} | which (referring to a whole idea) | {{Ele chegou atrasado, o que me irritou.}} |
| {{quem}} | who(m), after a preposition or as "the one who" | {{Quem chegou primeiro foi ela.}} / {{a pessoa de quem eu falei}} |
| {{onde}} | where | {{A rua onde eu moro}} |

{{Cujo}} (whose) exists, but it is formal, and you only need to recognize it: {{o homem cujo filho é médico}}. In speech people say {{o homem que o filho é médico}}.

## 13.7 Putting it together

Read this e-mail aloud. It uses all five tools.

> {{Oi, Clara! Cheguei bem em casa. A viagem foi mais longa do que eu pensava, mas o voo estava tão tranquilo quanto o ônibus de Belo Horizonte! Obrigada por tudo: pela hospedagem, pela comida e pelas dicas. O restaurante que você recomendou é o melhor da cidade, e nunca vou esquecer o peixe que comemos. Quando você puder, me manda uma mensagem. Se vier para cá, avise-me com antecedência: vou te levar para conhecer os lugares onde eu cresci. Um abraço, Emma.}}

Translation: *Hi, Clara! I got home safely. The trip was longer than I thought, but the flight was as calm as the bus in Belo Horizonte! Thank you for everything: for the hospitality, the food and the tips. The restaurant you recommended is the best in town, and I will never forget the fish we ate. When you can, send me a message. If you come here, let me know in advance: I will take you to see the places where I grew up. A hug, Emma.*

Two verbs in this e-mail, {{puder}} and {{vier}}, are in a form (the future subjunctive) that this book does not teach. Treat {{Quando você puder}} (when you can) and {{Se vier}} (if you come) as ready-made chunks. Now find the comparisons ({{mais longa do que}}, {{tão tranquilo quanto}}, {{o melhor da cidade}}), the command ({{avise-me}}, formal and written), the pronouns ({{me manda}}, {{te levar}}), {{por}} ({{por tudo}}, {{pela hospedagem}}, {{pelas dicas}}), {{para}} ({{para cá}}, {{para conhecer}}) and {{que}}/{{onde}} ({{que você recomendou}}, {{que comemos}}, {{onde eu cresci}}).

New words: {{o voo}} `oo VOH-oo` (the flight), {{a hospedagem}} `ah ohs-peh-DAH-zheh~ee` (the hospitality, lodging), {{antecedência}} `ah~-teh-seh-DEH~-see-ah` (advance notice), {{crescer}} `kreh-SER` (to grow up).

## 13.8 The mistakes English speakers make

::: erros
| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ele é mais alto do que mim. | {{Ele é mais alto do que eu.}} | after {{do que}} use the subject pronoun |
| Quero uma mesa. (to a waiter, as your first words) | {{Eu queria uma mesa, por favor.}} | {{queria}} is the polite "I'd like"; the blunt form sounds abrupt |
| Você poderia me ajuda? | {{Você poderia me ajudar?}} | after {{poder}} comes the infinitive |
| Eu gostaria um quarto. | {{Eu gostaria de um quarto.}} | {{gostar}} and {{gostaria}} always take {{de}} |
| Fala mais devagar, por favor. (said to a stranger) | {{Fale mais devagar, por favor.}} | with strangers and older people, the formal command is safer |
| Obrigado para a ajuda. | {{Obrigado pela ajuda.}} | thanks use {{por}} |
| Estudo português por trabalhar no Brasil. | {{Estudo português para trabalhar no Brasil.}} | purpose uses {{para}} + infinitive |

| Paguei 20 reais para o livro. | {{Paguei 20 reais pelo livro.}} | price/exchange uses {{por}} |

## 13.11 Pocket summary: Lesson 13

::: resumo
- **Compare:** {{mais/menos}} + adjective + {{do que}}: {{mais caro do que}}. Equal: {{tão ... quanto}}, {{tanto(s)/tanta(s) ... quanto}}. Numbers: {{mais de}}, {{menos de}}.
- **Irregular:** {{melhor}}, {{pior}}, {{maior}}, {{menor}}. Never {{mais bom}}.
- **Superlative:** {{o mais caro do cardápio}}, {{os melhores da cidade}}.
- **Imperative ({{você/vocês}}):** from the {{eu}} form, drop {{-o}}, add {{-e/-em}} for {{-ar}} or {{-a/-am}} for {{-er/-ir}}. Irregular: {{seja, esteja, vá, dê, saiba}}. Negative: {{não}} + the same form. Friends: {{Fala! Vem! Me liga!}}
- **Polite ladder:** {{Quero}} → {{Eu queria}} → {{Você poderia...?}} → {{Gostaria de...}} / {{Seria possível...?}} Plus {{Pode...?}}, {{Dá pra...?}}.
- **Para:** destination, purpose (+ infinitive), recipient, deadline, opinion. **Por:** cause, through, duration, price, means, per. {{pelo, pela}}; spoken {{pro, pra}}.
- **Porquês:** {{por que}} (question), {{porque}} (because), {{por quê}} (end), {{porquê}} (the reason).
- **Que:** links clauses, never omitted: {{o livro que eu li}}. With a preposition: {{em que}} / {{onde}}, {{com quem}}. {{O que}}: what, and "which" for a whole idea.
:::

## 13.12 A2 checkpoint

You have reached the end of the book. This checklist shows what you should be able to do in Portuguese now. Take it slowly, with a pen. For each statement, test yourself out loud or in writing. Tick a statement only when you can do it **without looking at the lesson**.

1. **Sounds (Lesson 1).** I can read any Portuguese word aloud with the right stress, a nasal vowel ({{pão}}), the *tch* and *dj* sounds ({{dia}}, {{noite}}), and the "h" sound of the initial *r* ({{rua}}), and spell my name with the Portuguese letter names. *Test:* read the paragraph in Lesson 1.13 aloud, then spell your full name.
2. **Who I am (Lesson 2).** I can say who I am, where I am from, my nationality and my job with {{ser}}, use {{eu, você, a gente, nós, vocês, eles}} correctly, and make adjectives agree. *Test:* introduce yourself and a friend in six sentences.
3. **Things (Lesson 3).** I can choose {{o, a, os, as, um, uma}} correctly, guess gender from the ending, make plurals, and use {{no, na, do, da}}. *Test:* name ten objects in your room with the right article.
4. **Habits and questions (Lesson 4).** I can use regular {{-ar}}, {{-er}} and {{-ir}} verbs in the present, make negatives with {{não}}, and ask with {{quem, o que, onde, quando, como}}. *Test:* write five things you do on Saturday and five questions to a friend.
5. **Being, having and existing (Lesson 5).** I can choose between {{ser}} and {{estar}}, say where things are, and say that something exists with {{tem}} or {{há}}. *Test:* describe your bedroom in six sentences.
6. **Numbers and time (Lesson 6).** I can say numbers up to a thousand, tell the time, say a date and ask and give a price. *Test:* say today's date, the time, and the price of a coffee.
7. **Wanting and being able (Lesson 7).** I can use {{ir, ter, fazer, poder, querer, saber, vir, dizer, ver}} in the present, and distinguish {{saber}} from {{conhecer}}. *Test:* ask three polite questions with {{poder}} and say three things you want to do.
8. **Mine, yours, this one (Lesson 8).** I can say whose something is with {{meu, seu, dele, dela}}, point with {{esse, aquele, aqui, ali}}, and say what I like and prefer with {{gostar de}} and {{preferir}}. *Test:* talk for thirty seconds about your family and what you like.
9. **My day and my plans (Lesson 9).** I can describe my routine with reflexive verbs, say what I am going to do with {{ir}} + infinitive, and say what I am doing with {{estar}} + gerund. *Test:* describe a normal day, tomorrow's plan and what you are doing now.
10. **The past (Lessons 10 and 11).** I can tell what happened with the simple past ({{fui, fiz, vi, comi}}), describe how things were with the imperfect ({{era, tinha, morava}}) and choose between them. *Test:* tell a ten-sentence story about last weekend, with at least three descriptions.
11. **Small words (Lesson 12).** I can use {{me}} and {{te}} before the verb, say {{vi ele}} and {{dei pra ela}} as Brazilians do, use {{mim}}, {{comigo}} and {{para eu fazer}}, and ask {{Você pode me ajudar?}} *Test:* write five messages to a friend with {{me}} or {{te}}.
12. **Asking, comparing, joining (Lesson 13).** I can compare things, give an instruction or a direction, make a request with {{queria}}, {{poderia}} or {{gostaria}}, choose {{por}} or {{para}}, and join ideas with {{que}}. *Test:* in a restaurant role-play, ask for a table, a recommendation and the bill.

**How to read your result.** If you can tick **ten to twelve**, you work comfortably at the A2 level and are ready to move on to the next book. If you tick **seven to nine**, go back to the lessons you did not tick and redo their practice before moving on. If you tick **fewer than seven**, do not worry: go back to the first lesson you did not tick and work forward one lesson per week, saying every exercise aloud. In every case, spend ten minutes a day listening to Brazilian Portuguese: podcasts, music, WhatsApp voice messages from your friends. The grammar in this book is the skeleton; the listening will give it flesh.

## 13.13 What comes next

This is the last lesson of the book. You started by learning to read {{bom dia}} aloud. You can now tell a story, ask for things politely, find your way around a Brazilian city, and make a friend feel that you are speaking their language. The next step is to use it every day: with Clara's cake, Seu Antônio's stories, a WhatsApp group, a short trip. At the A2 level you will make mistakes. That is not a problem. Brazilians are generous with learners, and every mistake you correct brings you closer to a natural voice.
