# How to use this book

This book teaches you the Portuguese that Brazilians speak, from your first greeting to telling a story about last weekend. It is built for adults who speak English, who have little or no Portuguese, and who want to be understood in real situations: a café, a hostel, a job interview, a dinner with a Brazilian family. It is not a phrasebook. It explains why Portuguese works the way it does, using the habits of English as the starting point, so that you can build your own sentences instead of repeating someone else's.

## 0.1 How to study with this book

Read one lesson in three or four sittings, not one. A lesson has about 4,000 to 6,000 words, and your memory will not absorb that in an hour. A good rhythm is twenty or thirty minutes a day:

1. **Day 1.** Read the opening scene aloud and the first explanations. Do not try to memorize anything.
2. **Day 2.** Finish the lesson. Say every example out loud, not in your head. Portuguese lives in the mouth.
3. **Day 3.** Do the practice, check the answer key, and write down the mistakes you made. Those mistakes are your real syllabus.
4. **Day 4 and after.** Answer the three questions in the **Return** section, which bring back earlier lessons just when you are about to forget them.

Four habits make the biggest difference:

- **Speak from the first day.** Say the sentences, answer the questions in the practice aloud, and talk to yourself in the shower. A sentence you have said ten times is a sentence you own.
- **Learn blocks, not single words.** Learn {{Moro em Londres}} ("I live in London") as one piece, then change the city. The book gives you many such blocks.
- **Use the audio.** Every example has audio in the Linvuu course at linvuu.com.br. Listen first, repeat second.
- **Welcome your mistakes.** Brazilians are generous with learners, and each correction teaches you more than a page of rules.

## 0.2 Brazilian and European Portuguese

Portuguese is the official language of nine countries, and roughly 200 million of its speakers live in Brazil. This book teaches **Brazilian Portuguese**, the variety you will hear in Brazilian cities, films, music, podcasts and business meetings.

The two main standards are the same language, in the way American and British English are the same language. A Brazilian and a Portuguese person understand each other without trouble in writing and, with a little patience, in speech. The differences you should know about:

| | Brazil | Portugal |
|---|---|---|
| Sound | open, clear vowels; final *e* sounds like *ee*; *t* and *d* before *i* become *tch* and *dj* | many unstressed vowels almost disappear; *s* at the end of a syllable sounds like *sh* |
| Words | {{ônibus}}, {{trem}}, {{café da manhã}}, {{celular}}, {{banheiro}} | {{autocarro}}, {{comboio}}, {{pequeno-almoço}}, {{telemóvel}}, {{casa de banho}} |
| "You" | {{você}} for almost everyone | {{você}} is used less; {{tu}} and the person's name or title are more common |
| Progressive | {{Estou trabalhando}} | {{Estou a trabalhar}} |
| Pronouns | {{Me ajuda}}, {{Eu vi ele}} in speech | {{Ajuda-me}}, {{Vi-o}} |

Since the 2009 spelling reform, the two spellings are almost identical, with a few words that still differ ({{fato}} in Brazil, {{facto}} in Portugal). When the European form matters, the book gives it in a short note, and that is all. If you later move to Lisbon, you will need a few weeks to adjust your ear, not a new course.

Brazil itself has many accents: the *sh*-like *s* of Rio, the open vowels of the Northeast, the clear *r* of the South. The pronunciation guides in this book describe the standard you hear in São Paulo and on national television, which is understood everywhere.

## 0.3 What A1 and A2 mean

Language levels follow the Common European Framework of Reference, known as the CEFR. You will see two of its levels here.

**A1, the beginner.** You can introduce yourself and others, ask and answer simple questions about where you live and what you have, use numbers, times and prices, and take part in a slow, friendly conversation. Lessons 1 to 8 give you this base.

**A2, the elementary user.** You can describe your routine, talk about past events, shop, ask for directions, make polite requests and write short messages about familiar things. Lessons 9 to 13 take you there.

These levels are modest, and that is the point. A1 and A2 are where learners most often stall, because the verbs, the pronouns and the two past tenses all arrive at once. This book slows down exactly there. When you finish, you will not speak like a native, but you will be able to live a normal day in Portuguese and keep learning on your own.

## 0.4 How to read the pronunciation guides

Next to new Portuguese words you will see a guide in backticks, like `bah-NAH-nah`. It is written with English sounds, so you do not need a special alphabet. Capital letters mark the stressed syllable. A tilde (`~`) marks a nasal vowel. The letters `tch` and `dj` stand for the sounds in *church* and *judge*, `zh` for the *s* in *measure*, `ly` and `ny` for the Portuguese *lh* and *nh*, and a plain `h` at the start of a word for the Brazilian *r*.

Lesson 1 explains the whole system in section 1.3 and teaches the sounds behind it. If a guide ever looks strange, go back to that table. After a few weeks you will stop needing the guides, and the book gradually gives them only for new words.

For **o**, the guide writes the open sound as `aw` and the closed sound as `oh`. One honest limit: a guide cannot show the melody of a sentence or the difference between an open and a closed **e** (both are written `eh`). Use the audio for those.

## 0.5 What you will find in each lesson

Every lesson has the same shape, so you always know where you are:

- an opening **scene**, a short situation you could really live, so that the grammar has a purpose
- numbered sections (2.1, 2.2, and so on) that build the idea step by step
- **Tip** boxes for shortcuts and **Watch out** boxes for traps
- **Typical mistakes** tables in three columns: *Wrong*, *Right*, *Why*. The wrong sentences are what English speakers really say; the *Why* column names the habit behind each one
- **Practice** in five parts, A to E, going from recognizing to producing
- an **Answer key** that explains the tricky items, not only the answers
- a **Return** section with three questions from earlier lessons
- a **Pocket summary** you can photograph and keep on your phone

## 0.7 Before you start

Decide three things now. When in the day will you study? A fixed time beats a good intention. Where can you speak aloud without feeling shy? And who can you greet in Portuguese this week? The sooner someone hears your first {{Bom dia}}, the sooner this book becomes real. Turn the page, and let us start with the sounds.
