# The imperfect, and choosing between the two pasts

By the end of this lesson you can describe how things used to be, say what was happening when something else happened, say how you felt at a moment in the past, and choose between {{fui}}-type and {{ia}}-type verbs by asking one question: is this a event in the story, or the background to the story?

## 11.1 A scene you will live

Your neighbor Seu Antônio, who is seventy-two, sits in front of the building every afternoon. Today you stop and ask him about his childhood.

> **Emma:** {{Seu Antônio, como era a sua infância?}}
> **Seu Antônio:** {{Ah, eu morava numa fazenda em Minas. A casa era pequena, mas tinha um quintal enorme. Todo dia eu acordava às cinco e ajudava meu pai.}}
> **Emma:** {{E o senhor gostava?}}
> **Seu Antônio:** {{Gostava! Mas um dia, eu tinha dez anos, caí do cavalo e quebrei o braço. Que susto!}}
> **Emma:** {{Nossa! E o senhor estava com medo?}}
> **Seu Antônio:** {{Estava, claro. Mas no dia seguinte eu já estava de novo no cavalo.}}

Translation: *Seu Antônio, what was your childhood like? / Ah, I lived on a farm in Minas. The house was small, but it had an enormous backyard. Every day I woke up at five and helped my father. / And did you like it? / I did! But one day, when I was ten, I fell off the horse and broke my arm. What a scare! / Wow! And were you afraid? / Yes, of course. But the next day I was already on the horse again.*

Count the verbs. Most of them end in {{-ava}}, {{-ia}} or {{-ava}}-like forms: {{morava}}, {{era}}, {{tinha}}, {{acordava}}, {{ajudava}}, {{gostava}}, {{tinha}}, {{estava}}. They describe the **setting**: where he lived, what the house was like, what he did every day, how old he was. Only three verbs are different: {{caí}}, {{quebrei}} and {{foi}}-type events (the fall, the broken arm). Those are the **events** of the story.

This is the central idea of the lesson, and the rest follows from it.

|---|---|---|
| {{a infância}} | `ah ee~-FAH~-syah` | childhood |
| {{a fazenda}} | `ah fah-ZEH~-dah` | farm, ranch |
| {{o quintal}} | `oo kee~-TAH-oo` | backyard |
| {{enorme}} | `eh-NOR-mee` | enormous |
| {{o cavalo}} | `oo kah-VAH-loo` | horse |
| {{o braço}} | `oo BRAH-soo` | arm |
| {{cair}} | `kah-EER` | to fall |
| {{quebrar}} | `keh-BRAR` | to break |

| {{o senhor, a senhora}} | `oo seh-NYOR, ah seh-NYOH-rah` | you (polite, for an older or respected person) |

## 11.2 The trap: English has three ways to say it, Portuguese has one tense and a choice

Look at what English does with *to live* in the past:

| English | What it means |
|---|---|
| *I lived in Rio.* | a period, probably over |
| *I used to live in Rio.* | a former state or habit |
| *I was living in Rio when…* | in progress at a moment |
| *I would go to the beach every summer.* | a repeated habit |

English separates these with **different structures**: *used to*, *would*, *was -ing*. Portuguese uses **one tense** for the last three, the imperfect, and keeps the simple past from Lesson 10 for the first.

| English | Portuguese |
|---|---|
| *I used to live in Rio.* | {{Eu morava no Rio.}} |
| *I was living in Rio.* | {{Eu morava no Rio.}} / {{Eu estava morando no Rio.}} |
| *I would go to the beach every summer.* | {{Eu ia à praia todo verão.}} |
| *I lived in Rio for two years.* | {{Eu morei no Rio por dois anos.}} |

The second trap is the other way round. Where English uses one past for two ideas, Portuguese forces you to choose. *I knew her* can mean "I was acquainted with her" or "I met her". Portuguese has {{eu a conhecia}} and {{eu a conheci}}, and they are not the same sentence. That is why this lesson is the most important one of the past tenses: it is not about forms, it is about **point of view**.

::: comparacao
| The simple past (Lesson 10) | The imperfect (this lesson) |
|---|---|
| a camera photograph: the event happened and is complete | a film in progress: the situation, with no start and no end in view |
| *what happened* | *what it was like / what used to happen / what was going on* |
| {{fiz, comi, fui}} | {{fazia, comia, ia}} |
:::

## 11.3 The forms: easy, and almost without exceptions

Take the infinitive, remove {{-r}}, and add the endings.

| Person | {{-ar}}: {{falar}} | {{-er}}: {{comer}} | {{-ir}}: {{abrir}} |
|---|---|---|---|
| eu | {{falava}} `fah-LAH-vah` | {{comia}} `koh-MEE-ah` | {{abria}} `ah-BREE-ah` |
| você, ele, ela, a gente | {{falava}} | {{comia}} | {{abria}} |
| nós | {{falávamos}} `fah-LAH-vah-mooss` | {{comíamos}} `koh-MEE-ah-mooss` | {{abríamos}} `ah-BREE-ah-mooss` |
| vocês, eles, elas | {{falavam}} `fah-LAH-vah~oo` | {{comiam}} `koh-MEE-ah~oo` | {{abriam}} `ah-BREE-ah~oo` |

The pattern is simple: {{-ava}} for {{-ar}} verbs, {{-ia}} for {{-er}} and {{-ir}} verbs. The {{eu}} form and the {{você/ele/ela}} form are **identical**, so when it matters, say the pronoun: {{eu morava}}, {{ela morava}}. The {{nós}} form carries an accent on the syllable before the ending ({{falávamos}}, {{comíamos}}). With {{a gente}}, use the singular form: {{a gente morava}}.

Good news: there are no spelling changes (nothing like {{fiquei}} or {{cheguei}} here), and almost no irregular verbs. **Only four verbs are irregular** in the imperfect:

| Infinitive | eu, você, ele, ela | nós | eles |
|---|---|---|---|
| {{ser}} | {{era}} `EH-rah` | {{éramos}} `EH-rah-mooss` | {{eram}} `EH-rah~oo` |
| {{ter}} | {{tinha}} `TCHEE-nyah` | {{tínhamos}} `TCHEE-nyah-mooss` | {{tinham}} `TCHEE-nyah~oo` |
| {{vir}} | {{vinha}} `VEE-nyah` | {{vínhamos}} `VEE-nyah-mooss` | {{vinham}} `VEE-nyah~oo` |
| {{pôr}} | {{punha}} `POO-nyah` | {{púnhamos}} `POO-nyah-mooss` | {{punham}} `POO-nyah~oo` |

Everything else is regular. This includes verbs that are very irregular in the simple past:

- {{ir}} is regular: {{ia}} `EE-ah`, {{íamos}}, {{iam}}
- {{ver}} is regular: {{via}} `VEE-ah`
- {{fazer}} is regular: {{fazia}} `fah-ZEE-ah`
- {{dizer}} is regular: {{dizia}} `djee-ZEE-ah`
- {{poder}}: {{podia}}; {{querer}}: {{queria}}; {{saber}}: {{sabia}}; {{estar}}: {{estava}} `ehs-TAH-vah`

::: dica
Learn first the forms you will use for describing: {{era}}, {{tinha}}, {{estava}}, {{ia}}, {{fazia}}. They give you almost every description of the past. If you know these five, you can already say *it was late*, *I had ten years*, *I was tired*, *I was going to*, *it was cold*.
:::

## 11.4 What the imperfect does

Five uses cover nearly everything. Each one follows from the idea of the background.

::: passo 1 Describe people, things, time and weather in the past
The imperfect paints the scene.

- {{Era tarde e fazia frio.}} (It was late and it was cold.)
- {{A casa era pequena e tinha um quintal enorme.}} (The house was small and had a huge backyard.)
- {{Eu tinha dez anos.}} (I was ten years old.) Age uses {{ter}}, as in Lesson 2.
- {{Ela era muito simpática.}} (She was very nice.)
- {{Eram quatro horas.}} (It was four o'clock.) Notice that the plural {{eram}} matches {{horas}}, as {{são}} does in the present.
:::

::: passo 2 Talk about habits and repeated actions in the past
Whatever you did regularly, you did in the imperfect. English often uses *used to* or *would*.

- {{Todo domingo a gente almoçava na casa da minha avó.}} (Every Sunday we had lunch at my grandmother's house.)
- {{Eu jogava futebol com os meus primos.}} (I used to play football with my cousins.)
- {{Naquela época, a gente não tinha celular.}} (In those days we did not have mobile phones.)

You can also use {{costumar}} `kohs-too-MAR` (to be used to) in the imperfect, followed by an infinitive: {{Eu costumava acordar cedo}} (I used to wake up early).
:::

::: passo 3 Say what was going on when something happened
The imperfect, or {{estar}} in the imperfect + gerund (Lesson 9), tells what was in progress. A short event interrupts it, and that event is in the simple past.

- {{Eu estava saindo quando o telefone tocou.}} (I was leaving when the phone rang.)
- {{Ela dormia quando você chegou.}} (She was asleep when you arrived.)
- {{Estava chovendo quando saímos.}} (It was raining when we left.)

Both {{eu saía}} and {{eu estava saindo}} are possible for the first part. The {{estar}} version is more common in everyday speech to say *in progress at that moment*.
:::

::: passo 4 Report what someone said or thought
In reported speech, the tense moves back, as in English, and the imperfect is the natural choice.

- {{Ela disse que estava cansada.}} (She said she was tired.)
- {{Ele falou que ia chegar tarde.}} (He said he was going to be late.)
- {{Eu achava que você morava em São Paulo.}} (I thought you lived in São Paulo.)
:::

::: passo 5 Be polite or soften a request
The imperfect of some verbs is used in the present to sound gentler. {{Eu queria um café}} means *I'd like a coffee*, not "I wanted". {{Você podia me ajudar?}} is *could you help me?* This use returns in Lesson 13.
:::

### "He was" and "he was with": estava and estava com

Feelings and physical states are background, so they use the imperfect, and Portuguese has two structures for them.

| Structure | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{estar}} + adjective | {{Ele estava cansado.}} | He was tired. |
| {{estar com}} + noun | {{Ele estava com fome.}} | He was hungry. |
| | {{Eu estava com sono.}} | I was sleepy. |
| | {{Nós estávamos com frio.}} | We were cold. |
| | {{Ela estava com medo.}} | She was afraid. |
| | {{Eu estava com dor de cabeça.}} | I had a headache. |

The same words in other tenses show the contrast:

| Tense | Example | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| present | {{Estou com fome.}} | I am hungry (now) |
| imperfect | {{Eu estava com fome.}} | I was hungry (state in the past) |
| simple past | {{Eu fiquei com fome.}} | I became hungry (the change, the event) |

With feelings, English says *I was afraid*, and that is always a state. Portuguese needs to know whether you mean "I was afraid" ({{estava com medo}}) or "I got scared" ({{fiquei com medo}}). Seu Antônio said {{estava com medo}} because he was describing his condition when he fell. If something had frightened him suddenly, he would have said {{fiquei com medo}}.

Brazilians also say {{ter fome}}, {{ter sono}}, {{ter medo}}, but {{estar com}} is the most common in conversation, so use it.

## 11.5 Choosing between the two pasts

Here is the method. For every verb in the past, ask one question.

::: passo 1 Is this a step in the story, or the setting?
- A **step in the story** (something happened, changed the situation, and is complete): simple past. *First I woke up. Then the phone rang. Then I went out.*
- The **setting** (a state, a habit, a description, something that was in progress): imperfect. *It was raining. I was tired. I used to work there.*
:::

::: passo 2 Use the time words as a clue, not as a rule
Time words help you, but the point of view decides.

| Usually the imperfect | Usually the simple past |
|---|---|
| {{sempre}} | {{ontem}} |
| {{todo dia}}, {{toda semana}} | {{de repente}} |
| {{às vezes}} | {{uma vez}}, {{duas vezes}} |
| {{naquela época}}, {{antigamente}} (in the old days) | {{na sexta-feira}}, {{em 2019}} |
| {{quando eu era criança}} | {{por dois anos}}, {{o dia todo}} (a closed period) |
| {{enquanto}} (while) | {{então}}, {{depois}} |
:::

::: passo 3 Put the two together in one sentence
A story alternates: one thing in progress, then an event.

- {{Eu morava no Rio quando conheci a Clara.}} (I was living in Rio when I met Clara.)
- {{Estava calor, então a gente foi à praia.}} (It was hot, so we went to the beach.)
- {{Eu andava pela rua quando vi um acidente.}} (I was walking down the street when I saw an accident.)
:::

### The same verb, two meanings

The same verb in the two tenses can give two different sentences. This is where you see the choice most clearly.

| Imperfect | Simple past |
|---|---|
| {{Eu morava no Rio.}} I lived in Rio (a situation, the period is open) | {{Eu morei no Rio por dois anos.}} I lived in Rio for two years (a closed period) |
| {{Ela estudava inglês.}} She was studying / used to study English | {{Ela estudou inglês e passou no exame.}} She studied English and passed the exam |
| {{Chovia.}} It was raining (background) | {{Choveu.}} It rained (the event: "it rained yesterday") |
| {{Eu tinha uma bicicleta.}} I had a bike (a possession) | {{Eu tive uma ideia.}} I had an idea (a moment) |

### Four verbs change their meaning

Here the choice is not nuance: it is a different translation. Learn these four pairs by heart.

| Verb | Imperfect | Simple past |
|---|---|---|
| {{saber}} | {{Eu sabia a verdade.}} I knew the truth. | {{Eu soube da notícia ontem.}} I found out the news yesterday. |
| {{conhecer}} | {{Eu conhecia a Marina.}} I knew Marina. | {{Eu conheci a Marina no Rio.}} I met Marina in Rio. |
| {{querer}} | {{Eu queria ir.}} I wanted to go. (a wish; maybe I did not) | {{Eu quis ir.}} I tried / insisted on going. {{Eu não quis ir.}} I refused to go. |
| {{poder}} | {{Eu podia ir.}} I was able to go / I could go. (it was possible) | {{Eu pude ir.}} I managed to go. {{Eu não pude ir.}} I could not go (and did not). |

A very common combination is {{Eu queria ir, mas não pude}} (I wanted to go, but I could not): the wish in the imperfect, the result in the simple past.

::: atencao
**Do not use the simple past for a description just because the thing is finished.** English speakers say *The house was small* and think "it is past, finished", so they use the simple past: ~~A casa foi pequena~~. Portuguese uses the simple past only when the sentence tells a **complete event**. A description is a state, so: {{A casa era pequena}}.
:::

## 11.6 For recognition: the past before the past

Sometimes you need to say that something had already happened before another past event: *When I arrived, Clara had already left.* English uses *had left*. Portuguese has a one-word form for it (the **mais-que-perfeito**, {{saíra}}), but it is used only in literary writing. In speech, Brazilians use the imperfect of {{ter}} + the past participle:

| Person | {{ter}} (imperfect) | Participle |
|---|---|---|
| eu, ele, ela, você | {{tinha}} | {{falado}}, {{comido}}, {{aberto}} |
| nós | {{tínhamos}} | |
| eles | {{tinham}} | |

- {{Quando eu cheguei, a Clara já tinha saído.}} (When I arrived, Clara had already left.)
- {{Eu já tinha visto esse filme.}} (I had already seen that film.)
- {{Eles não tinham comido nada.}} (They had not eaten anything.)

The participle is built from the stem + {{-ado}} for {{-ar}} verbs and {{-ido}} for {{-er}} and {{-ir}} verbs: {{falado}}, {{comido}}, {{saído}}. A few common verbs are irregular:

| Infinitive | Participle |
|---|---|
| {{fazer}} | {{feito}} `FAY-too` |
| {{dizer}} | {{dito}} `DJEE-too` |
| {{ver}} | {{visto}} `VEES-too` |
| {{escrever}} | {{escrito}} `ehs-KREE-too` |
| {{abrir}} | {{aberto}} `ah-BEHR-too` |
| {{pôr}} | {{posto}} `POHS-too` |
| {{vir}} | {{vindo}} `VEE~-doo` |

At this level, you only need to **recognize** {{tinha + participle}}. If you cannot use it yet, two simple pasts and {{já}} will get your meaning across.

## 11.7 Telling a story: a model

A story needs both. This is how a short anecdote looks. Read it aloud and underline the verbs in the imperfect (setting) and in the simple past (events).

> {{Era uma sexta-feira à noite e eu estava voltando do trabalho. Chovia muito e eu não tinha guarda-chuva. De repente, um ônibus passou e molhou toda a minha roupa. Eu fiquei com muita raiva, mas depois comecei a rir. Quando cheguei em casa, a Clara me viu e perguntou: "O que aconteceu?" Eu respondi: "Nada, só fui tomar um banho de graça!"}}

Translation: *It was a Friday night and I was coming back from work. It was raining hard and I did not have an umbrella. Suddenly, a bus went by and soaked all my clothes. I got really angry, but then I started to laugh. When I got home, Clara saw me and asked: "What happened?" I answered: "Nothing, I just took a free shower!"*

The scene is set with {{era}}, {{estava voltando}}, {{chovia}}, {{não tinha}}. The events follow in order: {{passou}}, {{molhou}}, {{fiquei}}, {{comecei}}, {{cheguei}}, {{viu}}, {{perguntou}}, {{respondi}}, {{fui}}. Notice {{fiquei com raiva}} ("I got angry"), a change of state, and {{aconteceu}}, the simple past in a question about an event.

New words: {{molhar}} `moh-LYAR` (to wet), {{a roupa}} `ah HOH-pah` (clothes), {{a raiva}} `ah HIE-vah` (anger), {{rir}} `heer` (to laugh), {{acontecer}} `ah-koh~-teh-SER` (to happen), {{de graça}} `djee GRAH-sah` (for free).

### A starter for stories

{{Era uma vez}} `EH-rah OO-mah vehss` is "once upon a time", and it is followed by the imperfect. In a casual anecdote, the starters are {{Uma vez eu...}} (once I...) and {{Outro dia eu...}} (the other day I...).

## 11.8 The mistakes English speakers make

::: erros
| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A casa foi pequena. | {{A casa era pequena.}} | a description is a state, so the imperfect |
| Quando eu fui criança, eu... | {{Quando eu era criança, eu...}} | "when I was a child" describes a period of life |
| Todo verão eu fui à praia. | {{Todo verão eu ia à praia.}} | a repeated habit in the past |
| Eu estava dez anos. | {{Eu tinha dez anos.}} | age uses {{ter}} |
| Ele fazia um bolo ontem e saiu. | {{Ele fez um bolo ontem.}} | a completed event needs the simple past |
| Eu sabi a resposta. | {{Eu sabia a resposta.}} | {{-er}} imperfect ends in {{-ia}} |
| Nós falavamos. | {{Nós falávamos.}} | the {{nós}} form carries an accent |
| Eu fui conhecer a Marina há muito tempo e ela era minha amiga. | {{Eu a conheci há muito tempo.}} | "met" is the simple past of {{conhecer}} |
| Eu não podia ir ontem, então não fui. (meaning I tried and failed) | {{Eu não pude ir ontem.}} | specific attempt, specific result: simple past |
| Eu estava com medo quando o cachorro latiu. (meaning: I got a fright) | {{Fiquei com medo quando o cachorro latiu.}} | a sudden change is an event |
| Quando ele chegou, eu cozinhei. (meaning: I was cooking) | {{Quando ele chegou, eu estava cozinhando.}} | something in progress when the other event happened |
:::

## 11.11 Pocket summary: Lesson 11

::: resumo
- **Forms:** {{-ar}} → {{-ava}}; {{-er}}, {{-ir}} → {{-ia}}. {{Nós}}: {{falávamos}}, {{comíamos}}. {{Eu}} = {{você}} = {{ele}}: say the pronoun when it matters.
- **Only four irregular verbs:** {{era}}, {{tinha}}, {{vinha}}, {{punha}}. {{Ir}} ({{ia}}), {{ver}} ({{via}}), {{fazer}} ({{fazia}}), {{dizer}} ({{dizia}}) are regular.
- **Uses:** description ({{era tarde}}, {{tinha dez anos}}); habit ({{todo dia eu acordava}}); in progress ({{estava saindo}}); reported speech ({{disse que ia}}); politeness ({{queria um café}}).
- **Feelings:** {{estava cansado}}, {{estava com fome}}. A change: {{fiquei com fome}}.
- **Choose:** event in the story → simple past; setting, habit, state → imperfect.
- **Four verbs change meaning:** {{sabia / soube}}, {{conhecia / conheci}}, {{queria / quis}}, {{podia / pude}}.
- **Past before past (recognition):** {{tinha}} + participle: {{já tinha saído}}.
- **Starters:** {{Era uma vez...}}, {{Quando eu era criança...}}, {{Naquela época...}}
:::
