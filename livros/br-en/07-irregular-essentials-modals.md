# Irregular essentials and modals: go, do, can, want, know

By the end of this lesson you can conjugate the ten most useful irregular verbs in the present tense, ask permission and make requests with {{poder}}, say what you want and what you have to do, and tell {{saber}} from {{conhecer}}.

## 7.1 A scene you will live

Sam, who works in São Paulo for a few months, is planning Saturday with two colleagues in a bar after work.

::: dialogo
**Bruno:** {{E aí, Sam? O que você quer fazer no sábado?}}
**Sam:** {{Quero conhecer o centro. Vocês podem ir comigo?}}
**Ana:** {{Eu posso, mas só de tarde. De manhã tenho que trabalhar.}}
**Bruno:** {{Eu vou também. Você sabe pegar o metrô?}}
**Sam:** {{Sei, sim. Mas não sei onde fica a estação.}}
**Bruno:** {{Sem problema. Eu vou com você.}}
**Ana:** {{Combinado! Às duas na estação.}}
:::

*Bruno: So, Sam? What do you want to do on Saturday? / Sam: I want to see downtown. Can you come with me? / Ana: I can, but only in the afternoon. In the morning I have to work. / Bruno: I'm going too. Do you know how to take the subway? / Sam: Yes, I do. But I don't know where the station is. / Bruno: No problem. I'll go with you. / Ana: Agreed! At two at the station.*

Count the irregular verbs: {{quer}}, {{podem}}, {{posso}}, {{tenho}}, {{vou}}, {{sabe}}, {{sei}}. They look nothing like the regular patterns of Lesson 4, and they are the verbs of daily life: wanting, being able, going, knowing. Notice a second thing: after {{querer}}, {{poder}} and {{saber}}, the next verb stays in the infinitive, with no "to" and no preposition: {{quero conhecer}}, {{podem ir}}, {{sabe pegar}}. That one pattern covers almost everything in this lesson.

| Word | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{E aí?}} | `ee ah-EE` | So? What's up? (a casual greeting) |
| {{conhecer}} | `koh-nyeh-SEHR` | to get to know, to visit a place for the first time |
| {{comigo}} | `koh-MEE-goo` | with me |
| {{só}} | `saw` | only, just |
| {{o metrô}} | `oo meh-TROH` | the subway |
| {{a estação}} | `ah ehs-tah-SOW~` | the station |
| {{sem problema}} | `seh~ee proh-BLEH-mah` | no problem |
| {{combinado}} | `koh~-bee-NAH-doo` | agreed, deal |

## 7.2 The trap: irregular, but in a pattern

Four habits cause trouble here.

1. **Irregular verbs change inside.** English *go / went* is irregular too, but Portuguese irregularity is concentrated in the {{eu}} form: {{faço}}, {{digo}}, {{trago}}, {{sei}}. The other forms are mostly regular.
2. **"Can" is a full verb.** English *can* has no endings. Portuguese {{poder}} changes with the person: {{posso}}, {{pode}}, {{podemos}}, {{podem}}.
3. **The next verb stays in the infinitive.** Not "I want to go" with a particle: {{Quero ir}}. Not "I can to go": {{Posso ir}}.
4. **One "to know" in English, two in Portuguese.** {{Saber}} is for facts and skills; {{conhecer}} is for people and places.

## 7.3 Ir: to go

{{Ir}} is the most irregular verb after {{ser}}. It does not resemble its infinitive at all.

| Person | Form | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| eu | {{vou}} | `VOH` |
| você / ele / ela / a gente | {{vai}} | `vie` |
| nós | {{vamos}} | `VAH-mooss` |
| vocês / eles / elas | {{vão}} | `VOW~` |

{{Ir}} takes a place with a preposition: {{ir a}} (formal, with the fusions {{ao}}, {{à}}), {{ir para}} (spoken {{pra}}, {{pro}}), and in speech simply {{ir em}}. All of these are correct in different registers: {{Vou ao banco}}, {{Vou para casa}}, {{Vou pro trabalho}}, {{Vou no banco}}.

| Portuguese | Meaning |
|---|---|
| {{Vou para casa.}} | I'm going home. |
| {{Vou de ônibus.}} | I'm going by bus. |
| {{Vou a pé.}} | I'm walking (literally: I go on foot). |
| {{Vamos?}} | Shall we go? / Let's go? |
| {{Vamos comer!}} | Let's eat! |
| {{Já vou!}} | I'm coming! (literally: I'm already going) |
| {{Vou embora.}} | I'm leaving. |

::: atencao
**English "I'm coming" is {{Já vou}}, not {{Já venho}}.** In Portuguese you use {{ir}} when you move toward **the person you are talking to** or toward a place the listener will be: {{Já vou!}}, {{Eu vou aí}}. You use {{vir}} when the movement is toward **the speaker**: {{Vem cá!}} (Come here!). English says "I'm coming" in both situations. Portuguese chooses by the speaker's position.
:::

With transport, use {{de}} for vehicles and {{a pé}} for walking: {{de ônibus}}, {{de metrô}}, {{de carro}}, {{de táxi}}, {{de bicicleta}}, {{a pé}}. English says "by bus"; Portuguese says "of bus".

A bonus: {{ir}} plus an infinitive builds the near future, "I am going to…". {{Vou estudar}}, {{Vamos comer}}. You will study it in Lesson 9, but you can already use it as a block.

## 7.4 Fazer: to do, to make

| Person | Form | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| eu | {{faço}} | `FAH-soo` |
| você / ele / ela / a gente | {{faz}} | `fahss` |
| nós | {{fazemos}} | `fah-ZEH-mooss` |
| vocês / eles / elas | {{fazem}} | `FAH-zeh~ee` |

{{Fazer}} covers "do" and "make", and it has uses that English "do" does not.

| Use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| doing | {{O que você faz?}} | What do you do? |
| making | {{Faço o jantar.}} | I make dinner. |
| activity | {{Faço exercício de manhã.}} | I exercise in the morning. |
| shopping | {{Faço compras no sábado.}} | I go shopping on Saturday. |
| weather | {{Faz calor. / Faz frio. / Faz sol.}} | It's hot. / It's cold. / It's sunny. |
| time passed | {{Faz dois anos que moro aqui.}} | I've lived here for two years. |
| question | {{Faço uma pergunta?}} | May I ask a question? |

Two of these deserve attention. First, **weather** uses {{fazer}} in the third person: {{Faz frio hoje}}. You can also say {{Está frio}} to describe the weather right now (Lesson 5). Second, {{Faz + time + que + present}} is the natural way to say "for + period" in Brazilian speech: {{Faz três meses que estudo português}}. You can also say {{Estudo português há três meses}}.

## 7.5 Vir: to come

| Person | Form | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| eu | {{venho}} | `VEH-nyoo` |
| você / ele / ela / a gente | {{vem}} | `VEH~ee` |
| nós | {{vimos}} | `VEE-mooss` |
| vocês / eles / elas | {{vêm}} | `VEH~ee` |

Like {{tem}} and {{têm}}, the singular {{vem}} and the plural {{vêm}} are only different in writing. The infinitive is {{vir}} `veer`. A few examples: {{Venho de ônibus}}, {{Ele vem amanhã}}, {{Elas vêm de Recife}}, {{De onde você vem?}}, {{Vem comigo!}}. Notice that {{vimos}} also means "we saw" (from {{ver}}) in the past; context tells you which one.

## 7.6 More verbs with an irregular *eu* form

A large group of verbs behave like {{ter}}, {{fazer}} and {{vir}}: the {{eu}} form is irregular, and the rest follow the usual endings (with a small change in the stem).

| | dizer (to say) | trazer (to bring) | ler (to read) | sair (to leave, go out) | pôr (to put) | conhecer (to know a person or place) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| eu | {{digo}} `DJEE-goo` | {{trago}} `TRAH-goo` | {{leio}} `LAY-oo` | {{saio}} `SIE-oo` | {{ponho}} `POH-nyoo` | {{conheço}} `koh-NYEH-soo` |
| você / ele / ela | {{diz}} `djeess` | {{traz}} `trahss` | {{lê}} `leh` | {{sai}} `sie` | {{põe}} `POH~ee` | {{conhece}} `koh-NYEH-see` |
| nós | {{dizemos}} `djee-ZEH-mooss` | {{trazemos}} `trah-ZEH-mooss` | {{lemos}} `LEH-mooss` | {{saímos}} `sah-EE-mooss` | {{pomos}} `POH-mooss` | {{conhecemos}} `koh-nyeh-SEH-mooss` |
| vocês / eles / elas | {{dizem}} `DJEE-zeh~ee` | {{trazem}} `TRAH-zeh~ee` | {{leem}} `LEH-eh~ee` | {{saem}} `SAH-eh~ee` | {{põem}} `POH~-eh~ee` | {{conhecem}} `koh-NYEH-seh~ee` |

A few more {{eu}} forms to learn as you meet them: {{peço}} (from {{pedir}}, to ask for), {{ouço}} (from {{ouvir}}, to hear), {{durmo}} (from {{dormir}}, to sleep), {{sirvo}} (from {{servir}}, to serve), {{caibo}} (from {{caber}}, to fit). Three that you have seen: {{tenho}} (from {{ter}}), {{vejo}} (from {{ver}}), {{dou}} (from {{dar}}).

Look at the pattern in the {{eu}} forms: they end in **-go** ({{digo}}, {{trago}}), **-ço** ({{conheço}}, {{peço}}), **-ho** ({{venho}}, {{tenho}}, {{ponho}}), **-io** ({{leio}}, {{saio}}), or have a vowel change ({{sei}}, {{vou}}, {{dou}}, {{estou}}). When you meet a new verb, learn the {{eu}} form with the infinitive.

::: dica
A spelling reform note. The third-person plural of {{ler}}, {{ver}} and {{crer}} is {{leem}}, {{veem}}, {{creem}}, with no circumflex since 2009. The circumflex survives only in the pair {{tem / têm}} and {{vem / vêm}}, and in {{pôr}}, to separate it from the preposition {{por}}.
:::

## 7.7 The modal verbs: poder, querer, saber, dever, precisar

A **modal verb** is a verb that changes the meaning of another verb: *can, want, know how to, must, need*. In Portuguese the modal is conjugated, and the second verb stays in the infinitive.

| Person | poder (can) | querer (want) | saber (know) | dever (must, should) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| eu | {{posso}} `PAW-soo` | {{quero}} `KEH-roo` | {{sei}} `say` | {{devo}} `DEH-voo` |
| você / ele / ela / a gente | {{pode}} `PAW-djee` | {{quer}} `kehr` | {{sabe}} `SAH-bee` | {{deve}} `DEH-vee` |
| nós | {{podemos}} `poh-DEH-mooss` | {{queremos}} `keh-REH-mooss` | {{sabemos}} `sah-BEH-mooss` | {{devemos}} `deh-VEH-mooss` |
| vocês / eles / elas | {{podem}} `PAW-deh~ee` | {{querem}} `KEH-reh~ee` | {{sabem}} `SAH-beh~ee` | {{devem}} `DEH-veh~ee` |

### Poder: can, may

{{Poder}} `poh-DEHR` covers ability, possibility, permission and request.

| Use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ability | {{Posso trabalhar até tarde.}} | I can work late. |
| permission | {{Posso entrar?}} | May I come in? |
| request | {{Você pode repetir?}} | Can you repeat? |
| request | {{Pode falar mais devagar?}} | Can you speak more slowly? |
| prohibition | {{Aqui não pode fumar.}} | Smoking isn't allowed here. |
| possibility | {{Pode ser.}} | Maybe. / Could be. |
| agreement | {{Pode ser às duas?}} | Is two o'clock OK? |

{{Devagar}} `deh-vah-GAR` means slowly. A very useful structure for learners is {{Pode + infinitive?}}: {{Pode repetir?}}, {{Pode ajudar?}}, {{Pode falar mais devagar?}} It is polite and short. For asking about possibility, Brazilians also say {{Dá para…?}} or {{Dá pra…?}} (literally "does it give for?"): {{Dá pra pagar no cartão?}}, {{Dá para ir a pé?}} It means "Is it possible to…?" and is extremely common in speech.

### Querer: want

{{Querer}} `keh-REHR` says what you want. It can be followed by a noun or by an infinitive.

| Use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| noun | {{Quero um café.}} | I want a coffee. |
| infinitive | {{Quero conhecer o centro.}} | I want to see downtown. |
| offer | {{Quer um café?}} | Would you like a coffee? |
| invitation | {{Quer ir comigo?}} | Do you want to come with me? |
| negative | {{Não quero.}} | I don't want to. |

{{Quero}} is direct, and in many service situations a Brazilian prefers the softer {{queria}} (Lesson 13). For now, add {{por favor}} and a smile: {{Quero um café, por favor.}} As an offer, {{Quer…?}} is very warm and natural: {{Quer ajuda?}} (Want some help?), {{Quer água?}}

### Saber: know a fact, know how to

{{Saber}} `sah-BEHR` is for knowledge: facts, information, and skills. The {{eu}} form {{sei}} `say` is irregular and very frequent.

| Use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| fact | {{Sei o seu nome.}} | I know your name. |
| information | {{Não sei onde fica a estação.}} | I don't know where the station is. |
| skill (+ infinitive) | {{Você sabe nadar?}} | Can you swim? |
| skill | {{Ela sabe falar quatro línguas.}} | She can speak four languages. |
| reply | {{Sei, sim. / Não sei.}} | Yes, I do. / I don't know. |
| informal | {{Sei lá!}} | Who knows! / I have no idea! |

When {{saber}} is followed by an infinitive, it means "know how to": {{Sabe cozinhar?}} It is not the same as {{poder}}: {{Posso nadar}} means "I am allowed to / I am able to swim here and now", {{Sei nadar}} means "I have the skill of swimming".

### Dever, precisar and ter que: must, need, have to

| Portuguese | Meaning |
|---|---|
| {{Preciso ir.}} | I need to go. |
| {{Preciso de ajuda.}} | I need help. |
| {{Tenho que trabalhar.}} | I have to work. |
| {{Você deve descansar.}} | You should rest. |
| {{Ele deve chegar às oito.}} | He is supposed to arrive at eight. / He should arrive at eight. |
| {{Deve ser difícil.}} | It must be difficult. |

For obligation, Brazilians prefer {{precisar}} `preh-see-ZAR` and {{ter que}} (also {{ter de}} in writing). {{Dever}} `deh-VEHR` is used for advice, for expectations ("he is supposed to") and for guesses ("it must be"). Be careful with the preposition: {{preciso de + noun}} ({{Preciso de um táxi}}), but {{preciso + infinitive}} with no {{de}}: {{Preciso pegar um táxi}}. And {{ter que}} has a fixed {{que}}: {{Tenho que ir}}.

::: erros
| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| {{Quero ir em a praia.}} | {{Quero ir à praia.}} | {{a + a}} always fuse into {{à}} (or {{na}} in speech) |
| {{Eu posso falo.}} | {{Eu posso falar.}} | after a modal verb the next verb stays in the infinitive |
| {{Eu quero para ir.}} | {{Eu quero ir.}} | no {{para}} between {{querer}} and an infinitive |
| {{Preciso ir ao banheiro de.}} | {{Preciso ir ao banheiro.}} | {{de}} belongs only before a noun: {{preciso de um banheiro}} |
:::

## 7.8 Saber × conhecer

English uses *to know* for both. Portuguese separates them.

| | {{Saber}} | {{Conhecer}} |
|---|---|---|
| Idea | knowledge: facts, information, skills | familiarity: people, places, things |
| Object | a clause or an infinitive | a person, a place, a thing |
| Example | {{Sei onde ele mora.}} | {{Conheço o João.}} |
| Example | {{Sei falar português.}} | {{Conheço o Brasil.}} |
| Question | {{Você sabe a resposta?}} | {{Você conhece este restaurante?}} |
| Negative | {{Não sei.}} | {{Não conheço.}} |

::: passo 1 Ask yourself what the object is
Is it a **person, a place or a thing you have experienced**? Use {{conhecer}}: {{Conheço Salvador}}, {{Conheço a Ana}}. Is it **information, a fact, or a skill**? Use {{saber}}: {{Sei o nome dela}}, {{Sei dançar}}, {{Sei que ela mora aqui}}.
:::

::: passo 2 Check what "meeting" means
{{Conhecer}} also means "to meet for the first time", "to get acquainted": {{Prazer em conhecer você}} (Nice to meet you), {{Quero conhecer o Brasil}} (I want to visit Brazil). It is not just "to know"; it is "to come to know".
:::

::: passo 3 Do not use saber with people or places
{{Sei o João}} sounds like "I know a fact about João". {{Conheço o João}} is "I know João". The same for places: {{Conheço o Rio}} means you have been there.
:::

## 7.9 Asking, offering, inviting: the language of daily life

With {{poder}}, {{querer}} and {{ir}} you already have the three most useful tools for social life. Here are the formulas.

| You want to… | Say… | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ask for permission | {{Posso sentar aqui?}} | May I sit here? |
| ask for help | {{Pode me ajudar?}} | Can you help me? |
| ask for a repetition | {{Pode repetir, por favor?}} | Can you repeat, please? |
| ask for slowness | {{Pode falar mais devagar?}} | Could you speak more slowly? |
| offer | {{Quer ajuda?}} | Do you want help? |
| invite | {{Vamos tomar um café?}} | Shall we have a coffee? |
| suggest | {{Vamos?}} | Shall we go? |
| accept | {{Vamos, sim!}} | Yes, let's! |
| refuse politely | {{Hoje não posso.}} | I can't today. |
| ask if it is possible | {{Dá para pagar no cartão?}} | Is it possible to pay by card? |

A short note about {{me}} in {{Pode me ajudar?}}: it means "me", and it goes before the verb in Brazilian speech. Lesson 12 explains why. For now, learn {{Pode me ajudar?}} as a block.

For invitations, the phrase {{Vamos + infinitive}} is the simplest way to say "Let's…": {{Vamos comer}}, {{Vamos ver}}, {{Vamos tomar um café}}. It has no pronoun and it works with everyone.

## 7.10 One event, five attitudes

The verbs of this lesson let you talk about the same event from different angles. This is the most useful exercise to make them automatic. Take one event, {{viajar em julho}} (to travel in July), and look at the sentences.

| Attitude | Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| wish | {{Quero viajar em julho.}} | I want to travel in July. |
| plan | {{Vou viajar em julho.}} | I'm going to travel in July. |
| ability, permission | {{Posso viajar em julho.}} | I can (I'm free to) travel in July. |
| need | {{Preciso viajar em julho.}} | I need to travel in July. |
| obligation | {{Tenho que viajar em julho.}} | I have to travel in July. |
| expectation | {{Devo viajar em julho.}} | I should / I'm expected to travel in July. |

In each sentence the second verb is the same infinitive, {{viajar}}. Only the first verb changes. This is why one pattern, "conjugated verb + infinitive", serves for a huge part of Portuguese conversation. You can negate it by putting {{não}} before the first verb: {{Não posso viajar em julho}}, {{Não quero viajar em julho}}.

Now watch the pattern in a short real exchange between two friends planning a weekend. Read it aloud, and notice that the verb after the modal never changes.

> {{O que você quer fazer no fim de semana?}} What do you want to do on the weekend?
> {{Não sei ainda. Posso ir à praia, mas tenho que estudar no domingo.}} I don't know yet. I can go to the beach, but I have to study on Sunday.
> {{Podemos ir sábado. Eu faço um lanche e a gente leva.}} We can go on Saturday. I'll make a snack and we'll take it.
> {{Boa ideia! A que horas você quer sair?}} Good idea! What time do you want to leave?
> {{Vamos sair às nove.}} Let's leave at nine.

New words: {{ainda}} `ah-EE~-dah` (yet, still), {{a praia}} `ah PRIE-ah` (the beach), {{o lanche}} `oo LAH~-shee` (the snack), {{a ideia}} `ah ee-DAY-ah` (the idea), {{levar}} `leh-VAR` (to take, to carry along). Notice {{a gente leva}}: {{a gente}} with the {{ele}} form, as you learned in Lesson 2.

## 7.11 Read a real text

Read the text aloud, first with the guide, then without it.

> {{Eu sei falar um pouco de português e quero aprender mais. Todo dia faço exercícios e vejo vídeos. Hoje não posso sair, porque tenho que trabalhar. Mas amanhã vou conhecer o centro.}}

Guide: `EH-oo say fah-LAR oo~ POH-koo djee por-too-GEHSS ee KEH-roo ah-preh~-DEHR mies. TOH-doo DJEE-ah FAH-soo eh-zer-SEE-see-ooss ee VEH-zhoo VEE-djee-ooss. OH-zhee NOW~ PAW-soo sah-EER, poor-KEE TEH-nyoo kee trah-bah-LYAR. mahss ah-mah-NYAH~ VOH koh-nyeh-SEHR oo SEH~-troo.`

Translation: *I can speak a little Portuguese and I want to learn more. Every day I do exercises and watch videos. Today I can't go out, because I have to work. But tomorrow I'm going to see downtown.*

Check the verbs: {{sei}} (skill), {{quero}} (want, with an infinitive), {{faço}}, {{vejo}} (irregular {{eu}} forms), {{posso}} (can), {{tenho que}} (have to), {{vou conhecer}} (will see for the first time). Now say the same text about another person ({{ele}}): {{Ele sabe falar…, quer aprender…, faz…, vê…}}.

## 7.15 Pocket summary: Lesson 7

::: resumo
- **Ir:** {{vou, vai, vamos, vão}}. **Fazer:** {{faço, faz, fazemos, fazem}}; also weather ({{faz frio}}) and "for a time" ({{faz dois anos que}}). **Vir:** {{venho, vem, vimos, vêm}}.
- **Irregular *eu* forms:** {{digo, trago, leio, saio, ponho, conheço, peço, ouço, sei, vejo, dou}}.
- **Modals + infinitive:** {{poder}} ({{posso, pode, podemos, podem}}), {{querer}} ({{quero, quer}}), {{saber}} ({{sei, sabe}}), {{dever}}. No preposition before the infinitive.
- **Poder** for ability, permission, request: {{Posso entrar?}} {{Pode repetir?}} {{Aqui não pode fumar.}} {{Dá para…?}} for "is it possible?"
- **Obligation:** {{preciso (de)}}, {{tenho que}}; **advice and probability:** {{deve}}.
- **Saber × conhecer:** facts and skills versus people and places.
- **Ir × vir:** {{Já vou!}} is "I'm coming"; {{Vem cá}} is "come here".
- **Invitations:** {{Vamos + infinitive}}.
:::
