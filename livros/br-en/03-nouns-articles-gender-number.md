# Nouns, articles, gender and number: the shape of a noun phrase

By the end of this lesson you can name everyday objects with the right article, guess the gender of most new nouns from their endings, make plurals correctly (including the tricky ones in -ão and -l), and use the small fused words {{no}}, {{na}}, {{do}}, {{da}}, {{pelo}} and {{pela}} without hesitation.

## 3.1 A scene you will live

It is Saturday morning at a street market in São Paulo. Tom, a visitor from Leeds, stops at a fruit stall. The woman behind it is already weighing a melon for someone else.

::: dialogo
**Feirante:** {{Bom dia! O que vai ser hoje, moço?}}

**Tom:** {{Bom dia! Quero um abacaxi e uma melancia, por favor.}}

**Feirante:** {{O abacaxi é bem doce. A melancia também. E as mangas? As mangas são ótimas hoje.}}

**Tom:** {{Então quero duas mangas. E os limões? Os limões são grandes?}}

**Feirante:** {{São, sim. São do interior.}}

**Tom:** {{Perfeito. Quanto é tudo?}}

**Feirante:** {{Fica trinta reais.}}
:::

*Vendor: Good morning! What will it be today, young man? / Tom: Good morning! I'd like a pineapple and a watermelon, please. / Vendor: The pineapple is very sweet. So is the watermelon. And the mangoes? The mangoes are great today. / Tom: Then I'll take two mangoes. And the limes? Are the limes big? / Vendor: Yes, they are. They're from the countryside. / Tom: Perfect. How much is everything? / Vendor: It comes to thirty reais.*

Count the small words in this scene: {{o}}, {{a}}, {{as}}, {{os}}, {{um}}, {{uma}}, {{duas}}, {{do}}. Almost every noun has one in front of it, and the little word is never the same: it depends on whether the noun is masculine or feminine, singular or plural. {{Do interior}} is also a fusion: {{de}} + {{o}} in a single word. This lesson makes all of that automatic.

| Word | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{o abacaxi}} | `ah-bah-kah-SHEE` | the pineapple |
| {{a melancia}} | `meh-lah~-SEE-ah` | the watermelon |
| {{a manga}} | `MAH~-gah` | the mango |
| {{o limão}} | `lee-MOW~` | the lime, the lemon |
| {{doce}} | `DOH-see` | sweet |
| {{ótimo / ótima}} | `AW-tchee-moo` | great |
| {{o interior}} | `ee~-teh-ree-OR` | the countryside, inland |
| {{moço}} | `MOH-soo` | young man (a friendly way to address a man) |
| {{Fica trinta reais.}} | `FEE-kah TREE~-tah heh-IESS` | It comes to thirty reais. |

## 3.2 The trap: everything has a gender

In English, a table is *it*, a house is *it*, a hotel is *it*. In Portuguese, **every noun is masculine or feminine**, even objects and ideas: {{o livro}} (masculine), {{a mesa}} (feminine). The gender does not mean the thing is male or female. It is a label that the whole sentence must respect: the article, the adjective, and later the pronouns all take the same label.

Three English habits cause trouble:

1. **"The" is one word.** Portuguese has four: {{o}}, {{a}}, {{os}}, {{as}}.
2. **"A" is two words, plus plurals.** {{Um}}, {{uma}}, and the plurals {{uns}}, {{umas}} ("some").
3. **Small words fuse.** English keeps *in the* as two words. Portuguese says {{no}} (em + o) and {{na}} (em + a), and you must do the same.

The good news is that gender is mostly predictable from the **ending**, as you will see in 3.3. And the rule you will use every day is simple: **learn each noun together with its article.** Do not learn {{mesa}}; learn {{a mesa}}.

## 3.3 Gender: how to guess

::: passo 1 Start from the ending
Most nouns ending in **-o** are masculine, and most nouns ending in **-a** are feminine.

| Masculine | Guide | Feminine | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| {{o livro}} (book) | `oo LEE-vroo` | {{a casa}} (house) | `ah KAH-zah` |
| {{o carro}} (car) | `oo KAH-hoo` | {{a mesa}} (table) | `ah MEH-zah` |
| {{o preço}} (price) | `oo PREH-soo` | {{a rua}} (street) | `ah HOO-ah` |
| {{o bairro}} (neighborhood) | `oo BIE-hoo` | {{a loja}} (shop) | `ah LAW-zhah` |
| {{o mercado}} (market) | `oo mer-KAH-doo` | {{a feira}} (street market) | `ah FAY-rah` |
:::

::: passo 2 Learn the endings that signal gender
Some endings are strong signals. Learn these and you can guess hundreds of nouns.

| Ending | Gender | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **-ção**, **-são** | feminine | {{a lição}} `ah lee-SOW~` (lesson), {{a nação}}, {{a televisão}}, {{a canção}} (song) |
| **-dade**, **-tade** | feminine | {{a cidade}} `ah see-DAH-djee` (city), {{a verdade}} (truth), {{a liberdade}} |
| **-agem** | feminine | {{a viagem}} `ah vee-AH-zheh~ee` (trip), {{a mensagem}} (message), {{a garagem}} |
| **-ice**, **-ez** | feminine | {{a velhice}} (old age), {{a timidez}} (shyness) |
| **-ema**, **-oma** (Greek origin) | masculine | {{o problema}} `oo proh-BLEH-mah`, {{o sistema}} `oo sees-TEH-mah`, {{o idioma}} `oo ee-djee-OH-mah` (language), {{o tema}} (theme), {{o diploma}} |
| **-or**, **-ismo** | masculine | {{o amor}} `oo ah-MOR` (love), {{o computador}}, {{o calor}} (heat), {{o turismo}} |
| **-l** | mostly masculine | {{o hotel}}, {{o papel}} `oo pah-PEH-oo` (paper), {{o jornal}} `oo zhor-NAH-oo` (newspaper), {{o sal}} (salt) |
:::

::: atencao
Every rule has well-known exceptions. The ones you need most are these. **Masculine words ending in -a:** {{o dia}} (day), {{o mapa}} (map), {{o planeta}} (planet), {{o cinema}}, {{o clima}} (climate), {{o programa}}. **Feminine words ending in -o:** {{a mão}} (hand), {{a foto}} (photo, short for *fotografia*), {{a moto}} (motorbike). **Masculine words in -agem:** {{o personagem}} (character in a story). **Feminine words in -or:** {{a flor}} (flower), {{a cor}} (color), {{a dor}} (pain). **Feminine words in -ão** other than -ção and -são: {{a razão}} (reason), {{a união}} (union), {{a opinião}}. Words in -ão can be masculine too: {{o pão}}, {{o avião}} (plane), {{o limão}}, {{o coração}}, {{o irmão}}.
:::

::: passo 3 Look at people and animals
When a noun names a person or an animal, the gender usually follows the sex of the person. A man is {{o amigo}} or {{o professor}}; a woman is {{a amiga}} or {{a professora}}. The same endings you saw in Lesson 2 appear again: **-o** becomes **-a**, **-or** becomes **-ora**.

| Masculine | Feminine | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{o amigo}} | {{a amiga}} | friend |
| {{o gato}} | {{a gata}} | cat |
| {{o irmão}} | {{a irmã}} | brother / sister |
| {{o pai}} | {{a mãe}} | father / mother |
| {{o homem}} | {{a mulher}} | man / woman |
| {{o garçom}} | {{a garçonete}} | waiter / waitress |

Some nouns have **one form for both** and only the article changes: {{o/a estudante}}, {{o/a dentista}}, {{o/a colega}} `koh-LEH-gah`, {{o/a turista}}, {{o/a cliente}}, {{o/a jornalista}}. The ending -a does not make {{o colega}} or {{o turista}} feminine.
:::

::: passo 4 Learn the rest one by one
Nouns ending in **-e** have no reliable rule. Compare:

| Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|
| {{o leite}} `oo LAY-tchee` (milk) | {{a noite}} `ah NOY-tchee` (night) |
| {{o nome}} `oo NOH-mee` (name) | {{a ponte}} `ah POH~-tchee` (bridge) |
| {{o dente}} `oo DEH~-tchee` (tooth) | {{a carne}} `ah KAR-nee` (meat) |
| {{o chocolate}} | {{a chave}} `ah SHAH-vee` (key) |
| {{o café}} `oo kah-FEH` (coffee) | {{a classe}} `ah KLAH-see` (class) |

This is why the rule from 3.2 matters: when you meet a new noun in -e, learn it with its article from the start.
:::

Two more things. A few words change meaning with gender: {{o capital}} is money invested, {{a capital}} is the capital city. And **loanwords** take a gender too, usually following the Portuguese word they replace: {{o e-mail}}, {{o site}}, {{o shopping}}, but {{a internet}}, {{a pizza}}.

::: dica
Many Brazilians think about gender less than you do. When they learn English they make the opposite mistake, *the table, he is*. Do not expect to get every gender right in the first months. A wrong article rarely blocks understanding. Choose the ending rules first, then add exceptions only as you meet them.
:::

## 3.4 The definite article: o, a, os, as

The **definite article** is the English *the*. It has four forms, and it matches the noun in gender and number.

| | Masculine | Feminine | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| singular | {{o}} | {{a}} | `oo` / `ah` |
| plural | {{os}} | {{as}} | `ooss` / `ahss` |

Compare {{o carro}}, {{a casa}}, {{os carros}}, {{as casas}}. In speech the article is pronounced very lightly, almost glued to the noun: {{o carro}} sounds like *ookahhoo*.

### When Portuguese uses the article and English does not

English drops "the" when we talk about things in general. Portuguese often keeps it. The main cases:

- **Subject of a general statement:** {{O café é bom para a saúde.}} (Coffee is good for health.) {{A vida é curta.}} (Life is short.) {{Os brasileiros adoram futebol.}}
- **Countries and regions:** {{o Brasil}}, {{a Argentina}}, {{os Estados Unidos}}, {{o Canadá}}. A few countries and nearly all cities take none: {{Portugal}}, {{Cuba}}, {{Israel}}, {{São Paulo}}, {{Londres}}. Some cities have an article as part of the name: {{o Rio de Janeiro}}, so Brazilians say {{no Rio}}.
- **Languages as subject:** {{O português é bonito.}}
- **Days of the week with a habit:** {{Na segunda eu trabalho em casa.}} (On Mondays I work from home.)
- **First names, in speech:** {{A Marina é de Recife.}} {{O João chega amanhã.}} This is normal in São Paulo, Rio, Minas and much of Brazil; in some regions, like parts of the Northeast and the North, it is less common. Both are correct. But never use the article when you **address** the person: {{Oi, Marina!}}, not {{Oi, a Marina!}}
- **Parts of the body and personal items:** English says *my hands*; Portuguese says {{as mãos}} when the owner is clear: {{Lavo as mãos.}} (I wash my hands.)

### When Portuguese leaves the article out

- **After a verb of speaking and studying, with a language:** {{Falo português}}, {{Estudo inglês}}.
- **After {{ser}}, with a profession or nationality:** {{Sou professora}}, {{Somos brasileiros}} (Lesson 2).
- **After {{de}} in a general sense:** compare {{Gosto de café}} (I like coffee, in general) and {{Gosto do café daqui}} (I like the coffee here, a specific one). The first has {{de}} alone, the second {{de + o = do}}.
- **With {{com}} and {{sem}} for states and amounts:** {{com fome}}, {{sem açúcar}}.

::: erros
| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| {{Brasil é grande.}} | {{O Brasil é grande.}} | most country names take the article |
| {{Sou o professor.}} (meaning only "I am a teacher") | {{Sou professor.}} | no article with a profession |
| {{Oi, a Marina!}} | {{Oi, Marina!}} | no article when you call someone directly |
| {{Falo o português.}} | {{Falo português.}} | no article after *falar* + language |
:::

## 3.5 The indefinite article: um, uma, uns, umas

The **indefinite article** is *a/an* and, in the plural, *some*.

| | Masculine | Feminine | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| singular | {{um}} | {{uma}} | `oo~` / `OO-mah` |
| plural | {{uns}} | {{umas}} | `oo~ss` / `OO-mahss` |

{{Um}} also means "one": {{um café}} (a coffee, one coffee), {{uma cerveja}} (a beer). {{Uns}} and {{umas}} mean *some* or *a few*: {{uns amigos}} (some friends). Before a number, {{uns}} and {{umas}} mean "about": {{uns vinte reais}} (about twenty reais).

Where English uses *a*, Portuguese often uses nothing:

- **Professions and nationalities:** {{Sou médica}}.
- **After {{ter}}, when you only say that you own one:** {{Tenho carro}} (I have a car), but {{Tenho um carro novo}} when you describe it.
- **After {{que}} in exclamations:** {{Que dia lindo!}} (What a beautiful day!), {{Que legal!}}
- **Before {{cem}} and {{mil}}:** {{cem reais}}, {{mil pessoas}}, not "um cem".

## 3.6 Plurals: one rule for vowels, four for consonants

The plural is easy in the large majority of cases, and the article and the adjective change with it.

::: passo 1 Words ending in a vowel: add -s
{{O livro}} → {{os livros}}; {{a casa}} → {{as casas}}; {{o carro}} → {{os carros}}; {{a noite}} → {{as noites}}; {{o café}} → {{os cafés}} `oos kah-FEHSS`; {{o sofá}} → {{os sofás}}.
:::

::: passo 2 Words ending in -r, -z, -s (stressed): add -es
| Singular | Plural | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| {{a mulher}} | {{as mulheres}} | `moo-LYEH-reess` |
| {{o lugar}} | {{os lugares}} | `loo-GAH-reess` (places) |
| {{a flor}} | {{as flores}} | `FLOH-reess` |
| {{a luz}} | {{as luzes}} | `LOO-zeess` (lights) |
| {{o país}} | {{os países}} | `pah-EE-zeess` (countries) |
| {{o mês}} | {{os meses}} | `MEH-zeess` (months) |
| {{o português}} | {{os portugueses}} | `por-too-GEH-zeess` |

Unstressed -s does not change: {{o ônibus}} `oo OH-nee-booss` → {{os ônibus}}; {{o lápis}} `oo LAH-peess` (pencil) → {{os lápis}}. Only the article shows the plural.
:::

::: passo 3 Words ending in -m: change to -ns
{{O homem}} → {{os homens}}; {{o jardim}} `zhar-DJEE~` → {{os jardins}} `zhar-DJEE~ss`; {{bom}} → {{bons}} `BOH~ss`; {{um}} → {{uns}}. Notice that the sound stays nasal.
:::

::: passo 4 Words ending in -l: drop the l and add -is
| Singular | Plural | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| {{o jornal}} | {{os jornais}} | `zhor-NIESS` |
| {{o animal}} | {{os animais}} | `ah-nee-MIESS` |
| {{o hotel}} | {{os hotéis}} | `oh-TEH-eess` |
| {{o papel}} | {{os papéis}} | `pah-PEH-eess` |
| {{o azul}} | {{os azuis}} | `ah-ZOO-eess` |

Notice the accents on {{hotéis}} and {{papéis}}: the plural of **-el** is **-éis**. And words in -il change differently depending on stress: stressed {{-il}} gives {{-is}} ({{o funil}} → {{os funis}}), unstressed {{-il}} gives {{-eis}} ({{fácil}} → {{fáceis}}).
:::

::: passo 5 Words ending in -ão: learn the three plurals
This is the one place where you must memorize. Almost all of these words take **-ões**; a small, very frequent group takes **-ães**, and another small group takes **-ãos**.

| Plural ending | Singular | Plural | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| **-ões** (most words) | {{a lição}} | {{as lições}} | `lee-SOH~eess` |
| | {{o avião}} | {{os aviões}} | `ah-vee-OH~eess` |
| | {{a nação}} | {{as nações}} | `nah-SOH~eess` |
| | {{o limão}} | {{os limões}} | `lee-MOH~eess` |
| | {{o coração}} | {{os corações}} | `koh-rah-SOH~eess` |
| **-ães** (a few) | {{o pão}} | {{os pães}} | `PAH~eess` |
| | {{o cão}} (dog) | {{os cães}} | `KAH~eess` |
| | {{o capitão}} | {{os capitães}} | `kah-pee-TAH~eess` |
| | {{o alemão}} | {{os alemães}} | `ah-leh-MAH~eess` |
| **-ãos** (a few) | {{a mão}} | {{as mãos}} | `MOW~ss` |
| | {{o irmão}} | {{os irmãos}} | `eer-MOW~ss` |
| | {{o cidadão}} | {{os cidadãos}} | `see-dah-DOW~ss` (citizens) |
:::

Use the table as a reference, not as something to memorize in one sitting. The default is **-ões**; when a word is not on the -ães or -ãos list, use -ões.

### A plural that changes the vowel sound

A few very common nouns have a closed **o** in the singular and an open **o** in the plural. The spelling does not change; only the sound does. Your ear will notice the difference, your eye will not.

| Singular | Plural | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{o ovo}} `OH-voo` | {{os ovos}} `AW-vooss` | egg |
| {{o olho}} `OH-lyoo` | {{os olhos}} `AW-lyooss` | eye |
| {{o jogo}} `ZHOH-goo` | {{os jogos}} `ZHAW-gooss` | game |
| {{o osso}} `OH-soo` | {{os ossos}} `AW-sooss` | bone |
| {{o fogo}} `FOH-goo` | {{os fogos}} `FAW-gooss` | fire |
| {{o almoço}} `ow-MOH-soo` | {{os almoços}} `ow-MAW-sooss` | lunch |

Most nouns keep the same vowel in the plural, so learn these from the audio rather than from a rule.

### Agreement in the whole noun phrase

Article, noun and adjective all agree: the noun sets the gender and number, and the others follow.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| {{o carro novo}} | {{os carros novos}} |
| {{a casa nova}} | {{as casas novas}} |
| {{o hotel barato}} | {{os hotéis baratos}} |
| {{a lição fácil}} | {{as lições fáceis}} |
| {{o pão francês}} | {{os pães franceses}} |
| {{um livro interessante}} | {{uns livros interessantes}} |

Colors follow the same rule. {{Branco}} `BRAH~-koo`, {{preto}} `PREH-too`, {{vermelho}} `ver-MEH-lyoo`, {{amarelo}} `ah-mah-REH-loo` have four forms: {{branco, branca, brancos, brancas}}. {{Azul}} and {{verde}} `VER-djee` have two: {{azul / azuis}}, {{verde / verdes}}. So: {{a casa branca}}, {{os carros azuis}}.

### The diminutive: small, kind and softer

Brazilians add **-inho** (masculine) or **-inha** (feminine) to nouns, adjectives and even adverbs all day long. The ending does not only mean *small*. It also means affection, politeness, softening and "just".

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Ends in an unstressed **-o**, **-a** or **-e**: drop it and add -inho / -inha | {{livro}} → {{livrinho}} `lee-VREE-nyoo`; {{casa}} → {{casinha}} `kah-ZEE-nyah`; {{leite}} → {{leitinho}} |
| Spelling keeps the sound: **c** → **qu**, **g** → **gu** | {{pouco}} → {{pouquinho}} `poh-KEE-nyoo`; {{amigo}} → {{amiguinho}} `ah-mee-GEE-nyoo` |
| Ends in a stressed vowel, a nasal or a consonant: add -zinho / -zinha | {{café}} → {{cafezinho}} `kah-feh-ZEE-nyoo`; {{pão}} → {{pãozinho}} `pow~-ZEE-nyoo`; {{flor}} → {{florzinha}} `flor-ZEE-nyah` |
| Plural of a diminutive in -zinho | {{pães}} → {{pãezinhos}}; {{cafés}} → {{cafezinhos}} |

What it does in a sentence:

| Meaning | Example |
|---|---|
| small | {{uma casinha no campo}} (a little house in the country) |
| affection | {{Que bonitinho!}} (How cute!); {{Aninha}}, {{Paulinho}} (names with warmth) |
| softening a request | {{Espera um minutinho.}} (Wait a little minute.) {{Me vê um cafezinho?}} |
| "just", "only" | {{Só um pouquinho.}} (Just a little.) |
| intensity | {{agorinha}} (right now), {{cedinho}} (very early), {{devagarinho}} (very slowly), {{tudinho}} (every bit) |

The opposite ending, **-ão**, makes things bigger or more impressive, and nicknames too: {{carro}} → {{carrão}} (a great car), {{festa}} → {{festão}} (a huge party), {{Paulo}} → {{Paulão}}.

::: atencao
**Some words only look like diminutives.** {{Vizinho}} (neighbour), {{caminho}} (path), {{sobrinho}} (nephew), {{cozinha}} (kitchen) and {{carinho}} (affection) are ordinary words. And {{um cafezinho}} is the standard name for the small black coffee Brazilians offer everywhere. Do not add -inho to every word: use it to be kind, and let the audio show you which words take it.
:::

## 3.7 Contractions: em, de, por and the article

Portuguese has a small set of prepositions that are used constantly: **em** (in, on, at), **de** (of, from), **por** (by, through, for). In front of an article, each one **fuses** with it into a single word. This is not optional. Saying {{de o}} or {{em a}} is always wrong.

| Preposition | + o | + a | + os | + as | + um | + uma |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **em** (in, on, at) | {{no}} | {{na}} | {{nos}} | {{nas}} | {{num}} | {{numa}} |
| **de** (of, from) | {{do}} | {{da}} | {{dos}} | {{das}} | {{dum}} | {{duma}} |
| **por** (through, by, for) | {{pelo}} | {{pela}} | {{pelos}} | {{pelas}} | | |
| **a** (to) | {{ao}} | {{à}} | {{aos}} | {{às}} | | |

Pronunciation: {{no}} `noo`, {{na}} `nah`, {{do}} `doo`, {{da}} `dah`, {{pelo}} `PEH-loo`, {{pela}} `PEH-lah`. {{Num}} and {{numa}} (`noo~`, `NOO-mah`) are very common in speech; {{dum}} and {{duma}} are rare, and you can skip them.

::: passo 1 Use "em" for place and time, with no movement
{{Moro no Brasil}}, {{Trabalho num banco}}, {{Moro na rua das Flores}}, {{Estou no hotel}}, {{na segunda}}, {{no sábado}}. With a city that has no article, em stays alone: {{Moro em Curitiba}}, {{Trabalho em Lisboa}}.
:::

::: passo 2 Use "de" for origin, owner and material
Origin: {{Sou do Brasil}}, {{Sou da Bahia}}, {{Sou de Recife}}. Owner: Portuguese has no apostrophe-s, so it says "the book **of** the Pedro": {{o livro do Pedro}}, {{a casa da Marta}}, {{o carro dos meus pais}}. Material: {{uma mesa de madeira}} (a wooden table; {{madeira}} is `mah-DAY-rah`).
:::

::: passo 3 Use "por" for passing through and for reasons
{{Passo pelo parque todo dia}} (I go through the park every day). {{Obrigado pela ajuda}} (Thank you for the help). {{Pelo menos}} (at least).
:::

A word about the fourth line of the table, {{a}}: it means *to*, and it fuses with the article too: {{ao}} and {{à}} (with the grave accent, as Lesson 1 promised). You will use these with {{ir}} in Lessons 5 and 7. For now, notice that {{à}} is the only place where the grave accent appears in everyday writing.

Also remember that {{de}} and {{em}} fuse with the pronouns that come later: {{dele}} (of him), {{nele}} (in him), {{desse}}, {{nesse}}. Lesson 8 shows them.

::: dica
**Do not translate "in the" word by word.** Think of {{no}} and {{na}} as single words that already include the. When you say {{no Brasil}}, you do not hear "em + o". Practice the whole phrase until it sounds like one word: {{no Brasil}}, {{na casa}}, {{do hotel}}, {{da Maria}}.
:::

## 3.8 Say it all together

You can now build complete phrases. Read each aloud.

| Portuguese | Meaning |
|---|---|
| {{Moro numa casa pequena no centro.}} | I live in a small house downtown. |
| {{O hotel fica na rua das Flores.}} | The hotel is on Flowers Street. |
| {{Preciso do passaporte e da passagem.}} | I need the passport and the ticket. |
| {{Os pães da padaria são ótimos.}} | The bread from the bakery is great. |
| {{Tem uns amigos aqui.}} | There are some friends here. |
| {{As crianças gostam do parque.}} | The children like the park. |

Four new things hide in these sentences, all of which you will meet again: {{moro}} and {{preciso}} are present-tense verbs (Lesson 4), {{fica}} and {{tem}} (Lesson 5) say where things are and what exists. Treat them as blocks for now.

## 3.12 Pocket summary: Lesson 3

::: resumo
- **Every noun has a gender.** Learn each noun with its article: {{a mesa}}, {{o livro}}.
- **Guess from the ending:** -o masculine, -a feminine; -ção, -são, -dade, -agem feminine; -ema, -oma, -or, -l mostly masculine. Exceptions to remember: {{o dia}}, {{o mapa}}, {{a mão}}, {{a flor}}.
- **Definite article:** {{o, a, os, as}}. **Indefinite:** {{um, uma, uns, umas}}.
- **No article** with professions and nationalities, with *falar* + language, and after *que* in exclamations.
- **Plural:** vowel + -s; -r, -z, stressed -s + -es; -m → -ns; -l → -is; -ão → -ões (default), -ães ({{pães}}, {{cães}}), -ãos ({{mãos}}, {{irmãos}}).
- **Agreement:** article, noun and adjective share gender and number: {{as casas brancas}}.
- **Contractions:** em → {{no, na, nos, nas, num, numa}}; de → {{do, da, dos, das}}; por → {{pelo, pela, pelos, pelas}}; a → {{ao, à, aos, às}}.
:::
