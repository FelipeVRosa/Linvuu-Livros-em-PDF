# The simple past: what happened

By the end of this lesson you can tell what you did, where you went and what happened yesterday, last week or last year, with the regular endings and the most frequent irregular verbs ({{fui}}, {{tive}}, {{fiz}}, {{disse}}, {{vi}}, {{dei}}, {{vim}}).

## 10.1 A scene you will live

It is Monday morning in the office in Belo Horizonte. Paulo, a colleague, sees you at the coffee machine.

> **Paulo:** {{Oi, Emma! Como foi o fim de semana?}}
> **Emma:** {{Foi ótimo! No sábado eu fui a Ouro Preto com a Clara.}}
> **Paulo:** {{Vocês foram de ônibus?}}
> **Emma:** {{Fomos. Saímos às sete e chegamos às dez. Visitamos duas igrejas e almoçamos comida mineira.}}
> **Paulo:** {{E no domingo?}}
> **Emma:** {{Fiquei em casa. Dormi bastante, li um livro e não fiz mais nada.}}

Translation: *Hi, Emma! How was the weekend? / It was great! On Saturday I went to Ouro Preto with Clara. / Did you go by bus? / Yes, we did. We left at seven and arrived at ten. We visited two churches and had lunch, Minas style. / And on Sunday? / I stayed at home. I slept a lot, read a book and did nothing else.*

Look at the verbs: {{foi}}, {{fui}}, {{foram}}, {{fomos}}, {{saímos}}, {{chegamos}}, {{visitamos}}, {{almoçamos}}, {{fiquei}}, {{dormi}}, {{li}}, {{fiz}}. Each one tells you something that **happened and is finished**. This tense is called the **pretérito perfeito**; in this book we say *the simple past*.

New words from the scene:

| Portuguese | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{o fim de semana}} | `oo FEE~ djee seh-MAH-nah` | the weekend |
| {{ótimo}} | `AW-tchee-moo` | great, excellent |
| {{a igreja}} | `ah ee-GREH-zhah` | the church |
| {{comida mineira}} | `koh-MEE-dah mee-NAY-rah` | food from Minas Gerais |
| {{visitar}} | `vee-zee-TAR` | to visit |
| {{ficar}} | `fee-KAR` | to stay, to remain; also to become |
| {{ontem}} | `OH~-teh~ee` | yesterday |

## 10.2 The trap: English has a past with one form, Portuguese has one for each person

English is kind with the simple past. *I ate, you ate, she ate, we ate, they ate.* One form for everybody, and a helper (*did*) for questions and negatives: *Did you eat? I did not eat.*

Portuguese does the opposite. The past tense has a **different ending for each person**, there is **no helper**, and the stress is on the ending. Three consequences follow.

| English | Portuguese | What is different |
|---|---|---|
| *I ate* | {{comi}} | the ending shows the person ({{-i}} = eu) |
| *you ate* | {{comeu}} | another ending ({{-eu}} = você, ele, ela) |
| *Did you eat?* | {{Você comeu?}} | no helper: just the verb and a rising voice |
| *I did not eat.* | {{Não comi.}} | no helper: {{não}} + verb |
| *I have eaten / I've already eaten.* | {{Já comi.}} | the same tense covers the English present perfect |

The last row is the big relief. In English, *I ate* and *I have eaten* are two tenses with different meanings. In Brazilian Portuguese, in the situations where you would say *I have already eaten*, *I have been to Rio*, *I have never seen that film*, you use **this** simple past ({{Já comi}}, {{Já fui ao Rio}}, {{Nunca vi esse filme}}). Section 10.8 explains what the other form ({{tenho comido}}) is.

## 10.3 The regular endings

Take the infinitive, remove {{-ar}}, {{-er}} or {{-ir}}, and add the endings. Say the forms aloud. The stress is on the ending, and that is what makes it different from the present tense.

| Person | {{-ar}}: {{falar}} | {{-er}}: {{comer}} | {{-ir}}: {{abrir}} |
|---|---|---|---|
| eu | {{falei}} `fah-LAY` | {{comi}} `koh-MEE` | {{abri}} `ah-BREE` |
| você, ele, ela, a gente | {{falou}} `fah-LOH` | {{comeu}} `koh-MEH-oo` | {{abriu}} `ah-BREE-oo` |
| nós | {{falamos}} `fah-LAH-mooss` | {{comemos}} `koh-MEH-mooss` | {{abrimos}} `ah-BREE-mooss` |
| vocês, eles, elas | {{falaram}} `fah-LAH-rah~oo` | {{comeram}} `koh-MEH-rah~oo` | {{abriram}} `ah-BREE-rah~oo` |

Spend a minute on this table. The {{-er}} and {{-ir}} endings are **identical** ({{-i, -eu / -iu, -emos / -imos, -eram / -iram}}) except for two letters. The {{-ar}} group has its own endings.

::: passo 1 Learn the {{eu}} and {{você}} endings first
They are the ones you will use most: {{-ei}} / {{-ou}} for {{-ar}}, {{-i}} / {{-eu}} (or {{-iu}}) for {{-er}} and {{-ir}}. Say them in pairs: {{falei, falou}}; {{comi, comeu}}; {{abri, abriu}}.
:::

::: passo 2 Use {{a gente}} with the third-person singular
{{A gente falou}}, {{a gente comeu}}, {{a gente abriu}}. It is the form you will hear in conversation for "we".
:::

::: passo 3 Do not confuse past and present in the third-person plural
The plural past has an extra syllable, and the stress moves.

| Present | Past |
|---|---|
| {{falam}} `FAH-lah~oo` | {{falaram}} `fah-LAH-rah~oo` |
| {{comem}} `KOH-meh~ee` | {{comeram}} `koh-MEH-rah~oo` |
| {{abrem}} `AH-breh~ee` | {{abriram}} `ah-BREE-rah~oo` |

For the singular, listen to where the stress is: {{ele fala}} `FAH-lah` (present) and {{ele falou}} `fah-LOH` (past).
:::

::: atencao
**For {{nós}}, the regular past looks exactly like the present:** {{falamos}}, {{comemos}} and {{abrimos}} can be present or past. Brazilians use the context to tell: {{Nós falamos todo dia}} (every day, so present) and {{Nós falamos ontem}} (yesterday, so past). In Portugal, the past of {{-ar}} verbs is written {{falámos}}, with an accent, but in Brazil it is not.
:::

### Spelling changes in the {{eu}} form

Three types of verbs change their spelling in the {{eu}} form only, so that the sound stays the same.

| Verb ends in | eu form | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| {{-car}} | {{-quei}} | {{ficar}} → {{fiquei}} `fee-KAY`, {{tocar}} → {{toquei}} | keeps the hard *k* sound before *e* |
| {{-gar}} | {{-guei}} | {{chegar}} → {{cheguei}} `sheh-GAY`, {{pagar}} → {{paguei}} | keeps the hard *g* sound before *e* |
| {{-çar}} | {{-cei}} | {{começar}} → {{comecei}} `koh-meh-SAY`, {{almoçar}} → {{almocei}} | no cedilla before *e* |

The other persons do not change: {{ele ficou}}, {{ele chegou}}, {{ele começou}}. (You learned in Lesson 1 why {{c}} before {{e}} is *s* and why {{gu}} is needed: this is the same logic.)

## 10.4 The irregular verbs you cannot avoid

Some of the most frequent verbs in the language are irregular in the past. You already know their infinitives; now learn the past forms. Do not learn them one by one: look at the patterns.

### {{Ser}} and {{ir}} have the same past

| Person | Form | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| eu | {{fui}} | `FOO-ee` |
| você, ele, ela, a gente | {{foi}} | `foy` |
| nós | {{fomos}} | `FOH-mooss` |
| vocês, eles, elas | {{foram}} | `FOH-rah~oo` |

{{Fui}} means both *I was* and *I went*. This sounds impossible, but the sentence tells you which one it is:

- {{Eu fui ao mercado.}} (I went to the market.) A place follows: {{ir}}.
- {{Eu fui professor por dez anos.}} (I was a teacher for ten years.) A noun follows: {{ser}}.
- {{A viagem foi ótima.}} (The trip was great.) An adjective follows: {{ser}}.
- {{Ela foi ao médico.}} (She went to the doctor.) A preposition + place follows: {{ir}}.

::: dica
**To tell {{ser}} from {{ir}}:** if the next word is a place with {{a}}, {{para}}, {{em}}, or its contraction ({{ao}}, {{à}}, {{pro}}, {{pra}}), the verb is {{ir}}. If the next word is a noun, an adjective, or a full sentence ({{foi um prazer}}), it is {{ser}}. In practice, nobody hesitates in conversation.
:::

### A group with a new stem: ter, estar, fazer, dizer, poder, querer, saber, pôr, trazer

These verbs change the stem of the past tense. Their endings follow one regular pattern: {{-e}} (or nothing), {{-e}} (or nothing), {{-emos}}, {{-eram}}, with a circumflex only in {{pôde}} and {{pôs}}. The stem is the same for the whole tense.

| Infinitive | eu | você, ele, ela | nós | eles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| {{ter}} | {{tive}} `TCHEE-vee` | {{teve}} `TEH-vee` | {{tivemos}} `tchee-VEH-mooss` | {{tiveram}} `tchee-VEH-rah~oo` |
| {{estar}} | {{estive}} `ehs-TCHEE-vee` | {{esteve}} `ehs-TEH-vee` | {{estivemos}} | {{estiveram}} |
| {{fazer}} | {{fiz}} `feess` | {{fez}} `fehss` | {{fizemos}} `fee-ZEH-mooss` | {{fizeram}} `fee-ZEH-rah~oo` |
| {{dizer}} | {{disse}} `DJEE-see` | {{disse}} | {{dissemos}} `djee-SEH-mooss` | {{disseram}} `djee-SEH-rah~oo` |
| {{poder}} | {{pude}} `POO-djee` | {{pôde}} `POH-djee` | {{pudemos}} | {{puderam}} |
| {{querer}} | {{quis}} `keess` | {{quis}} | {{quisemos}} | {{quiseram}} |
| {{saber}} | {{soube}} `SOH-bee` | {{soube}} | {{soubemos}} | {{souberam}} |
| {{pôr}} | {{pus}} `pooss` | {{pôs}} `pohss` | {{pusemos}} | {{puseram}} |
| {{trazer}} | {{trouxe}} `TROH-see` | {{trouxe}} | {{trouxemos}} | {{trouxeram}} |

Notice the circumflex on {{pôde}} (past) versus {{pode}} (present), and on {{pôs}} (past) versus {{põe}} (present of {{pôr}}). That accent is the only thing that separates them in writing.

### A group that is nearly regular but has a different stem: ver, vir, dar, ler

| Infinitive | eu | você, ele, ela | nós | eles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| {{ver}} | {{vi}} `vee` | {{viu}} `VEE-oo` | {{vimos}} `VEE-mooss` | {{viram}} `VEE-rah~oo` |
| {{vir}} | {{vim}} `VEE~` | {{veio}} `VAY-oo` | {{viemos}} `vee-EH-mooss` | {{vieram}} `vee-EH-rah~oo` |
| {{dar}} | {{dei}} `day` | {{deu}} `DEH-oo` | {{demos}} `DEH-mooss` | {{deram}} `DEH-rah~oo` |
| {{ler}} | {{li}} `lee` | {{leu}} `LEH-oo` | {{lemos}} `LEH-mooss` | {{leram}} `LEH-rah~oo` |

Notice that {{vir}} (past: *came*) and {{ver}} (past: *saw*) are close. {{Vi}} is *I saw*; {{vim}} is *I came*. Say them aloud until your mouth knows the difference: {{vi}} `vee` is short and oral; {{vim}} `VEE~` is nasal. {{Veio}} is *he came*, {{viu}} is *he saw*.

::: atencao
**Do not apply the regular pattern to these verbs.** The two classic errors from English speakers are ~~fazi~~ (for {{fiz}}) and ~~disseu~~ (for {{disse}}). If it helps, learn the {{eu}} and {{você}} forms of each verb first: {{tive/teve}}, {{fiz/fez}}, {{disse/disse}}, {{vi/viu}}, {{dei/deu}}, {{vim/veio}}, {{pude/pôde}}. The rest follows by pattern.
:::

### Everything else is regular

Verbs like {{sair}}, {{ouvir}}, {{dormir}}, {{pedir}} and {{chegar}} use the regular endings. {{Sair}} just needs the accent: {{saí}} `sah-EE`, {{saiu}} `sah-EE-oo`, {{saímos}} `sah-EE-mooss`, {{saíram}} `sah-EE-rah~oo`. {{Dormir}}: {{dormi}}, {{dormiu}}, {{dormimos}}, {{dormiram}}.

## 10.5 When to use the simple past

The simple past tells what **happened**: an event that began and ended, or a series of events in order.

- {{Ontem eu acordei às seis.}} (Yesterday I woke up at six.)
- {{Ela chegou, abriu a porta e entrou.}} (She arrived, opened the door and went in.)
- {{Nós moramos em Lisboa por dois anos.}} (We lived in Lisbon for two years. It is over.)
- {{Em 2019 eu fui ao Brasil pela primeira vez.}} (In 2019 I went to Brazil for the first time.)

It also answers the question *what happened?* in a story. Lesson 11 adds a second past tense for background and habits, and shows when to choose each one. For now, the rule is: a finished event with a clear beginning and end uses the simple past.

### Time markers for the past

| Portuguese | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{ontem}} | `OH~-teh~ee` | yesterday |
| {{anteontem}} | `ah~-tchee-OH~-teh~ee` | the day before yesterday |
| {{ontem à noite}} | `OH~-teh~ee ah NOY-tchee` | last night |
| {{na semana passada}} | `nah seh-MAH-nah pah-SAH-dah` | last week |
| {{no mês passado}} | `noo mehss pah-SAH-doo` | last month |
| {{no ano passado}} | `noo AH-noo pah-SAH-doo` | last year |
| {{no sábado}}, {{na segunda}} | | on Saturday, on Monday |
| {{em 2019}} | | in 2019 |
| {{uma vez}} / {{duas vezes}} | `OO-mah vehss` | once / twice |
| {{de repente}} | `djee heh-PEH~-tchee` | suddenly |
| {{já}} | `zhah` | already; ever (in questions) |
| {{ainda não}} | `ah-EE~-dah NOW~` | not yet |
| {{nunca}} | `NOO~-kah` | never |

### Linking events in order

A story in the simple past is a chain of events. These small words keep the chain together, and Brazilians use them constantly in speech.

| Portuguese | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{primeiro}} | `pree-MAY-roo` | first |
| {{depois}} | `deh-POYSS` | then, afterwards |
| {{em seguida}} | `eh~ee seh-GEE-dah` | right after that |
| {{aí}} | `ah-EE` | then (spoken, very frequent) |
| {{então}} | `eh~-TOW~` | so, then |
| {{por fim}} | `por FEE~` | finally |
| {{quando}} | `KWAH~-doo` | when |

- {{Primeiro eu fui ao banco, depois almocei e por fim voltei para casa.}} (First I went to the bank, then I had lunch and finally I went back home.)
- {{Cheguei, aí o telefone tocou e eu atendi.}} (I arrived, then the phone rang and I answered.)
- {{Quando eu cheguei, a Clara saiu.}} (When I arrived, Clara left.)

With {{quando}}, two simple pasts in a row mean that one event followed the other: first I arrived, then she left. If you want to say that she had already left before you arrived, Lesson 11 gives you the tool for it.

### How long ago?

Three ways, all common:

- {{Cheguei há duas semanas.}} (I arrived two weeks ago.) {{Há}} + time, **before** the verb or after it.
- {{Cheguei duas semanas atrás.}} (same: two weeks ago.) {{Atrás}} after the time.
- {{Faz duas semanas que cheguei.}} (It is two weeks since I arrived.)

::: comparacao
| In English | In Portuguese |
|---|---|
| *I have already eaten.* | {{Já comi.}} |
| *I haven't eaten yet.* | {{Ainda não comi.}} |
| *I have never been to Japan.* | {{Nunca fui ao Japão.}} |
| *Have you ever been to Rio?* | {{Você já foi ao Rio?}} |
| *I have lived here for three years* (and still do) | {{Moro aqui há três anos.}} (present tense + {{há}}) |
| *I lived there for three years* (finished) | {{Morei lá por três anos.}} |
:::

Look at the fifth row. This is a very common error for English speakers: when the situation is **still true now**, Portuguese uses the **present tense** plus {{há}} or {{faz}}: {{Estudo português há seis meses}} (I have been studying Portuguese for six months). When it is over, use the simple past.

## 10.6 Questions, negatives and short answers

No helper verb. The order is the same as in a statement, and the voice rises, or a question word starts the sentence.

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Yes/no question | {{Você foi à festa?}} |
| Question word | {{O que você fez ontem?}} |
| Where to | {{Para onde vocês foram?}} |
| When | {{Quando ela chegou?}} |
| Who with | {{Com quem você foi?}} |
| How was it | {{Como foi a viagem?}} |
| Negative | {{Eu não fui à festa.}} |
| Never | {{Nunca fui a Salvador.}} |

For short answers, Brazilians **repeat the verb**. They do not say *yes, I did*. They say the verb:

| Question | Short answers |
|---|---|
| {{Você viu o jogo?}} | {{Vi.}} / {{Vi, sim.}} / {{Não vi.}} |
| {{Vocês foram à praia?}} | {{Fomos.}} / {{Fomos, sim.}} / {{Não fomos.}} |
| {{Ela já chegou?}} | {{Já.}} / {{Já chegou.}} / {{Ainda não.}} |
| {{Você comeu?}} | {{Comi.}} / {{Ainda não comi.}} |

Two useful sentences for conversation: {{Como foi o seu dia?}} (How was your day?) and {{Foi ótimo!}} / {{Foi bom.}} / {{Foi mais ou menos.}} (It was great / good / so-so). Notice that these use {{ser}} in the simple past: {{foi}}.

## 10.7 A few verbs worth meeting in the past

Some verbs are used in the simple past with a nuance you should learn early.

- **{{Ter}}** in the past often means *to have* in the sense of *to experience*: {{Tive uma ideia}} (I had an idea), {{Tive febre}} (I had a fever), {{Não tive tempo}} (I did not have time). With {{que}}, it means *had to*: {{Tive que sair cedo}} (I had to leave early).
- **{{Estar}}** in the past means *was in a place and went away*: {{Estive em Lisboa em março}} (I was in Lisbon in March). To say *I stayed at home*, Brazilians prefer {{ficar}}: {{Fiquei em casa}}.
- **{{Ficar}}** also means *to become*, and in the past it is very frequent: {{Fiquei cansado}} (I got tired), {{Fiquei com fome}} (I got hungry), {{Ela ficou feliz}} (she became happy).
- **{{Fazer}}** can be used for time: {{Fez frio ontem}} (it was cold yesterday).

## 10.8 What about "tenho comido"?

Portuguese has a second form that looks like the English present perfect: {{ter}} in the present plus the past participle: {{tenho comido}}, {{tenho trabalhado}}. It is not the translation of *I have eaten*. It means that something has been happening **repeatedly over a period up to now**: {{Tenho trabalhado muito}} (I have been working a lot lately). You will meet it in reading. For now, remember that the English *I have eaten / seen / been* is simply the simple past: {{comi, vi, fui}}.

## 10.9 Putting it together

Read this paragraph aloud, then answer the question.

> {{No ano passado eu fiz uma viagem inesquecível. Cheguei ao Rio de Janeiro numa segunda-feira de manhã e fiquei num hostel em Santa Teresa. No primeiro dia eu andei pelo centro e conheci uma garota argentina. Nós almoçamos juntas e depois fomos ao Pão de Açúcar. No terceiro dia choveu muito, então a gente ficou no hostel e jogou cartas. Na sexta-feira eu voltei para casa. Gostei muito de tudo, mas não vi o Cristo Redentor! Por isso, vou voltar.}}

Translation: *Last year I took an unforgettable trip. I arrived in Rio de Janeiro on a Monday morning and stayed in a hostel in Santa Teresa. On the first day I walked around downtown and met an Argentine girl. We had lunch together and then went to Sugarloaf. On the third day it rained a lot, so we stayed at the hostel and played cards. On Friday I went back home. I liked everything very much, but I did not see Christ the Redeemer! So, I am going to go back.*

Find the three irregular verbs. (They are {{fiz}}, {{fomos}} and {{vi}}.)

New words: {{inesquecível}} `ee-nehs-keh-SEE-vehw`, {{viagem}} `vee-AH-zheh~ee`, {{a garota}} `ah gah-ROH-tah` (girl), {{chover}} `shoh-VER` (to rain), {{jogar cartas}} `zhoh-GAR KAR-tahss` (to play cards).

## 10.10 The mistakes English speakers make

::: erros
| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ontem eu falo com ele. | {{Ontem eu falei com ele.}} | {{ontem}} needs the past tense |
| Eu fazi o trabalho. | {{Eu fiz o trabalho.}} | {{fazer}} is irregular in the past |
| Ele disseu que sim. | {{Ele disse que sim.}} | {{disse}} is irregular |
| Eu chegei às dez. | {{Eu cheguei às dez.}} | {{-gar}} verbs need {{-gu-}} in the {{eu}} form |
| Eu ficei em casa. | {{Eu fiquei em casa.}} | {{-car}} verbs need {{-qu-}} |
| Nós comeu muito. | {{Nós comemos muito.}} | the ending must match the person |
| Você fui à festa? | {{Você foi à festa?}} | {{fui}} is only for {{eu}} |
| Ele vim ontem. | {{Ele veio ontem.}} | {{vim}} is {{eu}}; {{veio}} is {{ele}} |

| Eu tenho ido ao Brasil duas vezes. | {{Eu já fui ao Brasil duas vezes.}} | the English "have been" is the simple past |

| Eles falam ontem. | {{Eles falaram ontem.}} | the plural past ends in {{-aram}}, with the stress on the second-to-last syllable |

## 10.13 Pocket summary: Lesson 10

::: resumo
- **No helper verb:** {{Você foi?}} / {{Não fui.}} The same tense covers the English simple past and present perfect ({{Já comi}}).
- **Regular endings:** {{-ar}}: {{-ei, -ou, -amos, -aram}}; {{-er}}: {{-i, -eu, -emos, -eram}}; {{-ir}}: {{-i, -iu, -imos, -iram}}. The stress is on the ending: {{fala}} / {{falou}}, {{falam}} / {{falaram}}.
- **Nós:** same form as the present for regular verbs. **A gente** takes the third-person singular.
- **Spelling in {{eu}}:** {{-car}} → {{fiquei}}, {{-gar}} → {{cheguei}}, {{-çar}} → {{comecei}}.
- **{{Ser}} and {{ir}} are identical:** {{fui, foi, fomos, foram}}. A place = {{ir}}; a noun or adjective = {{ser}}.
- **New stem:** {{tive}}, {{estive}}, {{fiz}}, {{disse}}, {{pude/pôde}}, {{quis}}, {{soube}}, {{pus/pôs}}, {{trouxe}}. **Others:** {{vi/viu}}, {{vim/veio}}, {{dei/deu}}, {{li/leu}}.
- **Markers:** {{ontem}}, {{semana passada}}, {{no ano passado}}, {{em 2019}}, {{uma vez}}, {{de repente}}, {{já}}, {{ainda não}}, {{nunca}}. **Ago:** {{há dois anos}}, {{dois anos atrás}}, {{faz dois anos}}.
- **Still true now:** present + {{há}}: {{Moro aqui há três anos}}.
- **Short answers repeat the verb:** {{Vi.}} / {{Fomos.}} / {{Ainda não.}}
:::
