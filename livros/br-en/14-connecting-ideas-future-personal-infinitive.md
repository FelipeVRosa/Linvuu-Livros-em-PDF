# Connecting ideas, the future with quando and se, and the personal infinitive

By the end of this lesson you can join sentences with the right connector, use relative pronouns beyond {{que}}, talk about the future after {{quando}}, {{se}} and {{assim que}}, and use the personal infinitive that Brazilians use after {{para}}, {{antes de}} and {{sem}}. This lesson reaches into B1, so it is a little denser than the others. At the end you will find the checkpoint for the whole book.

## 14.1 A scene you will live

Months have passed. Emma is back in Canada and Clara calls her on video. They are planning a visit.

::: dialogo
**Clara:** {{Emma! Eu vou aí em julho, se o meu chefe deixar. Assim que ele responder, eu compro a passagem.}}

**Emma:** {{Que ótimo! Quando você chegar, eu te busco no aeroporto. Se você quiser, pode ficar aqui em casa.}}

**Clara:** {{Obrigada! Mas antes de eu viajar, o meu chefe precisa assinar uns papéis. Depois que eu terminar o projeto, fico livre.}}

**Emma:** {{Tudo bem. Me avisa quando você souber a data. Enquanto você não vem, eu vou treinar o meu português, para eu não passar vergonha!}}

**Clara:** {{Que nada! Você fala bem. Mas se eu tiver tempo, a gente pratica por vídeo todo domingo.}}
:::

Translation: *Emma! I'm coming over in July, if my boss allows it. As soon as he answers, I'll buy the ticket. / Great! When you arrive, I'll pick you up at the airport. If you like, you can stay at my place. / Thank you! But before I travel, my boss needs to sign a few papers. After I finish the project, I'll be free. / Fine. Let me know when you know the date. While you're not here, I'll practise my Portuguese, so I don't embarrass myself! / Nonsense! You speak well. But if I have time, we can practise by video every Sunday.*

Read the scene again and notice what is new. Every sentence about the future uses a verb you have not met: {{deixar}}, {{responder}}, {{chegar}}, {{quiser}}, {{terminar}}, {{souber}}, {{tiver}}. They look like the infinitive, and some look odd ({{quiser}}, {{souber}}), because they belong to the **future subjunctive**. The words {{antes de eu viajar}} and {{para eu não passar vergonha}} are the **personal infinitive**.

New words from the scene:

| Portuguese | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{a passagem}} | `ah pah-SAH-zheh~ee` | the ticket (bus, plane) |
| {{o chefe}} | `oo SHEH-fee` | the boss |
| {{assim que}} | `ah-SEE~ kee` | as soon as |
| {{buscar}} | `boos-KAR` | to pick up, to fetch |
| {{resolver}} | `heh-sol-VER` | to sort out, to solve |
| {{enquanto}} | `eh~-KWAH~-too` | while |
| {{passar vergonha}} | `pah-SAR ver-GOH~-nyah` | to be embarrassed |
| {{Que nada!}} | `kee NAH-dah` | Nonsense! Not at all! |

## 14.2 The trap: English uses the present for the future, Portuguese does not

In English you say *When you arrive, call me* and *If it rains, we stay home*. The verbs *arrive* and *rains* are in the present, even though the events are in the future. Portuguese is more precise. After **{{quando}}**, **{{se}}**, **{{assim que}}** and a few other words, a future event needs a special verb form, the **future subjunctive**.

| English | Portuguese |
|---|---|
| *When you arrive, call me.* | {{Quando você chegar, me liga.}} |
| *If it rains, we stay home.* | {{Se chover, a gente fica em casa.}} |
| *As soon as I finish, I'll call you.* | {{Assim que eu terminar, eu te ligo.}} |

The other trap is in the infinitive. English says *before I leave* and *so that I understand* with a plain verb. Portuguese lets the infinitive have its own subject, and that makes the sentence shorter and clearer ({{antes de eu sair}}). Both ideas are simpler than they sound, and each has a method.

## 14.3 Joining ideas with connectors

A connector is a small word that tells the listener how two ideas are related: addition, contrast, cause, consequence, time, condition. Learn them by function, as pairs of English and Portuguese.

| Function | Portuguese | Guide | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| addition | {{e}}, {{também}}, {{além disso}} | `ee`, `tah~-BEH~ee`, `ah-LEH~ee DJEE-soo` | {{Estudo e trabalho.}} {{Além disso, falo inglês.}} |
| contrast | {{mas}}, {{porém}}, {{só que}} | `mahss`, `poh-REH~ee`, `saw kee` | {{Quero ir, mas não posso.}} {{É bom, só que é caro.}} |
| contrast (although) | {{embora}}, {{apesar de}} | `eh~-BAW-rah`, `ah-peh-ZAR djee` | {{Apesar do frio, saímos.}} (Despite the cold, we went out.) |
| cause | {{porque}}, {{como}}, {{já que}} | `poor-KEE`, `KOH-moo`, `zhah kee` | {{Fiquei em casa porque estava doente.}} {{Como choveu, ficamos em casa.}} |
| consequence | {{então}}, {{por isso}}, {{portanto}} | `eh~-TOW~`, `por EE-soo`, `por-TAH~-too` | {{Estava cansado, por isso fui dormir.}} |
| time | {{quando}}, {{enquanto}}, {{assim que}}, {{depois que}}, {{antes de}} | | {{Enquanto você cozinha, eu arrumo a mesa.}} |
| condition | {{se}}, {{caso}} | `see`, `KAH-zoo` | {{Se der tempo, eu passo aí.}} |
| alternative | {{ou}}, {{ou... ou}} | `oh` | {{Ou você vai, ou eu vou.}} |

::: passo 1 Use {{então}} to start and to conclude
{{Então}} is the most common word in spoken Portuguese. It means "so" or "then": {{Então, o que você vai fazer?}} (So, what are you going to do?) It also opens a story: {{Então, ontem eu encontrei a Clara...}}
:::

::: passo 2 Use {{mas}} and not {{mais}}
{{Mas}} (but) and {{mais}} (more) look alike and sound different (`mahss` and `mah-EESS`). Do not write ~~Quero ir, mais não posso~~.
:::

::: passo 3 Put the verb that fits after the connector
After {{porque}}, {{mas}}, {{por isso}} and {{então}}, use the normal tenses you already know. After {{quando}}, {{se}} and {{assim que}} in the **future**, use the future subjunctive (section 14.5). After {{embora}}, Brazilians use a third form, the present subjunctive ({{embora faça frio}}), which you only need to recognize at this level.
:::

::: atencao
**{{Porque}} is not {{por isso}}.** {{Porque}} gives the cause and comes before it: {{Fui dormir porque estava cansado.}} {{Por isso}} gives the consequence and comes after the cause: {{Estava cansado, por isso fui dormir.}} English *so* sometimes translates one and sometimes the other.
:::

## 14.4 Relative pronouns beyond {{que}}

Lesson 13 gave you {{que}}, the pronoun that joins a clause to a noun. This section completes the set, so that you can say almost anything about a person, a place or a thing.

| Word | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| {{que}} | person or thing, subject or object | {{O livro que eu li.}} / {{A mulher que mora aqui.}} |
| {{quem}} | a person, after a preposition | {{A pessoa com quem eu falei.}} / {{Quem pergunta quer saber.}} (Whoever asks wants to know.) |
| {{onde}} | a place | {{A cidade onde eu nasci.}} |
| {{o que}} | "what", or "which" for a whole idea | {{Não sei o que ele disse.}} / {{Ele chegou atrasado, o que me irritou.}} |
| {{tudo o que}} | everything that | {{Tudo o que eu tenho é seu.}} |
| {{cujo}} / {{cuja}} | whose (formal; recognize it) | {{O homem cujo filho é médico.}} |

**Onde and aonde.** {{Onde}} asks or says where something **is**: {{Onde você mora?}} {{Aonde}} asks where you are **going to**, and goes with verbs of movement: {{Aonde você vai?}} In speech, many Brazilians use {{onde}} for both, but writing keeps the difference.

**The preposition travels with the pronoun.** The verb decides which preposition you need, and it goes before {{que}} or {{quem}}:

- {{O filme de que eu gosto}} (the film I like), because you say {{gostar de}}.
- {{A cidade em que eu moro}} or {{onde eu moro}} (the city I live in).
- {{As pessoas com quem eu trabalho}} (the people I work with).
- {{O amigo a quem eu escrevi}} (the friend I wrote to). In speech, {{para quem}} is more common.

::: dica
In conversation, Brazilians often drop the preposition: {{O filme que eu gosto}}, {{A cidade que eu moro}}. It is very common and you will hear it everywhere. In writing, and in an interview or an exam, keep the preposition: {{o filme de que eu gosto}}.
:::

## 14.5 The future with quando, se and assim que: the future subjunctive

This is the most useful new idea of the lesson, and it has a simple method.

### How to form it

Start with the **{{eles}} form of the simple past** (Lesson 10), the one that ends in **-ram**. Drop **-ram** and add the endings of the future subjunctive.

| | {{eu}}, {{você}}, {{ele}}, {{ela}} | {{nós}} | {{vocês}}, {{eles}}, {{elas}} |
|---|---|---|---|
| ending | **-r** | **-rmos** | **-rem** |

Look at what this gives you.

| Infinitive | {{eles}} form of the past | Stem | {{eu}}, {{você}}, {{ele}} | {{nós}} | {{vocês}}, {{eles}} |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| {{falar}} | {{falaram}} | {{fala-}} | {{falar}} | {{falarmos}} | {{falarem}} |
| {{comer}} | {{comeram}} | {{come-}} | {{comer}} | {{comermos}} | {{comerem}} |
| {{partir}} | {{partiram}} | {{parti-}} | {{partir}} | {{partirmos}} | {{partirem}} |
| {{fazer}} | {{fizeram}} | {{fize-}} | {{fizer}} `fee-ZEHR` | {{fizermos}} | {{fizerem}} |
| {{ter}} | {{tiveram}} | {{tive-}} | {{tiver}} `tchee-VEHR` | {{tivermos}} | {{tiverem}} |
| {{estar}} | {{estiveram}} | {{estive-}} | {{estiver}} | {{estivermos}} | {{estiverem}} |
| {{ser}}, {{ir}} | {{foram}} | {{fo-}} | {{for}} `for` | {{formos}} | {{forem}} |
| {{ver}} | {{viram}} | {{vi-}} | {{vir}} `veer` | {{virmos}} | {{virem}} |
| {{vir}} | {{vieram}} | {{vie-}} | {{vier}} `vee-EHR` | {{viermos}} | {{vierem}} |
| {{dizer}} | {{disseram}} | {{disse-}} | {{disser}} | {{dissermos}} | {{disserem}} |
| {{poder}} | {{puderam}} | {{pude-}} | {{puder}} `poo-DEHR` | {{pudermos}} | {{puderem}} |
| {{querer}} | {{quiseram}} | {{quise-}} | {{quiser}} `kee-ZEHR` | {{quisermos}} | {{quiserem}} |
| {{saber}} | {{souberam}} | {{soube-}} | {{souber}} `soh-BEHR` | {{soubermos}} | {{souberem}} |
| {{dar}} | {{deram}} | {{de-}} | {{der}} | {{dermos}} | {{derem}} |

Two facts make this easy. For all **regular** verbs, the future subjunctive of {{eu}}, {{você}} and {{ele}} is identical to the infinitive: {{falar}}, {{comer}}, {{partir}}. The context shows which one you mean. For **irregular** verbs, the method gives the form automatically from the past, so you only need to know the past forms you already studied in Lesson 10.

### When to use it

Use the future subjunctive when an event **has not happened yet** and the sentence is introduced by one of these words.

| Introduced by | Example |
|---|---|
| {{quando}} (when) | {{Quando você chegar, me liga.}} (When you arrive, call me.) |
| {{se}} (if) | {{Se chover, a gente fica em casa.}} (If it rains, we stay home.) |
| {{assim que}}, {{logo que}} (as soon as) | {{Assim que eu terminar, eu te ligo.}} |
| {{depois que}} (after) | {{Depois que ele sair, a gente conversa.}} |
| {{enquanto}} (while, in the future) | {{Enquanto você não vier, eu estudo.}} |
| {{sempre que}} (whenever) | {{Sempre que eu puder, eu vou.}} |
| {{quem}}, {{o que}}, {{como}}, {{onde}} (anything, anyone) | {{Quem quiser pode vir.}} {{Faça como você quiser.}} |

The other verb in the sentence is in a tense you already know: the present, the imperative, {{ir}} + infinitive, or the simple future.

- {{Quando eu tiver tempo, vou estudar mais.}} (When I have time, I will study more.)
- {{Se você quiser, eu te ajudo.}} (If you want, I'll help you.)
- {{Se eu puder, eu vou.}} (If I can, I'll go.)
- {{Quando vocês chegarem, liguem para mim.}} (When you arrive, call me.)
- {{Se a gente sair cedo, chega antes do almoço.}} (If we leave early, we arrive before lunch.)

### Compare with the habit and with the past

The future subjunctive is **only for the future**. For habits and for the past, use the tenses you already know:

| Time | Portuguese | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| habit | {{Quando chego em casa, ligo pra ela.}} | When I get home, I call her. (every time) |
| past | {{Quando cheguei em casa, liguei pra ela.}} | When I got home, I called her. |
| future | {{Quando eu chegar em casa, vou ligar pra ela.}} | When I get home, I'll call her. |

::: atencao
**Do not copy the English present.** ~~Quando você chega, me liga~~ sounds like "whenever you arrive", a habit. For one specific future event, use {{Quando você chegar}}. In the same way, ~~Se chove, a gente fica em casa~~ works only as a general rule; for tomorrow's rain, use {{Se chover}}.
:::

::: dica
For a "what if" that is unlikely or impossible ({{Se eu pudesse...}}, *If I could...*), Portuguese uses yet another form, the imperfect subjunctive. It belongs to the next level. For now, recognize {{Se eu pudesse}} and {{Se eu tivesse tempo}}, and answer with {{Se eu puder}} or {{Se eu tiver tempo}} for real plans.
:::

## 14.6 The personal infinitive

### The trap: English has one infinitive, Portuguese has an infinitive with a person

In English, *to speak* does not change. In Portuguese, the infinitive can take endings that show who is doing the action. This is useful when the subject of the infinitive is **different from the subject of the main verb**, and the result is clearer than English.

### How to form it

| Person | Ending | {{falar}} | {{fazer}} | {{ir}} |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| {{eu}}, {{você}}, {{ele}}, {{ela}} | nothing | {{falar}} | {{fazer}} | {{ir}} |
| {{nós}} | **-mos** | {{falarmos}} | {{fazermos}} | {{irmos}} |
| {{vocês}}, {{eles}}, {{elas}} | **-em** | {{falarem}} | {{fazerem}} | {{irem}} |

The forms are the same as the future subjunctive for regular verbs, but the use is different. In the personal infinitive, the verb comes after a **preposition or an expression**: {{para}}, {{antes de}}, {{depois de}}, {{sem}}, {{até}}, {{por}}, {{é importante}}.

### When to use it

::: passo 1 If the subject is the same, use the plain infinitive
{{Antes de sair, eu fecho a janela.}} (Before leaving, I close the window.) {{Estudo para passar no exame.}} (I study to pass the exam.) One person does both things, so you do not need anything more. (You may still hear {{para eu não passar vergonha}}, when the speaker wants to stress who does it.)
:::

::: passo 2 If the subject is different, give the infinitive its own subject
- {{Antes de eu sair, a Clara chegou.}} (Before I left, Clara arrived.)
- {{Para você entender, eu preciso explicar de novo.}} (For you to understand, I need to explain again.) The infinitive {{entender}} belongs to {{você}}, and the main verb belongs to {{eu}}.
- {{Depois de nós jantarmos, a Clara lava a louça.}} (After we have dinner, Clara washes the dishes.)
- {{Vou esperar até vocês chegarem.}} (I will wait until you arrive.)
- {{Sem eles saberem, eu preparei a festa.}} (Without them knowing, I prepared the party.)
:::

::: passo 3 After impersonal expressions, use the ending for {{nós}} and {{eles}}
- {{É importante vocês chegarem cedo.}} (It is important for you to arrive early.)
- {{É difícil eles aceitarem a proposta.}} (It is hard for them to accept the proposal.)
- {{É melhor a gente sair agora.}} (It is better for us to leave now.) With {{a gente}}, the verb stays in the plain form.
:::

::: dica
**A safe rule for beginners.** For {{eu}}, {{você}}, {{ele}} and {{ela}}, the personal infinitive looks exactly like the ordinary infinitive, so you can never get it wrong: {{para eu falar}}, {{antes de você sair}}. The only forms you must learn are the ones for {{nós}} (-mos) and {{eles}} (-em). In speech, Brazilians often replace the plural forms with {{a gente}} and a plain infinitive.
:::

## 14.7 Putting it together

Read this message from Emma to Clara. It uses connectors, relative pronouns, the future subjunctive and the personal infinitive.

> {{Oi, Clara! Se tudo der certo, o meu chefe vai me liberar em setembro, então a gente pode combinar a sua visita. Quando você souber a data, me avisa, porque eu preciso reservar o quarto onde você vai ficar. Antes de você chegar, vou comprar tudo o que você gosta: pão de queijo, café e o chocolate que a gente comeu em Belo Horizonte. Assim que eu tiver o itinerário, te mando. Estou treinando todo dia, para a gente conversar só em português. Se eu errar, me corrige, tá? Beijos!}}

Translation: *Hi, Clara! If everything works out, my boss will let me off in September, so we can plan your visit. When you know the date, let me know, because I need to book the room where you will stay. Before you arrive, I'll buy everything you like: pão de queijo, coffee and the chocolate we ate in Belo Horizonte. As soon as I have the itinerary, I'll send it to you. I am practising every day, so that we can talk only in Portuguese. If I make a mistake, correct me, OK? Kisses!*

Find the future subjunctive ({{der}}, {{souber}}, {{tiver}}, {{errar}}), the personal infinitive ({{antes de você chegar}}, {{para a gente conversar}}), the relatives ({{onde}}, {{tudo o que}}, {{que}}) and the connectors ({{então}}, {{porque}}, {{assim que}}).

New words: {{liberar}} `lee-beh-RAR` (to let off, to release), {{combinar}} `koh~-bee-NAR` (to arrange, to agree on), {{o itinerário}} `oo ee-tchee-neh-RAH-ryoo` (the itinerary), {{corrigir}} `koh-hee-ZHEER` (to correct).

## 14.8 The mistakes English speakers make

::: erros
| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Quando você chega, me liga. | {{Quando você chegar, me liga.}} | one future event needs the future subjunctive |
| Se eu tenho tempo, eu vou. | {{Se eu tiver tempo, eu vou.}} | after {{se}}, a real future condition uses {{tiver}} |
| Se eu poder, eu vou. | {{Se eu puder, eu vou.}} | the form is not the infinitive: {{puderam}} → {{puder}} |
| Assim que eu termino, te ligo. | {{Assim que eu terminar, te ligo.}} | {{assim que}} + future subjunctive |
| Quando eu fazer isso, ... | {{Quando eu fizer isso, ...}} | irregular verbs come from the past: {{fizeram}} → {{fizer}} |
| Antes de eu saio, ... | {{Antes de eu sair, ...}} | after a preposition, the infinitive stays an infinitive |
| Para nós falar, a professora repete. | {{Para nós falarmos, a professora repete.}} | {{nós}} takes {{-mos}} in the personal infinitive |
| Quero ir, mais não posso. | {{Quero ir, mas não posso.}} | {{mas}} (but) is not {{mais}} (more) |
| Estava cansado porque fui dormir. | {{Estava cansado, por isso fui dormir.}} | cause and consequence are not the same word |
| O filme que eu gosto (in an exam) | {{O filme de que eu gosto}} | in writing, keep the preposition before {{que}} |
| Aonde você mora? | {{Onde você mora?}} | {{aonde}} is only for movement |
| Todo mundo chegaram. | {{Todo mundo chegou.}} | {{todo mundo}} takes the singular |
:::

## 14.11 Pocket summary: Lesson 14

::: resumo
- **Connectors:** {{e}}, {{mas}}, {{ou}}; cause {{porque}}, {{como}}; consequence {{então}}, {{por isso}}; time {{quando}}, {{enquanto}}, {{assim que}}; condition {{se}}. {{Mas}} is "but", {{mais}} is "more".
- **Relatives:** {{que}}, {{quem}} (after a preposition), {{onde}}, {{o que}}, {{tudo o que}}, {{cujo}} (recognize). {{Aonde}} only with movement.
- **Future subjunctive:** {{eles}} form of the past, drop {{-ram}}, add {{-r}}, {{-rmos}}, {{-rem}}. Regular: the same as the infinitive. Irregular: {{fizer}}, {{tiver}}, {{for}}, {{vier}}, {{puder}}, {{quiser}}, {{souber}}.
- **Use:** a future event after {{quando}}, {{se}}, {{assim que}}, {{depois que}}, {{enquanto}}, {{sempre que}}. Habit: present. Past: simple past.
- **Personal infinitive:** infinitive + {{-mos}} ({{nós}}), + {{-em}} ({{vocês}}, {{eles}}). After {{para}}, {{antes de}}, {{depois de}}, {{sem}}, {{até}}, {{é importante}}. Use it when the subjects differ.
- **Safe rule:** for {{eu}}, {{você}}, {{ele}} the personal infinitive equals the plain infinitive: {{para eu falar}}, {{antes de você sair}}.

## 14.12 Checkpoint: A2 and the start of B1

You have reached the end of the book. This checklist shows what you should be able to do in Portuguese now. Take it slowly, with a pen. For each statement, test yourself out loud or in writing. Tick a statement only when you can do it **without looking at the lesson**.

1. **Sounds (Lesson 1).** I can read any Portuguese word aloud with the right stress, a nasal vowel ({{pão}}), the *tch* and *dj* sounds ({{dia}}, {{noite}}), and the "h" sound of the initial *r* ({{rua}}), and spell my name with the Portuguese letter names. *Test:* read the paragraph in Lesson 1.13 aloud, then spell your full name.
2. **Who I am (Lesson 2).** I can say who I am, where I am from, my nationality and my job with {{ser}}, use {{eu, você, a gente, nós, vocês, eles}} correctly, and make adjectives agree. *Test:* introduce yourself and a friend in six sentences.
3. **Things (Lesson 3).** I can choose {{o, a, os, as, um, uma}} correctly, guess gender from the ending, make plurals, and use {{no, na, do, da}}. *Test:* name ten objects in your room with the right article.
4. **Habits and questions (Lesson 4).** I can use regular {{-ar}}, {{-er}} and {{-ir}} verbs in the present, make negatives with {{não}}, and ask with {{quem, o que, onde, quando, como}}. *Test:* write five things you do on Saturday and five questions to a friend.
5. **Being, having and existing (Lesson 5).** I can choose between {{ser}} and {{estar}}, say where things are, and say that something exists with {{tem}} or {{há}}. *Test:* describe your bedroom in six sentences.
6. **Numbers and time (Lesson 6).** I can say numbers up to a thousand, tell the time, say a date and ask and give a price. *Test:* say today's date, the time, and the price of a coffee.
7. **Wanting and being able (Lesson 7).** I can use {{ir, ter, fazer, poder, querer, saber, vir, dizer, ver}} in the present, and distinguish {{saber}} from {{conhecer}}. *Test:* ask three polite questions with {{poder}} and say three things you want to do.
8. **Mine, yours, this one (Lesson 8).** I can say whose something is with {{meu, seu, dele, dela}}, point with {{esse, aquele, aqui, ali}}, and say what I like and prefer with {{gostar de}} and {{preferir}}. *Test:* talk for thirty seconds about your family and what you like.
9. **My day and my plans (Lesson 9).** I can describe my routine with reflexive verbs, say what I am going to do with {{ir}} + infinitive, and say what I am doing with {{estar}} + gerund. *Test:* describe a normal day, tomorrow's plan and what you are doing now.
10. **The past (Lessons 10 and 11).** I can tell what happened with the simple past ({{fui, fiz, vi, comi}}), describe how things were with the imperfect ({{era, tinha, morava}}) and choose between them. *Test:* tell a ten-sentence story about last weekend, with at least three descriptions.
11. **Small words (Lesson 12).** I can use {{me}} and {{te}} before the verb, say {{vi ele}} and {{dei pra ela}} as Brazilians do, use {{mim}}, {{comigo}} and {{para eu fazer}}, and ask {{Você pode me ajudar?}} *Test:* write five messages to a friend with {{me}} or {{te}}.
12. **Asking, comparing, relating (Lesson 13).** I can compare things, give an instruction or a direction, make a request with {{queria}}, {{poderia}} or {{gostaria}}, choose {{por}} or {{para}}, and join ideas with {{que}}. *Test:* in a restaurant role-play, ask for a table, a recommendation and the bill.
13. **The future and longer sentences (Lesson 14).** I can use {{quando}}, {{se}} and {{assim que}} with the future subjunctive, join ideas with connectors, use {{onde}}, {{quem}} and {{o que}}, and use the personal infinitive after {{para}} and {{antes de}}. *Test:* in one minute, describe your next trip with {{quando}}, {{se}}, {{antes de}} and {{para}}.

**How to read your result.** If you can tick **eleven to thirteen**, you work comfortably at the A2 level and you have started B1: you are ready to move on to the next book. If you tick **eight to ten**, go back to the lessons you did not tick and redo their practice before moving on. If you tick **fewer than eight**, do not worry: go back to the first lesson you did not tick and work forward one lesson per week, saying every exercise aloud. In every case, spend ten minutes a day listening to Brazilian Portuguese: podcasts, music, WhatsApp voice messages from your friends. The grammar in this book is the skeleton; the listening will give it flesh.

## 14.13 What comes next

This is the last lesson of the book. You started by learning to read {{bom dia}} aloud. You can now tell a story, ask for things politely, find your way around a Brazilian city, plan a future with {{quando}} and {{se}}, and make a friend feel that you are speaking their language. The appendices that follow are for consulting, not for reading: tenses and moods, irregular verbs, false friends, the verbs and their prepositions, and a table that sends you from your own mistake to the right lesson.

The next step is to use what you have learned every day: with Clara's cake, Seu Antônio's stories, a WhatsApp group, a short trip. You will make mistakes. That is not a problem. Brazilians are generous with learners, and every mistake you correct brings you closer to a natural voice.
