# Numbers, time, dates and prices: the practical lesson

By the end of this lesson you can count from zero to a thousand and beyond, tell the time and say when something happens, give the date and the day of the week, and ask about and understand prices in reais.

## 6.1 A scene you will live

Daniel stands at the ticket window of the bus station in São Paulo. He wants to go to Curitiba today. The clerk looks up from her screen.

::: dialogo
**Daniel:** {{Boa tarde. Tem ônibus para Curitiba hoje?}}

**Atendente:** {{Tem, sim. Tem um às quatro e meia e outro às oito da noite.}}

**Daniel:** {{Quanto custa a passagem?}}

**Atendente:** {{Cento e vinte e cinco reais.}}

**Daniel:** {{Posso pagar no cartão?}}

**Atendente:** {{Pode, sim. Débito ou crédito?}}

**Daniel:** {{Crédito. Que horas são agora?}}

**Atendente:** {{São duas e quinze.}}

**Daniel:** {{Então vou pegar o das quatro e meia. Obrigado!}}
:::

*Daniel: Good afternoon. Is there a bus to Curitiba today? / Clerk: Yes, there is. There's one at half past four and another at eight at night. / Daniel: How much is the ticket? / Clerk: One hundred and twenty-five reais. / Daniel: Can I pay by card? / Clerk: Yes, you can. Debit or credit? / Daniel: Credit. What time is it now? / Clerk: It's two fifteen. / Daniel: Then I'll take the four-thirty one. Thank you!*

In a single minute, the scene uses a time of day, a price in reais, a number in the hundreds, and the clock. Notice two details that this lesson explains. The clerk says {{às quatro e meia}} (at half past four) and {{às oito da noite}} (at eight in the evening), with {{às}} for "at" in front of the hour. And she says {{São duas e quinze}} with the plural verb {{são}}, because the hour is plural. At one o'clock she would say {{É uma hora}}, with {{é}}.

| Word | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{a passagem}} | `ah pah-SAH-zheh~ee` | the ticket (for transport) |
| {{o cartão}} | `oo kar-TOW~` | the card |
| {{o débito}} | `oo DEH-bee-too` | debit |
| {{o crédito}} | `oo KREH-djee-too` | credit |
| {{o atendente / a atendente}} | `ah-teh~-DEH~-tchee` | the clerk, the attendant |
| {{agora}} | `ah-GAW-rah` | now |

## 6.2 The trap: plural hours, genders in numbers, and decimal commas

English numbers are simple: they never change, and the time is always "it is". Portuguese has four small surprises.

1. **The time is plural, except at one o'clock.** {{São duas horas}}, {{São dez horas}}, but {{É uma hora}}, {{É meio-dia}} and {{É meia-noite}}. English says *it is* every time.
2. **Some numbers have gender.** {{Um}} / {{uma}}, {{dois}} / {{duas}}, and the hundreds from 200: {{duzentos}} / {{duzentas}}. You say {{duas casas}} and {{dois livros}}.
3. **"One thousand" is just {{mil}}.** Never {{um mil}}. And {{cem}} becomes {{cento}} when something follows: {{cem}} but {{cento e vinte}}.
4. **The punctuation is reversed.** Brazil writes the decimal with a comma and the thousands with a dot: R$ 12,50 is "twelve reais and fifty centavos", and R$ 1.250,00 is "one thousand two hundred fifty reais".

## 6.4 From twenty-one to ninety-nine

The tens are regular. To make a number between 21 and 99, say the ten, then **{{e}}** (and), then the unit.

| Number | Portuguese | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | {{vinte}} | `VEE~-tchee` |
| 30 | {{trinta}} | `TREE~-tah` |
| 40 | {{quarenta}} | `kwah-REH~-tah` |
| 50 | {{cinquenta}} | `see~-KWEH~-tah` |
| 60 | {{sessenta}} | `seh-SEH~-tah` |
| 70 | {{setenta}} | `seh-TEH~-tah` |
| 80 | {{oitenta}} | `oy-TEH~-tah` |
| 90 | {{noventa}} | `noh-VEH~-tah` |

So 21 is {{vinte e um}}, 35 is {{trinta e cinco}}, 48 is {{quarenta e oito}}, 99 is {{noventa e nove}}. For feminine nouns use the feminine forms: {{vinte e uma pessoas}}, {{trinta e duas mulheres}}.

::: dica
Say the tens aloud in a row: *vinte, trinta, quarenta, cinquenta, sessenta, setenta, oitenta, noventa*. The pattern never changes: ten + {{e}} + unit. In speech, many people drop the {{e}} when they read a number quickly, but the full form is the correct one.
:::

## 6.5 Hundreds, thousands and millions

The hundreds are the part where English speakers make mistakes, because there is a small change of form.

| Number | Portuguese | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | {{cem}} (alone) / {{cento}} (when more follows) | `SEH~ee` / `SEH~-too` |
| 200 | {{duzentos / duzentas}} | `doo-ZEH~-tooss` |
| 300 | {{trezentos / trezentas}} | `treh-ZEH~-tooss` |
| 400 | {{quatrocentos / quatrocentas}} | `kwah-troh-SEH~-tooss` |
| 500 | {{quinhentos / quinhentas}} | `kee-NYEH~-tooss` |
| 600 | {{seiscentos / seiscentas}} | `say-SEH~-tooss` |
| 700 | {{setecentos / setecentas}} | `seh-tchee-SEH~-tooss` |
| 800 | {{oitocentos / oitocentas}} | `oy-too-SEH~-tooss` |
| 900 | {{novecentos / novecentas}} | `naw-vee-SEH~-tooss` |
| 1,000 | {{mil}} | `MEE-oo` |

::: passo 1 Use "cem" and "cento" correctly
Exactly 100 is {{cem}}: {{cem reais}}. Anything between 101 and 199 uses {{cento}}: {{cento e um}} (101), {{cento e vinte e cinco}} (125, as in the scene), {{cento e noventa e nove}} (199).
:::

::: passo 2 Put "e" between the parts
Connect the hundreds, tens and units with {{e}}: {{trezentos e quarenta e dois}} (342), {{novecentos e noventa e nove}} (999). This is different from English, which does not use "and" (or uses it only once).
:::

::: passo 3 Make the hundreds agree
From 200, the hundreds agree in gender with the noun they count: {{duzentos livros}} (masculine), {{duzentas casas}} (feminine), {{quinhentas pessoas}}. The same holds for the final {{um/uma}} and {{dois/duas}} of a long number: {{cento e vinte e uma casas}}, {{trezentos e dois livros}}.
:::

### Thousands and beyond

{{Mil}} never changes and never needs {{um}}: {{mil reais}} (1,000), {{dois mil reais}} (2,000), {{vinte mil}} (20,000), {{cem mil}} (100,000). A million is a noun: {{um milhão}} `mee-LYOW~`, {{dois milhões}} `mee-LYOH~eess`, and it takes {{de}} before the noun: {{um milhão de reais}}, {{dois milhões de pessoas}}.

To join the parts of a large number, use {{e}} before the **last** element when it is a number below 100 or a round hundred: {{mil e vinte}} (1,020), {{mil e duzentos}} (1,200), {{dois mil e quinhentos}} (2,500). In other cases there is no {{e}} right after {{mil}}: {{mil duzentos e trinta}} (1,230).

Years are written and said as ordinary numbers: 2026 is {{dois mil e vinte e seis}}, 1999 is {{mil novecentos e noventa e nove}}. The preposition is {{em}}: {{em dois mil e vinte e seis}}.

## 6.7 What time is it?

### Ask and answer

The question is {{Que horas são?}} `kee AW-rahss SOW~`, literally "what hours are?" It is plural, and so is the answer:

| Time | Portuguese |
|---|---|
| 1:00 | {{É uma hora.}} |
| 2:00 | {{São duas horas.}} |
| 3:00 | {{São três horas.}} |
| 12:00 | {{É meio-dia.}} `MAY-oo DJEE-ah` |
| 12:00 at night | {{É meia-noite.}} `MAY-ah NOY-tchee` |

Rule: from 2:00 on, the verb is {{são}} and the hour is plural. At 1:00 (and at noon and midnight) the verb is {{é}}, singular. The same holds when minutes are added: {{É uma e dez}}, {{São duas e dez}}.

### Minutes and halves

To add minutes after the hour, say **{{e}}**: {{São três e dez}} (3:10), {{São três e vinte e cinco}} (3:25). The half hour is **{{e meia}}** `ee MAY-ah`: {{São três e meia}} (3:30). The quarter hour is **{{e quinze}}** (spoken) or **{{e um quarto}}**: {{São três e quinze}}.

To say minutes **before** the hour, use **{{para}}** (or the spoken {{pra}}) with the next hour: {{São quinze para as quatro}} (3:45, literally "fifteen to four"), {{São dez para as duas}} (1:50), {{É um quarto para a uma}} (12:45). You can say it with {{faltam}}: {{Faltam dez para as duas}} `FAH-oo-tah~oo` (ten minutes are missing until two).

| Time | Portuguese |
|---|---|
| 8:00 | {{São oito horas.}} |
| 8:15 | {{São oito e quinze.}} |
| 8:30 | {{São oito e meia.}} |
| 8:45 | {{São quinze para as nove.}} |
| 8:50 | {{São dez para as nove.}} |

Add **{{da manhã}}** (a.m.), **{{da tarde}}** (afternoon) and **{{da noite}}** (evening, night) to be clear: {{São oito horas da manhã}}, {{São três da tarde}}, {{São nove da noite}}. Airlines and timetables use the 24-hour clock: {{às catorze horas}}.

### When something happens

For "at" a time, use **{{à}} / {{às}}**: the fused form of {{a}} + the article that you saw in Lesson 5. It agrees with the hour: **{{à}} {{uma}}**, **{{às}} {{duas}}**, **{{às}} {{três}}**.

| Portuguese | Meaning |
|---|---|
| {{A aula é às oito.}} | The class is at eight. |
| {{O ônibus sai à uma e meia.}} | The bus leaves at one-thirty. |
| {{Almoço ao meio-dia.}} | I have lunch at noon. |
| {{Durmo à meia-noite.}} | I sleep at midnight. |
| {{A que horas é a reunião?}} | At what time is the meeting? |
| {{Chego às seis em ponto.}} | I arrive at six sharp. |

The question for "at what time" is {{A que horas…?}} `ah kee AW-rahss`, and the answer begins with {{às}} or {{à}}. A nice detail: *early* is {{cedo}} `SEH-doo`, *late* is {{tarde}}, *sharp* is {{em ponto}} `eh~ee POH~-too`, and *about* is {{mais ou menos}} (more or less).

## 6.8 Days, months and dates

### The days of the week

| Day | Guide |
|---|---|
| {{segunda-feira}} | `seh-GOO~-dah FAY-rah` |
| {{terça-feira}} | `TER-sah FAY-rah` |
| {{quarta-feira}} | `KWAR-tah FAY-rah` |
| {{quinta-feira}} | `KEE~-tah FAY-rah` |
| {{sexta-feira}} | `SEHS-tah FAY-rah` |
| {{sábado}} | `SAH-bah-doo` |
| {{domingo}} | `doh-MEE~-goo` |

In daily speech Brazilians drop {{-feira}}: {{segunda}}, {{terça}}, {{quarta}}, {{quinta}}, {{sexta}}. The days from Monday to Friday are feminine ({{a segunda}}), while {{sábado}} and {{domingo}} are masculine ({{o sábado}}). Days are not capitalized. To say "on a day", use **em** with the article: {{na segunda}}, {{no sábado}}; for "every Monday" use the plural: {{às segundas}}, {{aos sábados}}. Related words: {{hoje}} `OH-zhee` (today), {{amanhã}} `ah-mah-NYAH~` (tomorrow), {{ontem}} `OH~-teh~ee` (yesterday), {{o fim de semana}} (the weekend).

### The months and seasons

| Month | Guide | Month | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| {{janeiro}} | `zhah-NAY-roo` | {{julho}} | `ZHOO-lyoo` |
| {{fevereiro}} | `feh-veh-RAY-roo` | {{agosto}} | `ah-GOHS-too` |
| {{março}} | `MAR-soo` | {{setembro}} | `seh-TEH~-broo` |
| {{abril}} | `ah-BREE-oo` | {{outubro}} | `oh-TOO-broo` |
| {{maio}} | `MIE-oo` | {{novembro}} | `noh-VEH~-broo` |
| {{junho}} | `ZHOO-nyoo` | {{dezembro}} | `deh-ZEH~-broo` |

The months are not capitalized either. Use **em** with a month: {{em maio}}, {{em dezembro}}. The seasons: {{o verão}} `oo veh-ROW~` (summer), {{o outono}} `oo oh-TOH-noo` (autumn), {{o inverno}} `oo ee~-VEHR-noo` (winter), {{a primavera}} `ah pree-mah-VEH-rah` (spring). In Brazil, which is in the Southern Hemisphere, summer runs from December to March, and the New Year is celebrated in the heat. Say {{no verão}}, {{no inverno}}, {{na primavera}}.

### Saying the date

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| {{Que dia é hoje?}} | {{Hoje é dia treze de outubro.}} or {{Hoje é treze.}} |
| {{Qual é a data de hoje?}} | {{É treze de outubro de dois mil e vinte e seis.}} |
| {{Quando é o aniversário da Ana?}} | {{É dia cinco de março.}} |

The pattern is **cardinal number + {{de}} + month**: {{vinte e cinco de dezembro}}. The only exception is the first of the month, which uses the ordinal: {{primeiro de maio}} (not "um de maio"). In writing, Brazilians put the day first: **13/10/2026** is 13 October 2026, so 01/05 is the first of May, not January 5. The word for "birthday" is {{o aniversário}} `oo ah-nee-ver-SAH-ree-oo`.

## 6.9 Prices and money

The Brazilian currency is the {{real}} `heh-AH-oo` (plural {{reais}} `heh-IESS`), written R$. A hundredth is a {{centavo}} `seh~-TAH-voo`. Notice that Brazilians write the decimal comma: R$ 12,50.

| Written | Said |
|---|---|
| R$ 1,00 | {{um real}} |
| R$ 5,00 | {{cinco reais}} |
| R$ 12,50 | {{doze reais e cinquenta centavos}} (in a shop: {{doze e cinquenta}}) |
| R$ 125,00 | {{cento e vinte e cinco reais}} |
| R$ 1.200,00 | {{mil e duzentos reais}} |
| R$ 0,75 | {{setenta e cinco centavos}} |

### Asking the price and paying

| Portuguese | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{Quanto custa?}} | `KWAH~-too KOOS-tah` | How much does it cost? |
| {{Quanto é?}} | `KWAH~-too eh` | How much is it? |
| {{Quanto fica?}} | `KWAH~-too FEE-kah` | How much does it come to? |
| {{Quanto custam?}} | `KWAH~-too KOOS-tah~oo` | How much are they? |
| {{É caro. / É barato.}} | | It's expensive. / It's cheap. |
| {{Aceita cartão?}} | `ah-SAY-tah kar-TOW~` | Do you accept cards? |
| {{Posso pagar no cartão?}} | | Can I pay by card? |
| {{em dinheiro}} | `eh~ee djee-NYAY-roo` | in cash |
| {{no Pix}} | `noo PEEKS` | by Pix (the instant bank transfer used throughout Brazil) |
| {{Tem troco?}} | `TEH~ee TROH-koo` | Do you have change? |
| {{A conta, por favor.}} | `ah KOH~-tah, poor fah-VOR` | The check, please. |

Two verb notes. {{Custa}} is singular because it agrees with one thing, {{custam}} for several: {{Quanto custa o café?}}, {{Quanto custam os pães?}} And {{posso}} comes from {{poder}}, which you meet in Lesson 7: treat {{Posso pagar…?}} as a block for now.

::: atencao
Prices of food and drink are often given with a unit: {{o quilo}} `oo KEE-loo` (kilogram, as in {{vinte reais o quilo}}), {{a dúzia}} `ah DOO-zee-ah` (dozen), {{o litro}} `oo LEE-troo`, {{a unidade}}. Half a kilo is {{meio quilo}}. Note that "half" is {{meio}} before a masculine noun and {{meia}} before a feminine one: {{meio quilo}}, {{meia dúzia}}.
:::

## 6.10 Numbers in real situations: quantities, amounts and bargains

Numbers rarely travel alone. In a shop or on the street you hear them with measures, and with a few small words that are worth learning now.

| Portuguese | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{cada}} | `KAH-dah` | each, every |
| {{por}} | `poor` | per, for |
| {{o quilo}} | `oo KEE-loo` | the kilo |
| {{meio quilo}} | `MAY-oo KEE-loo` | half a kilo |
| {{a dúzia}} | `ah DOO-zee-ah` | the dozen |
| {{o litro}} | `oo LEE-troo` | the liter |
| {{o desconto}} | `oo djees-KOH~-too` | the discount |
| {{o total}} | `oo toh-TAH-oo` | the total |

Here is a short exchange at a fruit stand. Read it aloud and replace the numbers with your own.

> {{Quanto custa o quilo da manga?}} How much is a kilo of mango?
> {{Seis reais o quilo. Três quilos por quinze.}} Six reais a kilo. Three kilos for fifteen.
> {{Então quero três quilos. E uma dúzia de laranjas.}} Then I'll take three kilos. And a dozen oranges.
> {{Dá vinte e dois reais no total.}} That makes twenty-two reais in total.

Three small patterns to notice. First, prices with a unit use the article, never "per": {{seis reais o quilo}}. Second, the preposition {{por}} carries the meaning of "for" in offers: {{três por dez reais}}. Third, you hear {{dá}} (from {{dar}}) in the sense of "it comes to": {{Dá trinta reais}}. It has the same use as {{fica}} in Lesson 3.

**Round numbers and estimates.** When you do not know the exact number, Brazilians say {{uns}} or {{cerca de}} before it: {{uns vinte minutos}} (about twenty minutes), {{cerca de cem pessoas}} (around a hundred people). And when you want to say "a few", use {{alguns}} or {{poucos}} in front of the noun. These give your numbers a natural sound.

**Fractions and percentages.** {{Metade}} `meh-TAH-djee` is the word for "half" as a noun: {{a metade do preço}}. A percentage reads as a number plus {{por cento}} `poor SEH~-too`: {{dez por cento}} (10%), {{cinquenta por cento}} (50%). In a shop you will see {{desconto de vinte por cento}}.

## 6.11 Read a real text

Read the text aloud, first with the guide, then without it.

> {{O café da Rua das Flores abre às sete da manhã e fecha às nove da noite, de segunda a sábado. No domingo, abre ao meio-dia. Um café custa quatro reais e cinquenta centavos. Um pão de queijo custa seis reais.}}

Guide: `oo kah-FEH dah HOO-ah dahss FLOH-reess AH-bree ahss SEH-tchee dah mah-NYAH~ ee FEH-shah ahss NAW-vee dah NOY-tchee, djee seh-GOO~-dah ah SAH-bah-doo. noo doh-MEE~-goo, AH-bree ow MAY-oo DJEE-ah. oo~ kah-FEH KOOS-tah KWAH-troo heh-IESS ee see~-KWEH~-tah seh~-TAH-vooss. oo~ POW~ djee KAY-zhoo KOOS-tah SAYSS heh-IESS.`

Translation: *The café on Flowers Street opens at seven in the morning and closes at nine at night, from Monday to Saturday. On Sunday, it opens at noon. A coffee costs four reais fifty. A cheese bread costs six reais.*

Check the details: {{às sete}} and {{às nove}} (plural hours), {{ao meio-dia}} (the fusion of {{a}} + {{o}}), {{de segunda a sábado}} (from Monday to Saturday), and the price said in full words. The verbs {{abre}}, {{fecha}} and {{custa}} are third-person singular forms you already know from Lesson 4.

## 6.15 Pocket summary: Lesson 6

::: resumo
- **0–20:** {{zero, um/uma, dois/duas, três, quatro, cinco, seis, sete, oito, nove, dez, onze, doze, treze, catorze, quinze, dezesseis, dezessete, dezoito, dezenove, vinte}}.
- **Tens:** {{vinte, trinta, quarenta, cinquenta, sessenta, setenta, oitenta, noventa}}; join with **e**: {{trinta e cinco}}.
- **Hundreds:** {{cem}} (exactly 100), {{cento e…}}; {{duzentos/-as}} up to {{novecentos/-as}}; they agree with the noun. {{Mil}}, never {{um mil}}; {{um milhão de}}.
- **Time:** {{É uma hora}} / {{São duas horas}}; {{e meia}}, {{e quinze}}, {{quinze para as…}}; {{meio-dia}}, {{meia-noite}}. At a time: {{à uma}}, {{às duas}}, {{ao meio-dia}}.
- **Dates:** {{Hoje é dia treze de outubro}}; first of the month is {{primeiro}}; day first in writing (13/10).
- **Days and months:** lowercase; {{na segunda}}, {{em maio}}; weekdays Monday to Friday are feminine.
- **Prices:** {{Quanto custa?}} {{Quanto é?}} R$ 12,50 = {{doze reais e cinquenta centavos}}; decimal comma, thousands dot.
- **Phone numbers:** digit by digit; {{meia}} for 6.
:::
