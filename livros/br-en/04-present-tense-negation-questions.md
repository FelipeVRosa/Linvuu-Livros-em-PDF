# The present tense, negation and questions: say what you do

By the end of this lesson you can conjugate regular verbs in -ar, -er and -ir, talk about your habits and your daily life, say what you do not do, answer yes-or-no questions, and ask for information with {{quem}}, {{o que}}, {{onde}}, {{quando}}, {{como}}, {{por que}} and {{quanto}}.

## 4.1 A scene you will live

It is breakfast at a hostel in Salvador. Jess, an Australian traveler, sits with her coffee at the long table. Rafael, who works in the neighborhood and drops by every morning, starts a conversation.

::: dialogo
**Rafael:** {{Bom dia! Você mora aqui em Salvador?}}
**Jess:** {{Não, não moro. Moro em Sydney, mas viajo muito. E você? Trabalha onde?}}
**Rafael:** {{Trabalho num banco. Mas no fim de semana eu ensino capoeira.}}
**Jess:** {{Que legal! Eu não falo muito bem, mas estudo português todo dia.}}
**Rafael:** {{Você fala muito bem! Quer um café? A gente bebe café aqui o dia inteiro.}}
**Jess:** {{Quero, sim. Eu adoro café. Quanto custa?}}
**Rafael:** {{Nada. É de graça.}}
:::

*Rafael: Good morning! Do you live here in Salvador? / Jess: No, I don't. I live in Sydney, but I travel a lot. And you? Where do you work? / Rafael: I work at a bank. But on weekends I teach capoeira. / Jess: How cool! I don't speak very well, but I study Portuguese every day. / Rafael: You speak very well! Would you like a coffee? We drink coffee here all day long. / Jess: Yes, please. I love coffee. How much is it? / Rafael: Nothing. It's free.*

Look at the verbs: {{mora}}, {{moro}}, {{viajo}}, {{trabalha}}, {{trabalho}}, {{ensino}}, {{falo}}, {{estudo}}, {{fala}}, {{bebe}}, {{adoro}}. Each one is different from the infinitive ({{morar}}, {{trabalhar}}, {{falar}}), and each ending tells you who is speaking. Notice, too, what is missing: Rafael never says "do" or "does". {{Você mora aqui?}} is a statement with a question mark and a rising voice. And Jess says {{não moro}} with a single {{não}} in front of the verb. That is the whole negative.

| Word | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{morar}} | `moh-RAR` | to live (in a place) |
| {{viajar}} | `vee-ah-ZHAR` | to travel |
| {{ensinar}} | `eh~-see-NAR` | to teach |
| {{capoeira}} | `kah-poh-AY-rah` | capoeira (a Brazilian martial art and dance) |
| {{o fim de semana}} | `oo FEE~ djee seh-MAH-nah` | the weekend |
| {{beber}} | `beh-BEHR` | to drink |
| {{de graça}} | `djee GRAH-sah` | free, for nothing |

## 4.2 The trap: English hides the person, Portuguese shows it

English verbs hardly change: *I work, you work, we work, they work*, and only *he works* adds an -s. English also needs a helper for questions and negatives: *Do you work? I do not work.* Portuguese does the opposite.

1. **The verb changes with the person**: {{eu trabalho}}, {{ele trabalha}}, {{nós trabalhamos}}, {{eles trabalham}}.
2. **There is no *do* or *does*.** The question is the statement with a rising voice, and the negative is the statement with {{não}} in front of the verb.
3. **One present for many English tenses.** {{Moro em Salvador}} translates *I live in Salvador*, but {{Eu falo português todo dia}} is *I speak Portuguese every day*. Later you will meet {{estou falando}} (*I am speaking*), but the simple present also serves for what is happening around now and even for the near future: {{Amanhã eu viajo}} (I leave tomorrow).

## 4.3 How a verb is built

Every Portuguese verb has an **infinitive**, the "to ___" form, and it always ends in **-ar**, **-er** or **-ir**. Remove the ending and you have the **stem**. Then add the endings of the present tense.

::: passo 1 Find the group
Look at the last two letters of the infinitive. {{Falar}} (to speak) is an **-ar** verb; {{comer}} (to eat) is an **-er** verb; {{abrir}} (to open) is an **-ir** verb. Most Portuguese verbs are -ar verbs, so that is the group to learn first.
:::

::: passo 2 Remove the ending
{{Falar}} → **fal-**. {{Comer}} → **com-**. {{Abrir}} → **abr-**.
:::

::: passo 3 Add the ending that matches the person
There are only **four** forms you need, because {{você}}, {{ele}}, {{ela}} and {{a gente}} share one, and {{vocês}}, {{eles}} and {{elas}} share another.

| Person | -ar: falar | -er: comer | -ir: abrir |
|---|---|---|---|
| eu | {{falo}} `FAH-loo` | {{como}} `KOH-moo` | {{abro}} `AH-broo` |
| você / ele / ela / a gente | {{fala}} `FAH-lah` | {{come}} `KOH-mee` | {{abre}} `AH-bree` |
| nós | {{falamos}} `fah-LAH-mooss` | {{comemos}} `koh-MEH-mooss` | {{abrimos}} `ah-BREE-mooss` |
| vocês / eles / elas | {{falam}} `FAH-lah~oo` | {{comem}} `KOH-meh~ee` | {{abrem}} `AH-breh~ee` |
:::

Learn the endings, not the three verbs:

| | -ar | -er | -ir |
|---|---|---|---|
| eu | -o | -o | -o |
| você / ele / ela / a gente | -a | -e | -e |
| nós | -amos | -emos | -imos |
| vocês / eles / elas | -am | -em | -em |

Look at the table: the -er and -ir verbs differ in only one place, the {{nós}} form (**-emos** against **-imos**). Everything else is the same. And the {{eu}} form always ends in **-o**.

::: dica
**The pronoun is optional, the ending is not.** {{Falo}}, {{fala}} and {{falam}} work alone because the ending tells you the person. But {{fala}} could be {{você}}, {{ele}}, {{ela}} or {{a gente}}, so keep the pronoun or name when it is not clear from context: {{Você fala inglês?}}, {{Ela fala inglês}}.
:::

### The stress moves in the nós form

The stress sits on the stem in three of the four forms: {{FA-lo}}, {{FA-la}}, {{FA-lam}}. In the {{nós}} form it moves to the ending: {{fa-LA-mos}}. Say it aloud: {{falo}} `FAH-loo`, but {{falamos}} `fah-LAH-mooss`. This needs no new rule. Lesson 1 says that words ending in a vowel or in **-s** are stressed on the second-to-last syllable. In {{falo}} that syllable is the stem; in {{falamos}} the extra syllable **-mos** pushes the stress onto the ending.

The third-person plural ending **-am** is pronounced `ah~oo`, and **-em** is `eh~ee`. Do not say "AM" as in *ham*. Compare {{ele fala}} `EH-lee FAH-lah` and {{eles falam}} `EH-leess FAH-lah~oo`: in speech, the difference between singular and plural is precisely that final nasal sound, so make it clear.

### What about tu?

If you travel in the South or Northeast, you may hear {{tu}} forms: {{tu falas}}, {{tu comes}}, {{tu abres}}, ending in **-s**; or {{tu fala}}, {{tu come}}, with the {{ele}} form. You can safely stay with {{você}}.

Two verbs from the scene work with a small word after them. **{{Gostar}}** (to like) and **{{precisar}}** (to need) take **de**: {{Gosto de café}}, {{Preciso de ajuda}} (I need help). Other verbs, like {{esperar}} (to wait), {{pagar}} (to pay) and {{ajudar}} (to help), take no preposition in Portuguese: {{Espero o ônibus}}, {{Pago a conta}}, {{Ajudo o Pedro}}. You will meet more of these in Lesson 8.

Two more useful ones, with a meaning you cannot guess: {{tomar café}} `toh-MAR kah-FEH` (to have coffee; also *to drink*, *to take*) and {{pegar o ônibus}} `peh-GAR oo OH-nee-booss` (to catch the bus). Brazilians say {{tomar}} for drinks and medicines, {{pegar}} for transport, and {{comer}} for food.

### Small spelling facts about the *eu* form

In the present tense a regular verb does not change its spelling except in a few cases. Two you will see early:

- Verbs ending in **-ger** or **-gir** change the **g** to **j** in the {{eu}} form, to keep the *zh* sound: {{dirigir}} (to drive) → {{dirijo}}, {{proteger}} → {{protejo}}.
- Verbs ending in **-cer** change **c** to **ç**: {{conhecer}} → {{conheço}}. (This verb comes in Lesson 7.)

## 4.5 Using the present tense

The present tense of the Brazilian speaker covers more than the English one.

| Use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| a fact or a state | {{Moro em Salvador.}} | I live in Salvador. |
| a habit | {{Estudo português todo dia.}} | I study Portuguese every day. |
| a routine of a group | {{A gente bebe café o dia inteiro.}} | We drink coffee all day. |
| something planned | {{Amanhã eu viajo.}} | I'm leaving tomorrow. |
| a general truth | {{O sol nasce no leste.}} | The sun rises in the east. |

For frequency, the usual words go in front of the verb:

| Word | Guide | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| {{sempre}} | `SEH~-pree` | always | {{Sempre bebo café de manhã.}} |
| {{normalmente}} | `nor-mah-oo-MEH~-tchee` | normally | {{Normalmente chego cedo.}} |
| {{às vezes}} | `ahss VEH-zeess` | sometimes | {{Às vezes como fora.}} |
| {{nunca}} | `NOO~-kah` | never | {{Nunca bebo café à noite.}} |
| {{todo dia}} | `TOH-doo DJEE-ah` | every day | {{Estudo todo dia.}} |
| {{de manhã}} | `djee mah-NYAH~` | in the morning | {{De manhã trabalho em casa.}} |

({{Cedo}} `SEH-doo` means *early*.) {{Todo dia}} and {{todos os dias}} are the same, with {{todo dia}} the more natural in speech.

## 4.6 Making a negative: não

To say what you do not do, put **{{não}}** in front of the verb. Nothing else changes. There is no *do* or *does*.

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| {{Eu moro em Salvador.}} | {{Eu não moro em Salvador.}} |
| {{Ele fala alemão.}} | {{Ele não fala alemão.}} |
| {{Nós comemos carne.}} | {{Nós não comemos carne.}} |

When you answer a yes-or-no question, Brazilians often repeat the verb, with or without {{sim}}, instead of giving a bare *yes*:

| Question | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|
| {{Você mora aqui?}} | {{Moro, sim.}} | {{Não, não moro.}} |
| {{Ela fala inglês?}} | {{Fala, sim.}} | {{Não, não fala.}} |
| {{Vocês bebem café?}} | {{Bebemos, sim.}} | {{Não, não bebemos.}} |

Note that "no" appears twice: the answer {{não}}, and then the {{não}} that belongs to the verb. A common reply is just {{Não}} or {{Não moro}}.

### Negative words: Portuguese is fine with several

English avoids the double negative ("I don't know nothing"). Portuguese needs it. When the negative word **follows** the verb, the verb also gets {{não}}.

| Word | Guide | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| {{nada}} | `NAH-dah` | nothing | {{Não como nada.}} (I don't eat anything.) |
| {{ninguém}} | `nee~-GEH~ee` | nobody | {{Não conheço ninguém aqui.}} |
| {{nunca}} | `NOO~-kah` | never | {{Nunca como carne.}} or {{Não como carne nunca.}} |
| {{nenhum / nenhuma}} | `neh-NYOO~` | no, not one | {{Não tenho nenhum problema.}} |

When the negative word comes **before** the verb, you do not add {{não}}: {{Nunca como carne}}, {{Ninguém sabe}}, {{Nada funciona}}. Both patterns are correct and natural. Choose one and use it every time.

## 4.7 Asking questions

### Yes-or-no questions

In Portuguese, a yes-or-no question has the **same word order** as the statement. The only difference is the melody: your voice rises at the end.

| Statement (voice falls) | Question (voice rises) |
|---|---|
| {{Você mora aqui.}} | {{Você mora aqui?}} |
| {{Ela trabalha num banco.}} | {{Ela trabalha num banco?}} |
| {{Vocês falam inglês.}} | {{Vocês falam inglês?}} |

Brazilians often add a confirming word at the end: {{né?}} `neh` (right? isn't it?), a short form of {{não é?}}: {{Você mora aqui, né?}} It is a very natural way to ask for agreement, and it is one of the small words that make you sound Brazilian. You can also give two options with {{ou}} (or): {{Você quer café ou chá?}}

### Question words

| Word | Guide | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| {{quem}} | `KEH~ee` | who | {{Quem fala português aqui?}} |
| {{o que}} | `oo KEE` | what | {{O que você faz?}} |
| {{onde}} | `OH~-djee` | where | {{Onde você mora?}} |
| {{quando}} | `KWAH~-doo` | when | {{Quando você viaja?}} |
| {{como}} | `KOH-moo` | how | {{Como você vai para o trabalho?}} |
| {{por que}} | `poor KEE` | why | {{Por que você estuda português?}} |
| {{qual / quais}} | `KWAH-oo` / `KWIESS` | which | {{Qual é a sua cidade?}} |
| {{quanto / quanta}} | `KWAH~-too` / `KWAH~-tah` | how much | {{Quanto custa o café?}} |
| {{quantos / quantas}} | `KWAH~-tooss` / `KWAH~-tahss` | how many | {{Quantos anos você tem?}} |

::: passo 1 Put the question word first
In Portuguese the question word begins the sentence, and the rest follows in statement order: {{Onde você mora?}}, not "Onde mora você?" (although that also exists in writing). In casual speech you will often hear the word at the end instead: {{Você mora onde?}} It is correct, informal, and very common.
:::

::: passo 2 Use "quem" with a singular verb
With ordinary verbs, {{quem}} takes the {{ele/ela}} form, even when you expect several people: {{Quem fala português aqui?}} (Who here speaks Portuguese?).
:::

::: passo 3 Add "é que" when you want to sound natural
Brazilians often insert {{é que}} after the question word: {{Onde é que você mora?}} `OH~-djee eh kee voh-SEH MOH-rah`. It adds a little emphasis, like *where on earth do you live?*, and appears in everyday talk. You do not need to use it, but you will hear it.
:::

::: passo 4 Keep the four "why" words apart
There are four spellings, and Brazilian schools teach them separately. At the start or middle of a question: {{por que}} (two words): {{Por que você estuda?}} At the end of a sentence: {{por quê}}: {{Você estuda? Por quê?}} In an answer: {{porque}} (one word): {{Porque gosto da língua.}} The noun "the reason" is {{o porquê}}. You need only the first and third forms for now.
:::

::: atencao
{{Qual}} and {{o que}} both translate *what* in some English sentences. Use {{qual}} when you choose among possibilities: {{Qual é o seu nome?}}, {{Qual é a sua cidade?}} Use {{o que}} for open questions with a verb: {{O que você faz?}}, {{O que é isso?}}
:::

## 4.8 The irregular verbs you already need

A few of the most frequent verbs in Portuguese are irregular in the present. You have met {{ser}} (Lesson 2) and a few in the scenes. Here is a first look at six more; each form is given with a guide. Lessons 5 and 7 teach you to use them.

| | ter (to have) | ir (to go) | fazer (to do, to make) | ver (to see) | dar (to give) | estar (to be) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| eu | {{tenho}} `TEH-nyoo` | {{vou}} `VOH` | {{faço}} `FAH-soo` | {{vejo}} `VEH-zhoo` | {{dou}} `DOH` | {{estou}} `ehs-TOH` |
| você / ele / ela / a gente | {{tem}} `TEH~ee` | {{vai}} `vie` | {{faz}} `fahss` | {{vê}} `veh` | {{dá}} `dah` | {{está}} `ehs-TAH` |
| nós | {{temos}} `TEH-mooss` | {{vamos}} `VAH-mooss` | {{fazemos}} `fah-ZEH-mooss` | {{vemos}} `VEH-mooss` | {{damos}} `DAH-mooss` | {{estamos}} `ehs-TAH-mooss` |
| vocês / eles / elas | {{têm}} `TEH~ee` | {{vão}} `VOW~` | {{fazem}} `FAH-zeh~ee` | {{veem}} `VEH-eh~ee` | {{dão}} `DOW~` | {{estão}} `ehs-TOW~` |

Look at the pairs {{tem}} and {{têm}}. They sound the same, and the circumflex only tells you in writing that the second is plural: {{Ele tem}}, {{Eles têm}}. The same system works for {{vem}} (he comes) and {{vêm}} (they come) in Lesson 7. And remember that {{veem}} (they see) lost its circumflex after the 2009 spelling reform.

You already use these verbs in the blocks you have learned: {{Tenho trinta anos}}, {{O que você faz?}}, {{Vamos?}} Learn the full picture in the next lessons; for now, notice only that the {{nós}} form always ends in **-mos** and the {{eles}} form in **-m** or a nasal **-ão**.

## 4.9 A mini-interview: ask, answer, return the question

Real conversation moves in a loop: someone asks, you answer, you ask back. The little word that closes the loop is {{e você?}} (and you?). Read this short interview aloud, then cover the right-hand column and answer for yourself.

| Question | Possible answer |
|---|---|
| {{Você mora aqui?}} | {{Moro, sim. E você?}} |
| {{Onde você trabalha?}} | {{Trabalho num hospital. E você?}} |
| {{Você fala outras línguas?}} | {{Falo inglês e um pouco de espanhol.}} |
| {{O que você faz no fim de semana?}} | {{Assisto a filmes e encontro os amigos.}} |
| {{Você come carne?}} | {{Não, não como. Sou vegetariano.}} |
| {{Quando você estuda?}} | {{Estudo de manhã, antes do trabalho.}} |

Two things to notice. First, the verb in the answer repeats the verb in the question, but its person changes: {{Você mora}} becomes {{Moro}}. Second, English speakers tend to answer with a full sentence, "I live here". In Portuguese, the short answer is more natural: {{Moro}}, {{Trabalho num hospital}}. The pronoun {{eu}} appears only for contrast: {{Eu moro aqui, mas ela mora em Recife}}.

New words in the table: {{outro / outra}} `OH-troo` (other), {{encontrar}} `eh~-koh~-TRAR` (to meet, to find), {{o filme}} `oo FEE-oo-mee` (the film), {{antes de}} `AH~-tcheess djee` (before), {{vegetariano}} `veh-zheh-tah-ree-AH-noo`, {{o hospital}} `oo ohs-pee-TAH-oo`.

## 4.10 Read a real text

Read the text aloud, first with the guide and then without it.

> {{Eu sou o Rafael. Moro em Salvador e trabalho num banco. De manhã, tomo café e pego o ônibus. No almoço, como arroz e feijão com os colegas. À noite, assisto a uma série. Aos sábados, ensino capoeira.}}

Guide: `EH-oo SOH oo hah-fah-EH-oo. MOH-roo eh~ee sah-oo-vah-DOR ee trah-BAH-lyoo noo~ BAH~-koo. djee mah-NYAH~, TOH-moo kah-FEH ee PEH-goo oo OH-nee-booss. noo ah-oo-MOH-soo, KOH-moo ah-HOSS ee fay-ZHOW~ koh~ ooss koh-LEH-gahss. ah NOY-tchee, ah-SEES-too ah OO-mah SEH-ree-ee. ah-ooss SAH-bah-dooss, eh~-SEE-noo kah-poh-AY-rah.`

Translation: *I am Rafael. I live in Salvador and work at a bank. In the morning, I have coffee and catch the bus. At lunch, I eat rice and beans with my colleagues. At night, I watch a series. On Saturdays, I teach capoeira.*

Check the verbs: {{moro}}, {{trabalho}}, {{tomo}}, {{pego}}, {{como}}, {{assisto}}, {{ensino}}. All are {{eu}} forms in **-o**. Now rewrite the text about someone else, changing every verb to the {{ele}} form: {{Ele mora em Salvador e trabalha num banco…}}

## 4.14 Pocket summary: Lesson 4

::: resumo
- **Three groups:** -ar, -er, -ir. Take off the ending and add: **eu -o**; **você/ele/ela/a gente -a** (-ar) or **-e** (-er, -ir); **nós -amos / -emos / -imos**; **vocês/eles/elas -am** (-ar) or **-em** (-er, -ir).
- **Stress:** on the stem ({{FAlo}}, {{FAla}}, {{FAlam}}) except in {{nós}}: {{faLAmos}}.
- **Pronouns are optional**; keep them with {{é}}, {{fala}} and any ambiguous form.
- **Negative:** {{não}} + verb. Negative words after the verb need {{não}} before it: {{Não como nada}}. Before the verb, they stand alone: {{Nunca como carne}}.
- **Questions:** same order as the statement, voice up. Question word first: {{quem, o que, onde, quando, como, por que, qual, quanto}}. In speech: {{né?}} and {{é que}}.
- **Verbs with de:** {{gostar de}}, {{precisar de}}.
- **First irregulars:** {{ter, ir, fazer, ver, dar, estar}}, with {{eu tenho / vou / faço / vejo / dou / estou}}.
- **Spelling:** {{dirigir → dirijo}}, {{conhecer → conheço}}; {{têm}} (plural) and {{tem}} (singular) sound alike.
:::
