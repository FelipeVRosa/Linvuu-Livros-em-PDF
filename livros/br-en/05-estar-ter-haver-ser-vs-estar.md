# Estar, ter, haver: ser or estar, and "there is"

By the end of this lesson you can say where things and people are, describe how you feel right now, say what you have and what exists ("there is"), choose between {{ser}} and {{estar}} with confidence, and use the contractions {{ao}}, {{à}}, {{daqui}}, {{pra}} and {{pro}} in everyday speech.

## 5.1 A scene you will live

Laura has just arrived in Rio after a long flight. Marcos, who rents her the apartment, meets her at the door.

::: dialogo
**Laura:** {{Oi! Você é o Marcos?}}
**Marcos:** {{Sou eu! Bem-vinda! Você está cansada da viagem?}}
**Laura:** {{Um pouco. E estou com fome. Tem algum restaurante por aqui?}}
**Marcos:** {{Tem, sim. Tem uma pizzaria na esquina e um restaurante japonês em frente à praça.}}
**Laura:** {{Ótimo. E o banheiro? Onde fica?}}
**Marcos:** {{Está ali, ao lado da cozinha. A internet tem senha. Está na parede, perto da porta.}}
**Laura:** {{Perfeito. Muito obrigada!}}
:::

*Laura: Hi! Are you Marcos? / Marcos: That's me! Welcome! Are you tired from the trip? / Laura: A little. And I'm hungry. Is there a restaurant around here? / Marcos: Yes, there is. There's a pizzeria on the corner and a Japanese restaurant across from the square. / Laura: Great. And the bathroom? Where is it? / Marcos: It's right there, next to the kitchen. The internet has a password. It's on the wall, near the door. / Laura: Perfect. Thank you very much!*

Count the "to be" verbs: {{é}} ("Você é o Marcos?"), {{está}} ("Você está cansada"), {{estou}} ("Estou com fome"), {{fica}} (where the bathroom is), {{está}} again (it is there). Two verbs, {{ser}} and {{estar}}, where English uses only "to be". And there are two surprises: {{tem}} means *there is* in the question {{Tem algum restaurante?}}, and the same {{tem}} means *has* in {{A internet tem senha}}.

| Word | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{bem-vindo / bem-vinda}} | `beh~ee-VEE~-doo` | welcome |
| {{cansado / cansada}} | `kah~-SAH-doo` | tired |
| {{a fome}} | `ah FOH-mee` | hunger |
| {{algum}} | `ow-GOO~` | any, some |
| {{o restaurante}} | `oo heh-stow-RAH~-tchee` | the restaurant |
| {{a esquina}} | `ah ehs-KEE-nah` | the corner |
| {{a praça}} | `ah PRAH-sah` | the square |
| {{em frente a}} | `eh~ee FREH~-tchee ah` | in front of, across from |
| {{ao lado de}} | `ow LAH-doo djee` | next to |
| {{perto de}} | `PEHR-too djee` | near |
| {{a senha}} | `ah SEH-nyah` | the password |
| {{a parede}} | `ah pah-REH-djee` | the wall |

## 5.2 The trap: one "to be" in English, two in Portuguese

English says *I am Laura*, *I am tired*, *I am in Rio* and *I am twenty* with the same verb. Portuguese uses **different verbs**:

| English | Portuguese | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| I am Laura. | {{Eu sou a Laura.}} | {{ser}} |
| I am tired. | {{Estou cansada.}} | {{estar}} |
| I am in Rio. | {{Estou no Rio.}} | {{estar}} |
| I am twenty. | {{Tenho vinte anos.}} | {{ter}} |
| I am hungry. | {{Estou com fome.}} / {{Tenho fome.}} | {{estar}} / {{ter}} |
| There is a bank. | {{Tem um banco.}} | {{ter}} |

So the lesson has three traps in a row. First, English "to be" splits into **{{ser}} and {{estar}}**. Second, a lot of states that English says with "to be" (*I am hungry, cold, afraid*) use **{{ter}}** or **{{estar com}}** in Portuguese. Third, English "there is" uses no "be" verb in Brazilian speech: Brazilians say **{{tem}}**, literally "has".

## 5.3 Estar: how you are, where you are

You met the forms of {{estar}} in Lesson 4. Here they are again, with the informal forms that Brazilians really use.

| Person | Standard | Guide | Spoken |
|---|---|---|---|
| eu | {{estou}} | `ehs-TOH` | {{tô}} `toh` |
| você / ele / ela / a gente | {{está}} | `ehs-TAH` | {{tá}} `tah` |
| nós | {{estamos}} | `ehs-TAH-mooss` | {{(a gente) tá}} |
| vocês / eles / elas | {{estão}} | `ehs-TOW~` | {{tão}} `TOW~` |

In speech and in text messages, {{estou}} becomes {{tô}} and {{está}} becomes {{tá}}: {{Tô cansada}}, {{Tá bom}}, {{Tá frio}}. Both are normal in conversation and in chats; use {{estou}} and {{está}} when you write formally or want to be careful. The word {{tá}} alone also means *okay*: {{Tá}}, {{tá bom}}, {{tá certo}}.

**Estar** is the verb for **a situation that can change**: where someone or something is right now, and how someone feels or looks right now. It has three main uses.

::: passo 1 Use estar for where something or someone is
{{Estou no hotel.}} {{Os meus pais estão em Lisboa.}} {{A chave está na mesa.}} The place can be anywhere: even if you live in Rio for ten years, you say {{Estou no Rio}}.
:::

::: passo 2 Use estar for how someone is now
This is the "state of the moment": feelings, health, tiredness, mood. {{Estou cansada.}} {{Ele está doente.}} {{Elas estão felizes.}} {{O café está quente.}} (The coffee is hot, right now.)
:::

::: passo 3 Use estar before "com" for physical sensations
{{Estou com fome}} (I'm hungry), {{Estou com sede}} `SEH-djee` (thirsty), {{Estou com sono}} `SOH-noo` (sleepy), {{Estou com frio}} (cold), {{Estou com calor}} (hot), {{Estou com pressa}} `PREH-sah` (in a hurry), {{Estou com medo}} `MEH-doo` (afraid). Brazilians say it all day.
:::

You will also use {{estar}} to form the progressive, as in {{Estou estudando}} (I am studying). That belongs to Lesson 9. For now, memorize only that {{estar}} is the verb of "right now".

::: dica
The question {{Como você está?}} `KOH-moo voh-SEH ehs-TAH` means *How are you (right now)?* The standard answers: {{Estou bem, obrigado}} / {{obrigada}}, {{Tô bem, e você?}}, {{Mais ou menos}} `MIES oh MEH-nooss` (so-so), {{Estou cansado}}. {{Tudo bem?}} from Lesson 1 is the casual version.
:::

## 5.4 Saying where things are

Place is where learners most often get stuck. These are the words you need.

| Word | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{aqui}} | `ah-KEE` | here |
| {{aí}} | `ah-EE` | there (near you) |
| {{ali}} | `ah-LEE` | over there |
| {{lá}} | `lah` | there (far) |
| {{perto de}} | `PEHR-too djee` | near |
| {{longe de}} | `LOH~-zhee djee` | far from |
| {{ao lado de}} | `ow LAH-doo djee` | next to |
| {{em frente a / de}} | `eh~ee FREH~-tchee ah` | opposite, across from |
| {{atrás de}} | `ah-TRAHSS djee` | behind |
| {{entre}} | `EH~-tree` | between |
| {{em cima de}} | `eh~ee SEE-mah djee` | on top of |
| {{embaixo de}} | `eh~-BIE-shoo djee` | under |
| {{dentro de}} | `DEH~-troo djee` | inside |
| {{fora de}} | `FAW-rah djee` | outside |
| {{na esquina}} | `nah ehs-KEE-nah` | on the corner |
| {{no centro}} | `noo SEH~-troo` | downtown |

These words end in **de** or **a**, so they fuse with an article following them: {{perto do hotel}}, {{ao lado da farmácia}}, {{em frente ao banco}}, {{atrás da loja}}, {{longe do centro}}. The contractions are the ones from Lesson 3; the only new ones are **ao** and **à**, which you will see in 5.8.

Now you have two verbs for "is located", and a third is the one Brazilians use most.

| Verb | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| {{estar}} | a person or thing in a place now | {{A Marina está no trabalho.}} |
| {{ficar}} | a fixed place: a building, a city, a street | {{O banco fica na rua principal.}} |
| {{ser}} | the place of an event | {{A festa é na casa da Júlia.}} |

Look at the contrast between the first two. People move, so {{estar}} fits: {{Onde está o Pedro?}} (Where is Pedro?). Buildings do not move, so Brazilians say {{ficar}}: {{Onde fica o banco?}} (Where is the bank?). You can say {{Onde está o banco?}} too, and it is understood, but {{fica}} is the natural question. The third verb is the surprise: when you say where an **event** happens (a party, a class, a meeting, a wedding), use {{ser}}: {{A reunião é na sala dois}}, {{O casamento é na igreja}}. Think of an event as something that **is**, not something that is *located*.

::: comparacao
| English | Portuguese |
|---|---|
| Where is the bank? | {{Onde fica o banco?}} (fixed place) |
| Where is Pedro? | {{Onde está o Pedro?}} (a person, now) |
| Where is the party? | {{Onde é a festa?}} (an event) |
:::

## 5.5 Ter: to have, and much more

{{Ter}} is irregular in the present tense (it appeared in Lesson 4), and it is the second most useful verb in the language after {{ser}}.

| Person | Form | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| eu | {{tenho}} | `TEH-nyoo` | I have |
| você / ele / ela / a gente | {{tem}} | `TEH~ee` | you have / he has / she has / we have |
| nós | {{temos}} | `TEH-mooss` | we have |
| vocês / eles / elas | {{têm}} | `TEH~ee` | you have / they have |

Four uses to know right away:

1. **Possession and family:** {{Tenho um carro}}, {{Ela tem dois irmãos}}, {{Você tem filhos?}}
2. **Age:** {{Tenho trinta anos}} (Lesson 2).
3. **Feelings and sensations:** {{Tenho fome}}, {{Tenho sede}}, {{Tenho sono}}, {{Tenho frio}}, {{Tenho calor}}, {{Tenho medo}}, {{Tenho pressa}}. These are the same as {{estar com}}. {{Tenho fome}} and {{Estou com fome}} are both correct, and Brazilians use both all the time.
4. **"There is" and "there are":** see the next section.

::: atencao
The plural {{têm}} sounds exactly like the singular {{tem}}. The circumflex only appears in writing. In speech you hear the context: {{Ele tem}} and {{Eles têm}} sound the same.
:::

## 5.6 "There is": tem, há and existir

English "there is" and "there are" have no subject in Portuguese. Brazilians say **{{tem}}** in everyday speech. It is invariable: singular or plural, the verb does not change.

| Portuguese | Meaning |
|---|---|
| {{Tem um banco aqui perto?}} | Is there a bank near here? |
| {{Tem, sim.}} / {{Não tem.}} | Yes, there is. / No, there isn't. |
| {{Tem muita gente na praia.}} | There are a lot of people on the beach. |
| {{Tem dois restaurantes na rua.}} | There are two restaurants on the street. |
| {{Não tem táxi.}} | There are no taxis. |

You will also meet **{{há}}** `ah`, the same idea in a more formal or written style: {{Há muitos turistas na cidade}}. And **{{existir}}** `eh-zees-TCHEER` is used mostly for "exists" in a formal sense: {{Existe uma solução}}. A learner can use {{tem}} everywhere in conversation and {{há}} in writing.

There is a small trap: because {{tem}} also means "he has", the plural {{têm}} must not be used for "there are". Say {{Tem muitos carros na rua}}, never {{Têm muitos carros}}. When the speaker means *they have*, the plural is used: {{Os brasileiros têm muitos amigos}}.

{{Há}} has a second job: it marks **time that has passed**: {{Moro aqui há dois anos}} (I have lived here for two years). You will see this again with the numbers of Lesson 6.

## 5.7 Ser × estar: the great contrast

Now that you have both verbs, the central rule is this one.

**{{Ser}} says what something *is*: its identity, its nature, where it comes from.**
**{{Estar}} says how something *is right now*: its situation, its state, where it is.**

| | {{Ser}} | {{Estar}} |
|---|---|---|
| identity, name | {{Sou a Laura.}} | |
| nationality, origin | {{Sou do Brasil.}} | |
| profession | {{Ela é médica.}} | |
| permanent qualities | {{A casa é grande.}} | |
| time, dates | {{É uma hora.}} | |
| event location | {{A festa é na casa da Ana.}} | |
| location of a person or object | | {{Ela está em casa.}} |
| feeling, health, mood | | {{Ele está cansado.}} |
| temporary condition | | {{O café está frio.}} |
| progressive (Lesson 9) | | {{Estou estudando.}} |

Some adjectives work with **both** verbs, and the meaning changes. This is where you really learn the difference.

| With {{ser}} | With {{estar}} |
|---|---|
| {{Ele é chato.}} `SHAH-too` He is a boring or annoying person. | {{Ele está chato hoje.}} He is being annoying today. |
| {{Ela é bonita.}} She is pretty. | {{Ela está bonita hoje.}} She looks pretty today (she dressed up). |
| {{Ele é nervoso.}} `ner-VOH-zoo` He is a nervous person. | {{Ele está nervoso.}} He is nervous now. |
| {{Ela é feliz.}} She is a happy person. | {{Ela está feliz.}} She is happy now. |
| {{Ele é calmo.}} He is calm by nature. | {{Ele está calmo.}} He is calm at the moment. |
| {{O Pedro é doente.}} He has a long-term illness. | {{O Pedro está doente.}} He is sick (now). |

::: passo 1 Ask yourself: "Is it what it IS, or how it IS NOW?"
If you are naming or classifying (*who, what, where from, what kind*), use {{ser}}. If you are describing a situation that can change (*where, how right now*), use {{estar}}.
:::

::: passo 2 Check the verbs that you cannot translate
Some English sentences hide a trap. "The coffee is cold" is {{O café está frio}}, because the temperature is a state. "The coffee is expensive" is {{O café é caro}}, because the price is a feature. Likewise {{A sopa está quente}} (the soup is hot now) but {{A sopa é boa}} (the soup is a good one).
:::

::: passo 3 Remember what to do with marital status
In Brazil, people say {{Sou casado}} / {{Sou casada}} (I am married) and {{Sou solteiro}} / {{Sou solteira}} (I am single), with {{ser}}, even though you could argue the state can change. Learn these as blocks.
:::

::: dica
When you cannot decide, try both in your head with "usually" and "right now". "Pedro is usually friendly" → {{ser}}. "Pedro is sad right now" → {{estar}}. If both fit, the meaning will change, as in {{chato}}. If the sentence says where someone or something is, use {{estar}} (or {{ficar}} for a building). If it says where an event is, use {{ser}}.
:::

## 5.8 The contractions of place: ao, à, daqui, pra and pro

In Lesson 3 you learned {{no}}, {{na}}, {{do}}, {{da}}, {{pelo}}, {{pela}}. Now you add the contraction of **a** (to) and the forms Brazilians use in speech.

| Preposition | + o | + a | + os | + as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **a** (to) | {{ao}} | {{à}} | {{aos}} | {{às}} |

The **grave accent** on **à** and **às** is called the {{crase}} `KRAH-zee`. It marks the fusion of the preposition {{a}} with the article {{a}}. The rule: use it when the verb asks for {{a}} and the noun is feminine and takes {{a}}. A simple test: replace the noun with a masculine one. If you would say {{ao}}, write {{à}}: {{Vou ao mercado}} → {{Vou à feira}}. There is no crase before a masculine noun ({{ao}}), before a verb ({{a ler}}), or before a place with no article: {{Vou a Londres}}, {{Vou a Portugal}}.

| Portuguese | Meaning |
|---|---|
| {{Vou ao banco.}} | I'm going to the bank. |
| {{Vou à praia.}} | I'm going to the beach. |
| {{Vou aos Estados Unidos.}} | I'm going to the United States. |
| {{Em frente à praça.}} | Across from the square. |

In speech, Brazilians often use **em** instead of **a** with movement verbs: {{Vou no banco}}, {{Vou na praia}}. It is very common and not a mistake in conversation. In writing and formal contexts, choose {{ao}} and {{à}}. Another extremely common spoken form is **{{para}}** `PAH-rah` shortened to {{pra}} `prah` (and {{para o}} → {{pro}} `proo`): {{Vou pra casa}}, {{Vou pro trabalho}}, {{Pra viagem?}} (as in the padaria in Lesson 1). Write {{para}} in formal texts; use {{pra}} and {{pro}} in conversation and chats.

Some adverbs also fuse with {{de}}: {{aqui}} → {{daqui}} `dah-KEE`, {{aí}} → {{daí}} `dah-EE`, {{ali}} → {{dali}} `dah-LEE`, {{lá}} → {{de lá}}. You hear {{Sou daqui}} (I'm from here), {{Saio dali}}, {{Moro perto daqui}} (I live near here).

## 5.9 Tem or está? Introducing a thing, and locating a known thing

One more contrast makes your Portuguese sound natural, and it needs the vocabulary of a home. First the words.

| Word | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{a sala}} | `ah SAH-lah` | the living room |
| {{o quarto}} | `oo KWAR-too` | the bedroom |
| {{a cozinha}} | `ah koh-ZEE-nyah` | the kitchen |
| {{o banheiro}} | `oo bah-NYAY-roo` | the bathroom |
| {{a varanda}} | `ah vah-RAH~-dah` | the balcony |
| {{a cama}} | `ah KAH-mah` | the bed |
| {{o sofá}} | `oo soh-FAH` | the sofa |
| {{a geladeira}} | `ah zheh-lah-DAY-rah` | the refrigerator |
| {{o chuveiro}} | `oo shoo-VAY-roo` | the shower |

Now compare these two pairs.

| Introduce something new with {{tem}} | Locate something known with {{estar}} |
|---|---|
| {{Tem um sofá na sala.}} There is a sofa in the living room. | {{O sofá está na sala.}} The sofa is in the living room. |
| {{Tem uma geladeira na cozinha.}} There is a refrigerator in the kitchen. | {{A geladeira está na cozinha.}} The refrigerator is in the kitchen. |
| {{Tem um banheiro aqui?}} Is there a bathroom here? | {{Onde está o banheiro?}} Where is the bathroom? |

The difference is **new** against **known**. With {{tem}} you present something the listener may not know about, and the noun usually has {{um}}, {{uma}} or no article at all: {{Tem café?}} With {{estar}} (or {{ficar}}) you say where a specific thing the listener already knows is, and the noun has {{o}} or {{a}}: {{O café está na mesa}}. English does the same with *there is a coffee on the table* against *the coffee is on the table*. So do not say {{Tem o banheiro na sala}}; say {{O banheiro fica ao lado da sala}} or ask {{Onde fica o banheiro?}}

A short exchange to practice, with the roles of Laura and Marcos:

> {{Tem toalha no banheiro?}} Is there a towel in the bathroom?
> {{Tem, sim. A toalha está atrás da porta.}} Yes, there is. The towel is behind the door.

(New word: {{a toalha}} `ah toh-AH-lyah`, the towel.) Notice how the second answer moves from {{tem}} to {{está}} the moment the towel becomes a known object.

## 5.10 Read a real text

Read the text aloud, first with the guide, then without it.

> {{Eu sou a Laura e estou no Rio de Janeiro. O apartamento é pequeno, mas é bonito e fica perto da praia. Tem uma cozinha, uma sala e um quarto. Hoje estou cansada, mas estou muito feliz. A cidade é linda e as pessoas são simpáticas.}}

Guide: `EH-oo SOH ah LOW-rah ee ehs-TOH noo HEE-oo djee zhah-NAY-roo. oo ah-par-tah-MEH~-too eh peh-KEH-noo, mahss eh boh-NEE-too ee FEE-kah PEHR-too dah PRIE-ah. TEH~ee OO-mah koh-ZEE-nyah, OO-mah SAH-lah ee oo~ KWAR-too. OH-zhee ehs-TOH kah~-SAH-dah, mahss ehs-TOH MOO~ee-too feh-LEES. ah see-DAH-djee eh LEE~-dah ee ahss peh-SOH-ahss SOW~ see~-PAH-tchee-kahss.`

Translation: *I am Laura and I'm in Rio de Janeiro. The apartment is small, but it's pretty and it's near the beach. There is a kitchen, a living room and a bedroom. Today I'm tired, but I'm very happy. The city is beautiful and the people are friendly.*

Notice the choices: {{sou}} for her name, {{estou}} for her location and for tiredness, {{é pequeno}} for the apartment's size, {{fica}} for the building's position, {{tem}} for what exists, {{são simpáticas}} for the people's character.

## 5.11 The mistakes English speakers make

::: erros
| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| {{Eu sou cansada.}} | {{Estou cansada.}} | tiredness is a state of the moment: {{estar}} |
| {{Eu sou com fome.}} | {{Estou com fome.}} / {{Tenho fome.}} | hunger is not an identity; never {{ser com}} |
| {{Eu estou professora.}} | {{Sou professora.}} | a profession names who you are: {{ser}} |
| {{Eu estou de São Paulo.}} | {{Sou de São Paulo.}} | origin: {{ser}} |
| {{Têm muitos carros na rua.}} | {{Tem muitos carros na rua.}} | "there are" is {{tem}}, invariable |
| {{Há um banco aqui?}} (in speech) | {{Tem um banco aqui?}} | {{há}} is formal; {{tem}} is what Brazilians say |
| {{A festa está na casa da Ana.}} | {{A festa é na casa da Ana.}} | an event takes {{ser}} |
| {{Onde é o banco?}} | {{Onde fica o banco?}} | for a building, use {{ficar}} (or {{estar}}) |
| {{Eu tenho 20.}} | {{Eu tenho 20 anos.}} | do not forget {{anos}} |
| {{Tenho frio.}} (about the weather) | {{Está frio.}} | {{tenho frio}} is what you feel; the weather is {{está frio}} |
| {{Vou em a praia.}} | {{Vou à praia.}} | {{a + a = à}} (or {{na}} in speech) |
| {{O hotel está de o lado do banco.}} | {{O hotel fica ao lado do banco.}} | use {{ao lado de}} and fuse {{de + o = do}} |
:::

## 5.14 Pocket summary: Lesson 5

::: resumo
- **Ser** names what something is (identity, origin, profession, lasting qualities, time, event location). **Estar** names how or where it is now (location, feeling, temporary state).
- **Estar:** {{estou, está, estamos, estão}}; spoken {{tô, tá}}. **Ter:** {{tenho, tem, temos, têm}}.
- **Location:** {{estar}} for people and objects, {{ficar}} for buildings and places, {{ser}} for events.
- **Sensations:** {{estar com}} or {{ter}}: {{Estou com fome}} = {{Tenho fome}}. Age: {{ter}}.
- **There is / there are:** {{tem}} (invariable) in speech, {{há}} in writing.
- **Place words** ({{perto de, ao lado de, em frente a}}) fuse with the article: {{perto do banco}}, {{ao lado da praça}}.
- **Contractions of a:** {{ao, à, aos, às}}. In speech: {{vou no banco}}; {{pra}}, {{pro}} for {{para}}, {{para o}}.
- **Adjectives with both verbs change meaning:** {{ele é chato}} (annoying person) / {{ele está chato}} (being annoying today).
:::
