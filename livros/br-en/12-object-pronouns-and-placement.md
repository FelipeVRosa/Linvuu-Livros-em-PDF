# Object pronouns and where Brazilians put them

By the end of this lesson you can say "he called me", "I will call you", "I saw him" and "I gave it to her" the way Brazilians say them, put the little pronouns in the right place, ask "can you help me?", and recognize the written forms you will meet in books, e-mails and signs.

## 12.1 A scene you will live

Saturday afternoon. You and Clara are organizing a small party and you are texting and talking at the same time.

> **Clara:** {{Emma, você viu o Rafael?}}
> **Emma:** {{Vi ele ontem. Ele me ligou, mas eu não atendi.}}
> **Clara:** {{Me ajuda aqui? Eu preciso de alguém pra me dar uma mão.}}
> **Emma:** {{Claro. Eu te ligo mais tarde. Você já chamou a Marina?}}
> **Clara:** {{Chamei ela, sim. Ela me falou que vai trazer o bolo. Vou te mandar a lista.}}

Translation: *Emma, did you see Rafael? / I saw him yesterday. He called me, but I did not answer. / Can you help me here? I need someone to give me a hand. / Of course. I will call you later. Have you already invited Marina? / I did, yes. She told me she is going to bring the cake. I will send you the list.*

Every sentence contains a small pronoun, and every one of them stands in a place where English would not put it: {{ele me ligou}}, {{me ajuda}}, {{pra me dar}}, {{eu te ligo}}, {{ela me falou}}, {{vou te mandar}}. In one case there is a pronoun that is not small at all: {{vi ele}}, {{chamei ela}}. This lesson explains the pattern, so that you can do it automatically.

New words from the scene:

| Portuguese | Guide | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{atender}} | `ah-teh~-DER` | to answer (a phone, a door); to serve (a customer) |
| {{ajudar}} | `ah-zhoo-DAR` | to help |
| {{a mão}} | `ah mow~` | the hand |
| {{chamar}} | `shah-MAR` | to call; to invite (informal) |
| {{falar}} | `fah-LAR` | to speak, to tell |
| {{trazer}} | `trah-ZER` | to bring |
| {{mandar}} | `mah~-DAR` | to send |
| {{a lista}} | `ah LEES-tah` | the list |

## 12.2 The trap: English puts "it" and "him" after the verb, Portuguese puts "me" and "you" before it

In English, the object stays behind the verb whatever it is: *He called me. I saw him. She gave it to us.* In Portuguese, the object pronouns of the first and second person go **before** the verb: {{Ele me ligou}}, {{Eu te vi}}, {{Ela nos deu}}. This is not a trick of poetry: it is how the language sounds all day.

Two other differences hit English speakers hard.

| English | Portuguese | What is different |
|---|---|---|
| *I saw him / her / them.* | {{Vi ele.}} / {{Vi ela.}} / {{Vi eles.}} | in speech, the object for *him, her, them* is often the subject pronoun, placed after the verb |
| *Did you buy the bread? Yes, I bought it.* | {{Você comprou o pão? — Comprei.}} | "it" often disappears: the verb is enough |
| *Give me the keys.* | {{Me dá as chaves.}} | the pronoun starts the sentence in speech |
| *to me, for me* | {{pra mim}} | after a preposition, "me" is {{mim}} |

None of this is "bad Portuguese". It is the spoken standard of educated Brazilians. Writing and formal speech have a more traditional set of forms, and you will learn them in this lesson too. They are not the ones you need first.

## 12.3 The pronouns: direct and indirect objects

### What is a direct and an indirect object?

A **direct object** answers "whom? what?" without a preposition: *I saw (whom?) him*; {{Vi o Rafael}}. An **indirect object** answers "to whom? for whom?" and in Portuguese usually takes {{a}} or {{para}}: *I gave a book (to whom?) to her*; {{Dei um livro à Clara}}.

For the first person and the second person (tu), the same pronoun is used for both jobs.

| Person | Direct and indirect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| me | {{me}} | {{Ele me viu.}} (direct) / {{Ele me deu um livro.}} (indirect) |
| you (informal) | {{te}} | {{Eu te vi.}} / {{Eu te dei um livro.}} |
| us | {{nos}} (formal) / {{a gente}} (spoken) | {{Ele nos viu.}} / {{Ele viu a gente.}} |

The third person (him, her, them) and the formal "you" are different, and the written norm and the spoken norm differ strongly. This is where the real choices are.

### The big table

| Meaning | Written, formal | Spoken, everyday |
|---|---|---|
| *me* | {{me}} | {{me}} |
| *you* (informal, one person) | {{te}} (for {{tu}}); {{o}}, {{a}}, {{lhe}} (for {{você}}) | {{te}} or {{você}} |
| *him*, direct | {{o}} | {{ele}} |
| *her*, direct | {{a}} | {{ela}} |
| *them*, direct | {{os}}, {{as}} | {{eles}}, {{elas}} |
| *to him / to her* | {{lhe}} | {{pra ele}}, {{pra ela}} |
| *to them* | {{lhes}} | {{pra eles}}, {{pra elas}} |
| *us* | {{nos}} | {{a gente}} |
| *you* (plural) | {{os}}, {{as}}, {{lhes}} | {{vocês}} |

::: atencao
**{{Te}} and {{você}} in the same speech.** Strictly speaking, {{te}} belongs to {{tu}} and {{o/a/lhe}} belong to {{você}}. In everyday speech, in the south-east and in much of the country, people use {{você}} as the subject and {{te}} as the object, in the same sentence: {{Você sabe que eu te amo}}. Prescriptive grammar prefers consistency, but speakers of all backgrounds mix them, and you should too: {{Eu te ligo}} to a {{você}} friend is natural, and {{lhe}} is for formal writing and some formal service situations.
:::

## 12.4 Where the pronoun goes in Brazilian speech

The rule is simple: **the pronoun goes before the verb it belongs to.**

::: passo 1 One verb: put the pronoun right before it
- {{Ele me ligou.}} (He called me.)
- {{Eu te vejo amanhã.}} (I will see you tomorrow.)
- {{Ela nos convidou.}} (formal) / {{Ela convidou a gente.}} (She invited us.)
- {{Você me conhece?}} (Do you know me?)
:::

::: passo 2 With a negative, put {{não}} first
- {{Ele não me ligou.}} (He did not call me.)
- {{Eu não te vi.}} (I did not see you.)
:::

::: passo 3 With two verbs, put the pronoun before the second one
This is the pattern for {{ir}} + infinitive (Lesson 9), for {{poder}}, {{querer}}, {{precisar}} + infinitive (Lesson 7), and for the progressive.

- {{Vou te ligar mais tarde.}} (I will call you later.)
- {{Quero te ver.}} (I want to see you.)
- {{Você pode me ajudar?}} (Can you help me?)
- {{Preciso te falar uma coisa.}} (I need to tell you something.)
- {{Estou te esperando.}} (I am waiting for you.)
- {{Ele tinha me avisado.}} (He had warned me.)
:::

::: passo 4 Start the sentence with the pronoun if you like
In a conversation you will hear commands and requests that begin with the pronoun:

- {{Me ajuda!}} (Help me!)
- {{Me dá um minuto.}} (Give me a minute.)
- {{Me liga depois.}} (Call me later.)
- {{Te vejo amanhã.}} (See you tomorrow.)
:::

Writing and formal speech in Brazil avoid starting a sentence with an unstressed pronoun. A formal text would say {{Ajude-me}}, {{Dê-me}} and {{Ligue-me depois}}, with the pronoun **after** the verb and joined to it by a hyphen. That is also the usual position in European Portuguese: {{Disse-me}}, {{Vi-o}}, {{Telefono-te}}.

::: comparacao
| Brazilian speech | Writing, formal, European |
|---|---|
| {{Me ajuda!}} | {{Ajude-me!}} |
| {{Eu te vejo amanhã.}} | {{Vejo-te amanhã.}} (Portugal) |
| {{Ele me disse que sim.}} | {{Disse-me que sim.}} (Portugal) |
| {{Vou te ligar.}} | {{Vou ligar-te.}} / {{Vou ligar-lhe.}} |
:::

You need to **recognize** the hyphenated forms because you will see them on signs and in books, and you will hear a few of them in fixed phrases, even in Brazil:

- {{Sente-se, por favor.}} (Please sit down.)
- {{Sinta-se em casa.}} (Make yourself at home.)
- {{Divirta-se!}} (Have fun!)
- {{Prazer em conhecê-lo.}} / {{Prazer em conhecê-la.}} (Pleased to meet you; formal, to a man or a woman.)

## 12.5 Him, her, them: "vi ele" and "eu o vi"

This is the point where Brazilian Portuguese is most different from the textbooks, so let us be precise.

The traditional rule says that for a direct object in the third person you use {{o}}, {{a}}, {{os}}, {{as}}: {{Eu o vi}}, {{Conheço-a}}, {{Chamei-os}}. The subject pronouns {{ele}}, {{ela}}, {{eles}}, {{elas}} are the subject forms and should not be objects.

In everyday Brazilian speech, people say {{Vi ele}}, {{Conheço ela}}, {{Chamei eles}}, {{Ajudei ela}}. These are natural spoken forms, used by speakers of all social and educational levels. They are very frequent, and the traditional {{o/a}} forms sound formal or written. In formal writing, in an exam, or in a business e-mail, the traditional forms are expected.

::: passo 1 Choose according to where you are
- **Chatting with friends, at home, in the street:** {{Vi ele ontem.}} {{Conheci ela no Rio.}} {{Chamei eles pra festa.}}
- **Writing a formal e-mail, a cover letter, an exam:** {{Eu o vi ontem.}} {{Conheci-a no Rio.}} {{Convidei-os para a festa.}}
:::

::: passo 2 Remember the third way: drop the pronoun
Brazilians often say nothing at all. If the object is obvious, the verb is enough. This is the most natural option, and it is also the one that avoids the whole question.

| Question | Natural answer |
|---|---|
| {{Você viu o Rafael?}} | {{Vi.}} / {{Vi, sim.}} |
| {{Você comprou o pão?}} | {{Comprei.}} |
| {{Você gostou do filme?}} | {{Gostei.}} / {{Gostei muito.}} |
| {{Você leu o e-mail?}} | {{Li.}} / {{Ainda não li.}} |
| {{Você pegou as chaves?}} | {{Peguei.}} |

English needs "it" (*I bought it*). Portuguese does not. If you say {{Comprei ele}} for a thing like bread, a Brazilian will understand, but it sounds odd; for things, drop the pronoun or repeat the noun: {{Comprei o pão.}}
:::

::: passo 3 Keep {{ele}} and {{ela}} for people
The spoken object pronoun is natural for people ({{vi ele}}). For things, use the verb alone or the noun.
:::

## 12.6 To him, to her, to them: "dei pra ele"

For the indirect object in the third person, written Portuguese has {{lhe}} (singular) and {{lhes}} (plural): {{Dei-lhe um livro}}, {{Disse-lhes a verdade}}. These are very formal in Brazil. In speech, use {{pra}} + the pronoun:

| Formal | Everyday |
|---|---|
| {{Dei-lhe um presente.}} | {{Dei um presente pra ele.}} / {{Dei um presente pra ela.}} |
| {{Disse-lhes a verdade.}} | {{Falei a verdade pra eles.}} |
| {{Enviamos-lhe o contrato.}} | {{Mandei o contrato pra ele.}} |

This works for any verb whose person receives the action: {{dar}} (to give), {{dizer}} (to say), {{falar}} (to tell), {{mandar}} and {{enviar}} (to send), {{mostrar}} (to show), {{emprestar}} (to lend), {{entregar}} (to hand over), {{oferecer}} (to offer), {{perguntar}} (to ask), {{responder}} (to answer), {{escrever}} (to write), {{contar}} (to tell a story), {{pedir}} (to ask for).

With the first person and with {{te}}, the pronoun comes before the verb as usual: {{Ela me deu}}, {{Eu te mandei}}, {{Ele me falou}}, {{Ele te mostrou}}.

### Verbs with a person as direct object

Don't be confused by English. These verbs take a **direct** object in Portuguese:

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| {{ver}} | {{Eu te vi ontem.}} |
| {{conhecer}} | {{Conheço ela.}} |
| {{chamar}} | {{Chamei ele.}} |
| {{convidar}} | {{Vou te convidar.}} |
| {{ajudar}} | {{Posso te ajudar?}} |
| {{esperar}} | {{Estou te esperando.}} |
| {{buscar}} | {{Te busco às cinco.}} (I'll pick you up at five.) |
| {{amar}} | {{Eu te amo.}} |

Notice {{ajudar}}: in English *help* takes a direct object too, so this is easy. In shops and call centers you may hear {{Posso lhe ajudar?}} even though grammars prefer {{ajudá-lo}}. Between friends, say {{Posso te ajudar?}}

::: dica
For "call someone on the phone", Brazilians use {{ligar para}} or just a pronoun: {{Vou ligar pra ele}} or {{Vou ligar pra você}}. With {{te}}, the pronoun is enough: {{Te ligo depois}}. With {{me}}: {{Ele me ligou}}.
:::

### Recognizing {{-lo}}, {{-la}} in writing

When a written text puts {{o}} or {{a}} after an infinitive, you will see it fused to the verb: the final {{-r}} drops and the pronoun becomes {{lo}} or {{la}}, joined by a hyphen, with an accent on the vowel if needed.

| Written | Spoken equivalent | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| {{Vou vê-lo amanhã.}} | {{Vou ver ele amanhã.}} | I will see him tomorrow. |
| {{Quero conhecê-la.}} | {{Quero conhecer ela.}} | I want to meet her. |
| {{Posso ajudá-lo?}} | {{Posso te ajudar?}} | Can I help you? |
| {{Preciso fazê-lo hoje.}} | {{Preciso fazer isso hoje.}} | I need to do it today. |

You do not need to produce these forms, and many Brazilians do not produce them in speech either. But you will meet them in contracts, instructions, news and literature, so read them as "the verb plus the person or thing it acts on".

### Same idea, four registers

Here is one meaning, *I'll send it to you*, said in four ways. Seeing them side by side shows you how the system works.

| Register | Sentence |
|---|---|
| Spoken, friends | {{Vou te mandar.}} / {{Te mando já.}} |
| Spoken, neutral | {{Vou mandar pra você.}} |
| Formal written (Brazil) | {{Vou enviar-lhe o documento.}} |
| European Portuguese | {{Vou enviar-te o documento.}} / {{Envio-lhe já.}} |

In conversation the first two are enough. A learner who chooses {{Vou te mandar}} will sound like a Brazilian in São Paulo, in Rio or in Belo Horizonte, and will be understood in every region.

## 12.7 After a preposition: {{mim}} and {{eu}}

After a preposition, the pronoun for "me" is {{mim}} `mee~`, not {{me}} and not {{eu}}. For the other persons the form is the same as the subject pronoun.

| Person | After a preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| me | {{mim}} | {{pra mim}}, {{de mim}}, {{sem mim}}, {{por mim}} |
| you (informal) | {{você}} (or {{ti}} for {{tu}}) | {{pra você}}, {{com você}} |
| him / her | {{ele}} / {{ela}} | {{pra ele}}, {{sem ela}} |
| us | {{nós}} / {{a gente}} | {{pra gente}}, {{sem a gente}} |
| them | {{eles}} / {{elas}} | {{com eles}} |

Three prepositions fuse with the pronouns {{com}} + {{mim}}, {{ti}} and {{nós}}:

| With… | Form | Guide | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| me | {{comigo}} | `koh-MEE-goo` | {{Vem comigo.}} (Come with me.) |
| you (tu) | {{contigo}} | `koh~-TCHEE-goo` | {{Quero falar contigo.}} |
| you (você) | {{com você}} | | {{Quero falar com você.}} |
| us | {{conosco}} / {{com a gente}} | `koh-NOHS-koo` | {{Vem com a gente.}} (Come with us.) |

### {{Para mim}} or {{para eu}}?

This is a classic Brazilian spelling and usage question, and the rule is clean.

- If the preposition is followed by **a pronoun alone**, use {{mim}}: {{Isso é difícil para mim.}} {{Este presente é para mim.}}
- If the preposition is followed by **a pronoun + a verb**, the pronoun is the **subject** of that verb, so use {{eu}}: {{Trouxe um livro para eu ler.}} {{Isso é para eu fazer.}} {{Ele abriu a porta para eu entrar.}}

You will hear many Brazilians say {{para mim fazer}} in conversation, but it is a mistake in the formal standard, and teachers correct it. Learn it the right way: {{para eu fazer}}.

## 12.9 Putting it together

Read this exchange of messages aloud. Notice that each pronoun is placed before the verb it belongs to.

> **Clara:** {{Oi, Emma! Te liguei ontem, mas você não atendeu.}}
> **Emma:** {{Desculpa! Estava no ônibus e não ouvi o celular. Me liga agora?}}
> **Clara:** {{Posso te ligar daqui a pouco? Estou esperando o Rafael.}}
> **Emma:** {{Claro. Ele te ligou?}}
> **Clara:** {{Ligou, sim. Ele me disse que não vai poder vir. Eu avisei a Marina e ela me falou que vai trazer o bolo mesmo assim.}}
> **Emma:** {{Que bom! Eu te ajudo com a mesa.}}
> **Clara:** {{Obrigada! Vou te mandar a lista de compras.}}

Translation: *Hi, Emma! I called you yesterday, but you did not answer. / Sorry! I was on the bus and did not hear the phone. Can you call me now? / Can I call you in a little while? I am waiting for Rafael. / Of course. Did he call you? / He did, yes. He told me he will not be able to come. I told Marina and she told me she is going to bring the cake anyway. / Great! I will help you with the table. / Thanks! I will send you the shopping list.*

There is one spot to notice: {{Te liguei}} begins with the pronoun, as in spoken Brazilian, and the {{-gar}} spelling change from Lesson 10 appears in {{liguei}}. Notice also {{avisei a Marina}}: {{avisar}} takes the person as a direct object, like {{ajudar}}.

## 12.10 The mistakes English speakers make

::: erros
| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Eu vou ligar te. | {{Vou te ligar.}} | the pronoun goes before the infinitive |
| Ele disse a mim. | {{Ele me disse.}} | first person has no "to me" with {{a}} |
| Ele deu-me um livro. (in speech) | {{Ele me deu um livro.}} | in Brazilian speech the pronoun goes before the verb |
| Ela gosta de eu. | {{Ela gosta de mim.}} | after a preposition, "me" is {{mim}} |
| É para mim fazer. | {{É para eu fazer.}} | the pronoun is the subject of the verb that follows |
| Vem com mim. | {{Vem comigo.}} | {{com}} + {{mim}} fuse into {{comigo}} |
| Eu vi lhe ontem. | {{Eu vi ele ontem.}} / {{Eu o vi ontem.}} | {{lhe}} is the indirect object of a person, not a direct object |
| Dei ele um livro. | {{Dei um livro pra ele.}} | the third-person indirect object takes {{pra}} |
| Você comprou o pão? — Sim, comprei ele. | {{Comprei.}} | for things, drop the pronoun |
| Eu te amo você. | {{Eu te amo.}} / {{Eu amo você.}} | one pronoun is enough |
| Eu não conheço-a. | {{Eu não a conheço.}} / {{Não conheço ela.}} | even in the written style, the pronoun goes before the verb after {{não}} |
:::

## 12.13 Pocket summary: Lesson 12

::: resumo
- **Spoken placement:** the pronoun goes before the verb: {{Ele me ligou}}, {{Eu te vejo}}, {{Não me viu}}. With two verbs, before the second one: {{Vou te ligar}}, {{Pode me ajudar?}}, {{Estou te esperando}}.
- **Starting with the pronoun** is natural in speech: {{Me ajuda!}} {{Me liga.}} Writing and Portugal: {{Ajude-me}}, {{Disse-me}}, {{Vi-o}}.
- **Forms:** {{me}}, {{te}} (also for {{você}} in speech), {{nos}} (formal; spoken: {{a gente}}).
- **Him, her, them:** spoken {{ele}}, {{ela}}, {{eles}}, {{elas}}: {{Vi ele}}. Formal {{o}}, {{a}}, {{os}}, {{as}}: {{Eu o vi}}. Often best: drop it ({{Vi.}}). Things: no pronoun.
- **To him / her / them:** spoken {{pra ele}}, {{pra ela}}, {{pra eles}}; formal {{lhe}}, {{lhes}}.
- **After a preposition:** {{mim}} ({{pra mim}}, {{sem mim}}), {{comigo}}, {{com você}}, {{com a gente}}; but {{para eu fazer}}.
- **Fixed phrases with the pronoun after the verb:** {{Sente-se}}, {{Sinta-se em casa}}, {{Divirta-se}}, {{Prazer em conhecê-lo/a}}.
:::
